# Working brief: ages 6 to 8 (group C, stories C01 to C15)

This brief stands alone. A writer, an artist or an activity designer working on a group-C story needs only this page
plus the style guide for their language. A child joins this group on the sixth birthday; the group ends at the ninth.

**How to read the tags.** Every rule carries one tag:
- **[S]** a source states it. `ages §K` means `docs/research/ages-raw.md`, section K; `safety §1.5` means
  `docs/research/activities-safety-raw.md`, section 1.5; `vowels §2` means `docs/research/vowel-marks-raw.md`.
- **[D]** our own judgement, built from the nearest source named next to it.
- **[U]** unverified: weak, secondary or not opened. Never quote it to parents as fact.
- **[P]** a proposal to test.

Honest warning from the research: the 6-to-8 goal list rests mostly on one CDC page, and no trusted figure was found
for attention or memory at this age [ages §K and §L item 11]. Several rules below are therefore our judgement and are
tagged so.

---

## 1. Who this child is

**Where they are in life.** School has started: grade 1 at 6 in Israel, the børnehaveklasse at about 6 in Denmark
[S, ages §D2 and §D3]. The larger world opens up; independence from the family matters more; friendships matter
more and more; this is a key time for confidence built through friends, schoolwork and sport [S, ages §B, CDC 6 to 8].

**What they think about.** Mental skills grow fast; they find better ways to describe experiences and to talk about
thoughts and feelings; they focus less on themselves and show more concern for others; they start to think about the
future and about their place in the world [S, ages §B]. They want to be liked and accepted [S, ages §B].

**Fairness.** Preference for fair splits grows strongly between 3 and 8; most 7- and 8-year-olds prefer an equal
share, yet they still favour their own group [S, ages §F8]. So a fairness story at this age can ask the harder
question: fair also to the children who are not "ours".

**Honesty.** A story in which honesty is praised raised truth-telling in children up to 7; stories that punish the
liar did not [S, ages §F7; one laboratory study, long-term effect untested].

**Reading.** Children begin to read at 6; many 8-year-olds enjoy reading on their own [S, ages §B; the "many enjoy
reading" point is from a secondary source, U]. CDC advises taking turns reading with the child [S, ages §B]. So the
same book is read to a six-year-old and read by an eight-year-old. In Hebrew, children read pointed text first and
move to unpointed text from about grade 3 [S, ages §H and vowels §2]. In Arabic, vowel marks help beginners; when to
reduce them is not settled by research [S, vowels §1].

**Their bodies.** They dress themselves, tie shoes and catch a ball with their hands [S, ages §B].

**What we write for.** A child of about seven. A six-year-old hears it read; an eight-year-old can read most of it
alone [D, ages §I recommendation 4].

## 2. Hard limits for the book

Limits are set on the English text. Arabic and Hebrew match the English in ideas per spread, not word count [D].

| Limit | Rule | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| Total words | 450 to 900 | [D] from the single publishing source: picture books for 4 to 8 at or under 500 words, for 7 to 10 at or under 1,000 words, 32 to 48 pages (ages §G) |
| Spreads | 14 to 16, plus a cover | [D] from 32 to 48 pages (ages §G) |
| Words per spread | 30 to 70 | [D]. When this and the total disagree, the total wins: 16 spreads of 70 words would break the 900-word cap |
| Sentence length | usually 8 to 14 words; never more than 20; vary short and long for rhythm | [D] |
| Plot | a real problem that gets worse before it gets better; one main turn; the children solve it; a warm ending | [D] from "encourage solving small disagreements on their own" (ages §B) |
| Characters | at most 5 who speak or move the plot | [D] |
| Inner life | the main character may think and feel on the page ("Dana wasn't sure. What if they laughed?") | [S] better ways to talk about thoughts and feelings (ages §B) |
| Dialogue | lively and frequent; tagged when more than two speak | [D] |
| Repetition | a motif rather than a refrain: an object, a phrase or an action that returns three times and changes meaning | [D] |
| Rhyme | optional: a rhyme, chant or song inside the story works well; a fully rhymed text is not required | [D] |
| Read-alone layer | each spread has at least one short sentence (6 to 10 words, common words) that a beginning reader can manage | [D] from "take turns reading" (ages §B) |
| Facts | any fact in the story (birds, clocks, water) must be checked and true, with its source written in the outline; nothing invented about nature or the body | project rule 4 |
| Numbers in pictures | none: clock faces carry twelve marks, charts carry ticks, countdowns are shrinking dots; numbers live only in the text, as words | master plan §7 (one picture for four languages) |

**What the text may not contain.** A spoken moral, sarcasm aimed at a child, bullying shown without repair, real
violence, romance, brand names, school grades as a measure of worth, praise for being "smart" (praise effort instead:
"you worked hard") [S for the last point, ages §B].

## 3. Film limits [P]

No trusted source gives a figure for attention at this age [S, ages §K]. AAP gives no numeric cap for 6 to 12; it
warns that screen misuse is linked to weaker attention control and to poorer school results, and advises watching
together and avoiding autoplay [S, ages §C].

| Item | Proposal to test |
|---|---|
| Length | 6 to 8 minutes [P] (master plan §3.4). The research file's own draft range was 12 to 20 minutes; the owner's first range 4 to 10. We start at 6 to 8 because total film time and cost matter, and lengthen only if pilots show children want more |
| Scenes | 7 to 10, with a clear middle where things go wrong [P] |
| Shot length | free, 3 to 12 seconds; faster cuts allowed in action moments [P] |
| Suspense | real but safe, resolved before the end [D] |
| Sound | music carries mood changes; sound effects can carry jokes [D] |
| Ending | a closing image, then the film stops; no autoplay; an optional "talk about it" question for the family [S] ages §C |
| Words in picture | none; captions outside the art, in the voice's language (master plan §7) |

## 5. Tone, and what to avoid

**Tone:** confident, warm, humorous, sometimes moving. Children are capable heroes; adults are present, sometimes
learning from the children. A story can hold a real disappointment, as long as it ends with repair and hope.

**Avoid:**
- Lessons learned by punishment, fear, shame or humiliation [S, ages §F7].
- The excluded child who is accepted only because of a hidden talent. Inclusion is good because it is right, and the
  game becomes better for everyone [D].
- A spoken moral at the end, or an adult lecture in the middle.
- Unsafe acts shown as fun without consequence (climbing high without help, swimming without an adult, using the
  stove alone) [S, ages §B safety list].
- Exclusion or bullying shown without repair [D, ages §J3 item 14].
- Screens as a reward, or the message "screens are bad". The research message is "choose well and stop when you
  choose" [S, ages §C].

## 7. Activity safety checklist for group C

**Rule for the whole group:** the under-3 and under-5 lists no longer bind this group, but the balloon rule does
(latex balloons are a choking risk up to age 8) [S, safety §1.1], and heat, knives and power tools stay out [S,
safety §1.9 and §3].

**Red list: never in a group-C activity**
- [ ] Button or coin batteries; small loose magnets (large fridge-type magnets held by the adult are the only exception) [S, safety §1.2; the exception is a derived rule, D]
- [ ] Latex balloons, whole or burst [S, safety §1.1]
- [ ] Stove, oven, kettle, candles, matches, hot glue; cold or lukewarm materials only [S, safety §1.9 and §3]
- [ ] Knives (a butter knife on soft food with an adult is the only exception), craft knives, needles, pins, staples, split pins in the child's hands, hammers, saws [S, safety §1.9, §2 and §3]
- [ ] Water play without a named adult watching the water the whole time [S, safety §1.7 and §3]
- [ ] Tasting seeds, berries, mushrooms or wild plants; planting seeds is fine in a pot, with handwashing after [S, safety §2 and §1.11]
- [ ] Household chemicals, medicines and cleaning bottles in the child's hands (looking at a label held by an adult is fine) [S, safety §1.3]
- [ ] Solvent glue, spray paint, permanent markers with strong solvent, art products without the AP seal [S, safety §1.6]
- [ ] Rubber bands used to fling or shoot; more than one band at a time [D, safety §2]
- [ ] String or yarn made into necklaces or loops around the neck; anything hung where a child could climb to it [D, safety §1.8 and §2]
- [ ] Glass jars or glass bottles [D: breakage; not in the research]
- [ ] Raw flour and raw-flour dough [S, CDC "Raw Flour and Dough": untreated flour can carry E. coli; checked in the review]
- [ ] Animals not suited to children; touching wild or stray animals [S, safety §1.11]

**Safe materials for group C** (everything in the group-A and group-B lists, plus) [D, safety §2]
Child scissors (checked by the adult), paper for folding, cardboard for building, plastic bottles with caps removed,
one rubber band at a time with an adult, sticky tape, yarn or thread with an adult (never as a necklace), a ruler, a
notebook, a pencil, seeds for planting in a pot, a mirror without sharp edges, a torch whose battery compartment is
taped shut, aluminium foil, coins only where counting them back afterwards is part of the activity.

**Allergy check before starting** [D, safety §1.10; U] Wheat, egg, milk, nuts (never used), sesame (never used in
food activities, D), latex (never used).

**Outdoors** [S, safety §1.11] Collect only leaves, sticks, stones, pinecones, dry grass and shells; never pull
plants from beds; never taste; wash hands after. Hats, shade and water in summer; outdoor collecting before 10:00 or
after 16:00 in hot months [D, safety §1.11].

**Emergency line on every activity card:** Israel 101 and Denmark 112 [U, general knowledge; the Israeli poison
centre number was not confirmed, safety §1.12].
