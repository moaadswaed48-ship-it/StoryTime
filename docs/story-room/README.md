# Story Room: what is in this folder

The writing foundation for StoryTime's 45 stories: who each age group is, how each language is written, and the 45
story outlines with their activities. Written 22 Nov 2026 for Stage 1 of the master plan (research) and to be used in
Stages 5 and 6 (pilot stories, then the other 42).

**Status:** drafts, ready for the owner's review and for one native editor per language. Nothing here is final text
for children; the stories themselves are written in Stage 5 and 6. An editorial review on 22 Nov 2026 changed 41 of
the 45 outlines and tightened the briefs and style guides; its findings and the three recommended pilot stories are in
`REVIEW.md`.

## Decisions I made (say if you disagree)

1. **Arabic vowel marks: the full layer is written first; the light layer is made from it by deleting marks under
   eight fixed rules, never by choosing.** So two writers who agree on the grammar always produce the same light text.
   The light layer keeps the vowels inside words, every shadda (except after "al-"), tanwin with fatha and the endings
   that show "you (male)" versus "you (female)"; it drops case endings, sukun, predictable vowels and marks on common
   small words. Our own convention: no publisher rule exists in the research.
2. **Arabic for ages 6 to 8 shows the light layer by default, with a visible "I read by myself" switch to the full
   layer.** Research says full marks help beginners, and the research file's own working convention is full marks in
   grades 1 and 2; the owner does not want mosquito-covered pages. This departs from the research on purpose and is
   the decision most in need of the Arabic editor; flipping the default changes nothing else.
3. **Hebrew shows full niqqud at every age.** Research says children read pointed text until about grade 3, and all
   our groups are younger. The voice layer adds three marks that are never shown: stress on words not stressed on the
   last syllable, the spoken shva, and the short "o" kamatz, following the open tool Phonikud's conventions.
4. **Hebrew pointed text uses the traditional pointed spelling** (שֻׁלְחָן, not שׁוּלְחָן), as the Academy's rules for
   pointed text require. Many modern picture books mix the two; the editor decides.
5. **Group A applies the under-3 safety rules to every child in it**, including three-year-olds, because the same
   activity reaches a two-year-old.
6. **Book word counts are set for English**; Arabic and Hebrew match the English in ideas per page, not in word count,
   because their words carry attached prefixes and endings.
7. **Film lengths stay at the master plan's proposals** (2 to 3, 4 to 5 and 6 to 8 minutes), marked as proposals to
   test. The research file's own drafts were longer; no trusted source gives attention minutes by age.
8. **One name per character in all four languages**, chosen from names that sound at home in both Arabic- and
   Hebrew-speaking families; Danish changes Y to J (Jara, Jasmin, Josef, Maja); a few shared names take each
   language's own form (Noah/نوح/נֹחַ, Miriam/مريم, Karam/כֶּרֶם, Tamir/تامِر). 47 names, none used twice, 24 girls and
   23 boys. Every name was checked for unwanted meanings in the four languages; the rejected ones and the reasons are
   in the ledger.
9. **Numbers are written as words in story text** in every language; which digit shapes Arabic uses is left open.
10. **Culturally neutral for every family in Israel:** no holidays, no religious formulas or symbols, places as types
    (the sea, a village) not named towns, no flags or uniforms, no pork, no meat with milk in one meal, no named sabbath
    days, families of many shapes.
11. **Respect for the elderly and for classmates** have no specific trusted source as goals (the research says so).
    I built them on sourced goals (kindness and respect for others, noticing others, patience) and marked them [D].
12. **The owner's four lessons each have more than one story**, with a different goal and plot shape each time:
    elders A04, B14, C04; mother A03, C03; classmates A15, B02, C02; numbers A01, B01, C01.
13. **Balance:** 23 value stories and 22 skill stories (A 7/8, B 8/7, C 8/7).
14. **Adult-facing text** (activity explanations, captions): Arabic uses the light layer; Hebrew is unpointed, in
    standard full spelling.
15. **English uses British spelling** and Mum and Dad.
16. **Group activity ratios are tighter than the care ratios in the research** (for example one adult per 4 children
    for water, food or paint at 2 to 3). This is my judgement; the research's own ratio rule is also the researcher's.
17. **Story B11 (drumming syllables):** beat counts differ between languages, so the drum track is laid per language
    with the voice, not baked into the shared film.

## Open questions for a native editor, per language

### Arabic
1. Do the twelve full-layer rules and eight light-layer rules in `style-ar.md` §2 produce text you would publish for
   children? Run them on the two worked examples and on one page of your own.
2. Is the light layer right for ages 6 to 8, or should the full layer be the default for beginning readers?
3. Eastern Arabic digits (٣) or Western digits (3) for Arabic-speaking children in Israel?
4. Should dialogue lines use a light spoken register (one 2024 study found spoken-Arabic stories understood best), or
   stay in Standard Arabic as the owner decided?
5. Pausal tanwin fatha: written ـًا in the voice layer; if the voice says "-an", switch to ـَا at pauses only?
6. Counting forms in refrains (واحِد، اثْنان، ثَلاثَة) and number agreement in our examples.
7. Hamza seat conventions (اقرؤوا or اقرءوا) and tanwin placed on the letter before the alif (شُكْرًا).
8. Is the list of everyday-Standard word choices (بيت over منزل, شبّاك over نافذة) right for children in Israel?
9. Strike any name in the ledger that sounds foreign or loaded.

### Hebrew
1. Traditional pointed spelling (כתיב חסר מנוקד) or full spelling with niqqud for these ages?
2. Could groups A and B (read by an adult) use lighter niqqud, or is full niqqud expected?
3. The speech-layer marks (U+05AB stress, U+05BD spoken shva, U+05C7 kamatz katan): confirm with the voice test.
4. Spelling of crossing names in Hebrew (יָארָה for Yara; לִיאַם; סָאמִי), and the names added in the review (marked †
   in the ledger).
5. Counting aloud in feminine forms (אַחַת, שְׁתַּיִם, שָׁלֹשׁ) in refrains: natural for children?
6. Maqaf: we use none; is that acceptable in pointed children's text?
7. Present or past tense for group-A narration.

### English
1. British or American spelling for families in Israel (and Mum or Mom)?
2. Are the English outline titles worth keeping as book titles?

### Danish
1. Comma rule: we write without the optional start comma; confirm Dansk Sprognævn's current rule.
2. Bedstemor and bedstefar in narration, mormor and farfar only when the side of the family is fixed: natural?
3. Y-to-J name changes (Jara, Jasmin, Josef, Maja): right, or keep the international spelling?
4. Is a Mediterranean picture world without Danish seasons acceptable for families in Denmark?
5. Is there a remse (rhyme) tradition we should draw on for refrains? The research could not source one.

### All languages
- One native read per language of the pilot stories before the other 42 are written (master plan §11).

## What the research could not give us (and what this means)

- **No source for vowel marks or niqqud by age.** Both conventions are ours and wait for the editors.
- **No trusted figure for attention in minutes** at any age: film lengths and activity lengths are proposals to test.
- **The CDC 4- and 5-year milestone checklists and Israel's kindergarten curriculum were not read**, so group B rests
  on England's Development Matters and the CDC 3-to-5 parenting page, and group C on one CDC page.
- **Respect for elders, kindness to classmates and money** have no specific trusted source as teaching goals.
- **Allergens in craft materials:** no trusted source; the allergy checks are our judgement.
- **The Israeli poison centre's number** was not confirmed; Israel 101 and Denmark 112 come from general knowledge.
- **Story C10's stork facts** were checked in the review (sources in the outline); the first draft's mother stork
  travelling with her young was removed, because young storks can migrate without their parents.
- **Raw flour:** the research listed flour dough as a safe material; the CDC advises that children should not play
  with raw-flour dough. The briefs and A09 now follow the CDC.
- **Danish children's-book conventions and the Danish rhyme tradition** were not found.
- The craft advice in `.claude/skills/kids-book-writer/` (for example its word-count chart) is unsourced and was used
  only as advice, never as a rule.
