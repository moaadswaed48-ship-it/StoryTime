# Danish review of the pilot stories A04, B02, C01 (22 Nov 2026)

**Result.** The Danish of all three stories has been read aloud as Danish, not against the English, and corrected:
about 45 changes, none to the plot. The main fixes: lines that sounded translated from English ("synger den havet",
"Du skal blive gemt", "Det gav et sæt i maven"), one verb form I could not confirm ("Ingen pev"), the start comma
used against our own rule in A04, capitals after a colon, and a B02 title that now sounds like a Danish book. The
check tool reports no Danish errors. **A native Danish editor must still read all three stories:** this is the
language where my ear is least reliable (last section).

Files changed: `content/stories/{a04,b02,c01}/da.json` only. Reviewed against `style-da.md`, the age briefs,
`SCHEMA.md`, the outlines, and Dansk Sprognævn's 2025 rule changes for the three dots
([overview, §43](https://dsn.dk/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/Oversigt-over-aendringer-i-retskrivningsreglerne-2025-1.pdf)).

## 2. Decisions

1. **Emergency numbers removed from the safety lines of all three stories** and replaced by "Sker der et uheld, så
   ring til det lokale alarmnummer." The reasons are in `REVIEW-en.md` §3.1 (alarming on a calm toddler walk; numbers
   still unverified; only useful in two countries). "Alarmnummer" is the Danish word for 112. **For the Arabic and
   Hebrew reviewers:** please make the matching change in your files.
2. **Sound words.** "tok" (A04, the stick): kept. It is a plain wooden knock, it is not a word, and "tak" (thanks) and
   "tik" (clock) would mislead. "Fiiiit!" (whistle): kept, shortened by one i. "Piv" (rubber duck): kept, the usual
   Danish squeak. "Dryp, dryp, dryp" (rain): kept; it is the rain sound Danish children know. "Hys" for shushing:
   kept. "Bzzz" (bee): kept.
3. **Title "Den syngende konkylie".** "Konkylien der synger" is correct (no comma, by our rule), but it follows the
   English word order and reads like a translation. "Den syngende konkylie" is the shape of a Danish book title (as
   in "Den grimme ælling"). "Konkylie" is the right word: it is the spiral shell Danes hold to the ear "to hear the
   sea", which a børnehave child knows.
4. **Ellipsis with a space on both sides** ("løb … stop", "… sammen") is correct: Dansk Sprognævn's 2025 rules (§43)
   say hesitation dots take a space to the words around them, and none before a following ! or ?.
5. **Kept on purpose:** "Løb, løb, løb!" (a narrated chant works in Danish as an imperative cheer); "bedstefar" in
   narration and dialogue (the outline does not say which side of the family he is on, style-da §1.3); "saft" for the
   baker's drink (a Danish word exists, so no loan "juice"; a baker making pomegranate saft is natural); "I dag måtte
   alle tage noget med hjemmefra" (a native way to say "show-and-tell day"; "legetøjsdag" would make the shell the odd
   one out); "Keglespil med ti" and "Kiggerammen" as activity titles; "Så er det retfærdigt" (Liam's judge voice).
6. **Emotion directions** stay in English, as in all four files.

## 4. What a native Danish editor must still read (my limits)

I write Danish well, but I am a model, not a Danish pædagog, and my sense of what sounds natural to a Danish
four-year-old is weaker than in English. I could not open Den Danske Ordbog or Retskrivningsordbogen from here (the
pages returned nothing), so word forms were judged from knowledge, except the three-dot rule, which I checked in
Sprognævnet's 2025 overview. Please read, above all:

1. The sound words: "tok", "Fiiiit!", "dryp" for rain on a roof, "Bzzz".
2. "Han fik et sug i maven" (C01 p8) and "Sælgeren slog en skraldlatter op, den største hele dagen" (p15).
3. "så synger den om havet" (B02 p11): is it Dalia's voice, or would a Danish child say "så kan man høre havet"?
4. "Ingen sagde et pip" (B02 p13) instead of a verb for the duck; and the past tense of "pive", if you prefer a verb.
5. "Det larmede mere … og mere" (B02 f08), "Du skal gemme dig lidt endnu" (f03), "Fortæl så dit eget" (step 3).
6. "saft" versus "juice" for the baker's drink.
7. The comma rule: our files use no optional start comma. Confirm this is still what the editor wants (style-da §6.2
   is marked [U]).
8. Whether "bedstefar" should become "morfar" or "farfar" in the toddler's own lines once the outline decides.
