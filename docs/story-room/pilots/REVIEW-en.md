## 2. The arithmetic in C01 (checked line by line, identical in English and Danish)

Ten on the table → five and five (p4) → two moved: seven and three (p6) → one set on the table: seven and two in the
baskets, one loose (p7) → Liam counts nine (p8), because he is holding the loose one (p10) → seven, two and one make ten
(p11) → eight and two (p11) → Sofia's list 5+5, 7+3, 8+2 (p12) → Liam tries nine and one, moves it back (p12) → eight
and two leave (p13). The eleventh pomegranate was under the table, not on it ("only ten… left on the table", p2), so
the count never breaks. Film: f30 to f32 go nine and one → six and four with three silent hops between, as shot 38
asks. Activity examples: three fallen leaves seven standing; four and six make ten; 3 + 7 = 10. All correct.

## 3. Decisions

1. **Emergency numbers removed from all three safety lines, replaced by one general line.** In A04 the line sat
   after "look only, nothing in the mouth" on a calm walk with a two-year-old and read as a warning of danger. The
   numbers are also still unverified (`age-2-3.md` §7 marks them [U]), they only serve families in two countries, and
   they cannot be digits-checked per country inside the story files. The new line keeps the brief's rule (every card
   names the local emergency number) without the alarm: "If there is an accident, call your local emergency number."
   I made the same change in B02 and C01 so the three cards read alike. If the owner wants the actual numbers, the
   app can show them once, by the reader's country, outside the activity text.
   **For the Arabic and Hebrew reviewers:** please make the same change in `ar.json` (via `src/<id>.ar.json`) and
   `he.json` for all three stories. Suggested sense: "If there is an accident, call your local emergency number."
2. **"Show-and-tell day."** "Bring-something day" is not an English expression. "Show and tell" is the standard name
   for this classroom ritual in both British and American English, so it works for families with either background.
   As a modifier before "day" it is hyphenated (show-and-tell day) in both spellings.
3. **British throughout, as the guide says.** Kept: colour words, "ladybird", "kitchen-roll", "metres", "Grandma",
   "practises", "round the circle", "Shall I". Nothing American was found to remove except the two tense choices
   above ("what you brought", "what did you bring"). "Kindergarten" is kept: it is the setting's own word and is
   understood in both countries ("nursery" would make the children too young for Americans, "reception" is
   UK-only).
4. **Kept on purpose:** "Step… tap" (the stick's sound; easy to chant), the present tense in A04 (allowed for group A,
   one tense throughout), "it sings the sea" (Dalia's own odd phrase, which keeps the story clear of the claim that
   a shell holds the sea), "What would you do?" (the one question the B brief asks for), Grandpa saying "Step… tap"
   himself in f08 (it is a game he plays with the child).
5. **Emotion directions** were all useful to a voice actor; none changed.
