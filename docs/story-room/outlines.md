# The 45 story outlines

Fifteen stories per age group: **A01 to A15** for ages 2 to 3, **B01 to B15** for ages 4 to 5, **C01 to C15** for
ages 6 to 8. Each outline is the brief a writer, the Studio and the activity designer work from. Titles are working
titles in English; each language names its own book.

**Read with:** the age brief for the group (`age-2-3.md`, `age-4-5.md`, `age-6-8.md`) and the style guide for the
language. Research references: `ages §J1.24` means `docs/research/ages-raw.md`, section J1, goal 24; `safety §1.5`
means `docs/research/activities-safety-raw.md`, section 1.5. Tags: [S] the source states it, [D] our judgement from
the nearest source, [U] unverified.

**Marks used below**
- ★ a lesson the owner named (respect for the elderly, respect for classmates, being good to one's mother, numbers).
- **Kind:** *value* (behaviour, feelings, relationships) or *skill* (numbers, colours, shapes, letters, time, nature,
  body, safety, hygiene).
- **Also fits:** the lesson suits a second group; the story may be offered there too (master plan §3.2).

**Rules every outline follows:** one teaching goal, unique across all 45; a beginning, a turn and a warm ending; no
moral spoken at the end; no lesson taught by punishment (honesty and kindness are shown with a good result, as the
research advises, ages §F7); no character appears in two stories; settings and seasons spread across home,
kindergarten, school, market, sea, desert, hills, woods, city and village, day and night, all four seasons; nothing
resembles the old autumn story of a boy and falling leaves (no falling leaves appear in any story). Every activity uses
free or near-free materials and passes the red list in its group's brief.

## Group A: ages 2 to 3

Balance: 7 value stories, 8 skill stories. Seasons: summer 4, autumn 4, winter 3, spring 4. Night or dusk: A01, A06,
A07, A13.

### A01 · One, Two, Where Is Three? ★ numbers
- **Kind:** skill (numbers)
- **Teaching goal:** Count three things, saying one number for each thing.
- **Why this age:** counting objects to about three to five and finger-number rhymes are goals for ages 2 to 3 [S, ages
  §J1.24; Development Matters maths and Reach Out and Read 24 to 30 months].
- **Plot:** Every summer evening Adam and his father sit on the harbour wall to wait for the fishing boats. Adam holds
  up his fingers. "One boat!" A red boat chugs in. "Two boats!" A blue boat follows. Then the sea is empty, and the sun
  is sinking. Where is three? A gull lands beside them. Papa hums. Adam waits, three fingers ready. Far out, a little
  light blinks: the yellow boat, slow because its nets are full. "Three boats!" Three fingers, three boats, three
  fishers waving. Adam counts them again, all the way home.
- **Characters:** Adam (a boy of about two and a half); his father; the three fishers; a gull.
- **Setting and season:** a fishing harbour by the sea; summer; sunset.
- **Activity, "One Stone, One Cup":** set out three cups in a row; the child drops one big stone into each while you
  count together, then tips them out and starts again. *Materials:* three paper or plastic cups, three smooth stones
  bigger than the child's fist (tube test, safety §1.1).
- **Activity goal:** Put one object in each place (one-to-one matching).
- **Film can show:** the long wait as real time: an empty sea, a slow sunset, a light far away that grows into a boat;
  three horn toots as a counting sound.

### A02 · The Sparrow's Warm Nest
- **Kind:** skill (colours)
- **Teaching goal:** Name four colours: red, yellow, green and blue.
- **Why this age:** pointing to and naming colours is a goal at 24 to 30 months [S, ages §J1.23; Reach Out and Read].
- **Plot:** On a cold winter morning a little sparrow wakes on the market roof. Her nest is grey and thin. She hops
  down to the stalls. Yellow lemons: too heavy. Green cucumbers: too cold. Red tomatoes: too squishy. "Not yellow, not
  green, not red." At the very last stall an old man is knitting, and little bits of wool fall around his feet: red,
  yellow, green and one long blue piece. The sparrow carries them up, one by one. Now her nest is the warmest and
  brightest on the roof, and she falls asleep in the middle, on the blue.
- **Characters:** the sparrow; the vegetable sellers; the old man who knits at the wool stall.
- **Setting and season:** a city market; winter; early morning.
- **Activity, "Sock Pairs":** tip a basket of clean socks onto the floor; the child finds two that are exactly the
  same and lays them together; you name the colour as each pair is found. *Materials:* clean socks, a basket.
- **Activity goal:** Find and match pairs that are exactly the same.
- **Film can show:** the bustle of a market in motion and the colours waking up as the morning light rises; the
  sparrow's hops; the wool drifting down.

### A03 · Here You Are, Mama ★ being good to one's mother
- **Kind:** value
- **Teaching goal:** Notice when Mama is tired and offer her comfort.
- **Why this age:** by age 2 children notice when someone is upset and may respond; responding kindly is a goal [S,
  ages §J1.6; CDC milestones by 2].
- **Plot:** It is a very hot afternoon. Mama comes home with heavy bags and sits down with a big "Phew." Lina looks at
  her. Mama's face is red. Mama's eyes are closing. Lina brings her rabbit. "Here you are, Mama." Mama smiles a little.
  Lina brings Mama's slippers. "Here you are, Mama." Then Lina picks up a picture book and waves it: whoosh, whoosh, a
  little wind. Mama's eyes open. Mama laughs. "Thank you, Lina." They lie together on the cool floor, and now Mama
  waves the book over Lina: whoosh, whoosh.
- **Characters:** Lina (a girl of about three); her mother; Lina's toy rabbit.
- **Setting and season:** home, a city flat; summer; afternoon.
- **Activity, "Paper Fan":** the child colours a sheet of paper with chunky crayons; the adult folds it back and forth
  into a fan; take turns fanning each other's face and hands and say what you feel. *Materials:* one sheet of paper,
  chunky crayons.
- **Activity goal:** Feel that moving air cools the skin (cause and effect).
- **Film can show:** heat and coolness: the shimmering hot afternoon, the breeze of the book lifting Mama's hair and
  the curtain.

### A04 · Slow, Slow, Together ★ respect for the elderly
- **Kind:** value
- **Teaching goal:** Slow down and hold hands to walk at an older person's pace.
- **Why this age:** waiting and holding back an impulse ("effortful control") is a goal at 2 to 3 [S, ages §J1.3];
  applying it to a grandparent is our choice, since no trusted source names respect for elders as a goal [D, ages §M].
- **Plot:** Grandpa promised Sami the red flowers at the top of the hill. Sami runs. Run, run, run! He looks back.
  Grandpa is far below, leaning on his stick. "Come, Grandpa!" Grandpa waves and walks: step... step... Sami runs back
  down and takes Grandpa's hand. Slow, slow, together. Now Sami sees what he missed: a butterfly, a ladybird, a bee in a
  flower. At the top the whole hill is red with anemones. They sit on a warm rock, and the ladybird walks over both
  their hands.
- **Characters:** Sami (a boy of about three); his grandfather, who walks with a stick; a ladybird.
- **Setting and season:** green hills; spring; morning.
- **Activity, "Here You Are, Thank You":** the child carries a cushion or an empty cup to an older family member (or
  to you), holds it out with both hands and says "here you are"; the other says "thank you"; swap roles. *Materials:*
  a cushion, a plastic cup.
- **Activity goal:** Use the giving words "here you are" and "thank you" in play.
- **Film can show:** two speeds side by side, the boy's dash and the grandfather's steps, then the matched pace; small
  creatures that appear only when the camera slows down with them.

### A05 · Little Camel Shakes the Sand
- **Kind:** skill (body)
- **Teaching goal:** Name and point to six body parts: head, ears, nose, back, knees and feet.
- **Why this age:** pointing to body parts is a milestone by 2 and naming them a goal [S, ages §J1.15; CDC by 2].
- **Plot:** A warm wind blows over the desert, and sand gets everywhere. Little Camel has sand on his head. Shake,
  shake! Sand in his ears. Flap, flap! Sand on his nose. Achoo! Sand on his back, on his knobbly knees, on his big flat
  feet. Shake, flap, achoo, wiggle, stomp! A small lizard on a rock laughs and laughs. Mother Camel kneels beside him
  and blows softly: whoo. The last sand flies away. Little Camel tucks his head under her neck, and they rest in the
  shade of a rock.
- **Characters:** Little Camel (a calf); Mother Camel; a lizard.
- **Setting and season:** the desert; autumn; midday.
- **Activity, "Leaf on Your Knee":** take two big leaves; give a short instruction ("Put the leaf on your knee", "Put
  the leaf on your head"); the child follows, then gives you one. *Materials:* two leaves bigger than a hand.
- **Activity goal:** Follow a spoken instruction with two key words.
- **Film can show:** sand blowing and being shaken from each body part in turn, a slow-motion sneeze, the wind's
  sound: comedy that lives in movement.

### A06 · Little Hippo's Big Brush
- **Kind:** skill (hygiene)
- **Teaching goal:** Brush the top teeth and the bottom teeth as part of going to bed.
- **Why this age:** tooth-brushing and bedtime routine books are recommended from 15 to 18 months; routines are a
  goal at 2 to 3 [S, ages §J1.13; Reach Out and Read; AAP choosing books].
- **Plot:** Night is coming to the river. Papa Hippo opens his mouth very wide: "Brush time!" Little Hippo opens his
  mouth too, just a little. Brush, brush, top teeth. Brush, brush, bottom teeth. Little Hippo giggles: the brush
  tickles! A small bird lands on Papa's nose to watch. "Your turn," says Papa, and the bird hops into his big mouth and
  pecks out a crumb. Everyone laughs. Swish, spit, splash. Two clean smiles shine in the moonlight, and one small bird
  goes to sleep on a big warm head.
- **Characters:** Little Hippo; Papa Hippo; a small bird.
- **Setting and season:** a river; autumn; dusk into night.
- **Activity, "Brush to the Song":** brush your teeth side by side in front of a mirror while you sing one short song
  twice; stop when the song stops. *Materials:* toothbrushes and toothpaste as your dentist or nurse advises.
- **Activity goal:** Keep going at a task until a song ends (a song as a timer).
- **Film can show:** the river turning from dusk to moonlight, the giant comic mouth, splashing water and the bird's
  hops.

### A07 · Boom, Says the Sky
- **Kind:** value (feelings)
- **Teaching goal:** Let a cuddle and a favourite soft toy calm a scared feeling.
- **Why this age:** calming down with a comforting adult and finding ways to soothe oneself is a goal at 2 to 3; stories
  let children "safely explore emotions beyond their normal range" [S, ages §J1.2 and §D1].
- **Plot:** Yara is sleeping at Grandma's house in the village. Rain taps on the window. Then: BOOM! The sky shouts.
  Yara sits up, eyes wide. Grandma comes with a little lamp. "Big noise," she says, and holds Yara close. Flash...
  BOOM! Yara squeezes her toy lamb. Grandma breathes slowly: in... out... Yara breathes too: in... out... The next boom
  is smaller. Then only rain. Tap, tap, tap. "It sounds like little feet dancing on the roof," Grandma whispers. Yara
  listens to the dancing feet until her eyes close.
- **Characters:** Yara (a girl of about three); her grandmother; Yara's toy lamb.
- **Setting and season:** Grandma's house in a village; winter; a stormy night.
- **Activity, "Blow the Paper Ball":** crumple a whole sheet of newspaper into a big ball; lie on the floor facing each
  other and blow it slowly across: one long breath out, "whoooo". *Materials:* one sheet of newspaper.
- **Activity goal:** Practise long, slow breaths out through play.
- **Film can show:** the storm's flashes and rain rhythm, gentle and never startling (each boom is softened and comes
  after a warning flash); the breathing shown as the lamb rising and falling.

### A08 · After the Nap
- **Kind:** value (feelings)
- **Teaching goal:** Cope with goodbye by knowing that the parent comes back after a known moment of the day.
- **Why this age:** by 3 most children calm within ten minutes after a parent leaves them at childcare; managing
  separation is a goal [S, ages §J1.9; CDC by 3; Development Matters].
- **Plot:** At the kindergarten gate Papa kneels down. "After the nap, I come back." Elias holds his sleeve. Then Papa
  is gone, and Elias cries a little. The teacher shows him the goldfish. After the fish, a song. After the song, a
  snack. After the snack, the sandpit. Each time, Elias looks at the door. After the sandpit: the nap. Elias closes his
  eyes on his little mat. When he opens them, there is a sleeve at the door, a sleeve he knows. "After the nap!"
  shouts Elias, and runs.
- **Characters:** Elias (a boy of about two and a half); his father; the kindergarten teacher; the class goldfish.
- **Setting and season:** a kindergarten; autumn; morning to afternoon.
- **Activity, "My Day in Three Pictures":** draw three simple pictures together (breakfast, playing, going home); mix
  them up; the child lays them in order on the floor: first, then, last. *Materials:* three pieces of paper, crayons.
- **Activity goal:** Put three moments of the day in order.
- **Film can show:** time passing in a way a page cannot: the sunlight moving across the kindergarten floor between
  each "after".

### A09 · Hot, Hot, Hands Back!
- **Kind:** skill (safety)
- **Teaching goal:** Pull your hands back and wait when a grown-up says "Hot!".
- **Why this age:** by 3 children avoid touching hot objects when warned; accepting the warning is a goal [S, ages
  §J1.11; CDC by 3].
- **Plot:** Before the sun is up, Dina helps her aunt in the village bakery. Dina pats the dough: pat, pat. Dina
  sprinkles flour: snow on the table! Then Auntie opens the big oven, and warm air rolls out. Dina reaches toward the
  shining loaves. "Hot!" says Auntie. Dina's hands fly back. Hands back, hands on the table. Auntie lays the round
  loaves on the shelf to cool. Dina waits. She blows: whoo, whoo. At last Auntie touches one. "Warm now." Dina holds a
  warm piece in both hands as the first neighbours come in from the cold.
- **Characters:** Dina (a girl of about three); her aunt, the baker; the first neighbours of the morning.
- **Setting and season:** a village bakery; winter; before dawn.
- **Activity, "Little Bakers":** mix flour and water (no salt, no heat) into a soft dough at the table; the child
  squeezes, rolls and pats it into small round "loaves" on a plate; the dough is for play, not for eating. *Materials:*
  flour (rice flour if there is a wheat allergy), water, a bowl, a plate. Hands washed after.
- **Activity goal:** Strengthen the hands by squeezing, rolling and patting dough.
- **Film can show:** heat made visible (shimmering air, steam, the oven's glow) and the dawn light growing over the
  village roofs.

### A10 · Soft Hand, Slow Hand
- **Kind:** value (nature)
- **Teaching goal:** Touch an animal gently, and let it go when it walks away.
- **Why this age:** being gentle with living things is a goal we derive from Development Matters ("living things") and
  the Danish nature theme [D, ages §J1.31].
- **Plot:** A grey cat called Cloud lives in Grandpa's courtyard. Ella runs at her: "Kitty!" Whoosh, Cloud is up on
  the wall. Grandpa sits on the step. "Soft hand, slow hand," he whispers, and holds out his fingers. Cloud comes down.
  She sniffs. Grandpa strokes her back the way the fur goes. Ella tries: soft hand, slow hand. Cloud purrs. Then Cloud
  stands up and walks away to the sunny corner. "Bye, Cloud," says Ella, and lets her go. They wash their hands at
  the tap, and Cloud sleeps in the sun.
- **Characters:** Ella (a girl of about two and a half); her grandfather; Cloud, the courtyard cat.
- **Setting and season:** a village courtyard; spring; afternoon.
- **Activity, "The Feel Bag":** put four big things in a cloth bag (a woolly hat, a large pinecone, a smooth stone, a
  large leaf); the child reaches in, feels one, and you find the word together: soft, rough, smooth. *Materials:* a
  cloth bag and the four objects, all bigger than the tube test.
- **Activity goal:** Name how things feel: soft, rough, smooth.
- **Film can show:** the cat's real behaviour (the leap away, the slow return, a purr shown as a gentle shiver) and the
  direction of a gentle stroke.

### A11 · Round and Round
- **Kind:** skill (shapes)
- **Teaching goal:** Spot circles in everyday things.
- **Why this age:** by 3 a child draws a circle when shown; the circle is the first shape [S, ages §J1.27; CDC by 3].
- **Plot:** Daniel and his uncle wait at the big station for the train to the sea. Daniel finds a round thing: the
  clock on the wall! Another: the light that turns green! Round, round, the buttons on Uncle's shirt. "Round!" says
  Daniel, and draws a circle in the air. Then: whoo-whooo! The train comes in, and under it the wheels turn round and
  round. Daniel and Uncle climb aboard. Through the round window the city slides away, and far ahead the sun, the
  biggest round of all, waits over the sea.
- **Characters:** Daniel (a boy of about three); his uncle; the train driver, who waves.
- **Setting and season:** a city train station; summer; morning.
- **Activity, "Water Circles":** on a sunny pavement or balcony, dip a brush in a cup of water; you paint a big circle,
  the child copies it; watch the circles dry and paint again. *Materials:* a paintbrush or sponge, a cup of water.
- **Activity goal:** Copy a circle drawn by an adult (early drawing).
- **Film can show:** circles in motion (turning wheels, the clock's sweeping hand, a circle drawn in the air that
  leaves a trail of light).

### A12 · Teddy Needs a Wee Too
- **Kind:** skill (body)
- **Teaching goal:** Notice the body's "I need a wee" feeling and go to the potty in time.
- **Why this age:** toilet learning, first with help and then alone, is a goal at 2 to 3 [S, ages §J1.5; Development
  Matters].
- **Plot:** Lian is building with blocks, and Teddy is helping. Suddenly Lian stops. A little feeling, low in her
  tummy. A wee feeling! "Teddy needs a wee too!" Lian grabs Teddy and runs: past the blocks, past the sofa, into the
  bathroom. Teddy sits on the edge of the bath. Lian sits on the potty. Wait... wait... tinkle! "I did it!" Wipe, pants
  up, wash hands with Mama: splash, bubbles. Mama claps. Lian makes Teddy clap. Back to the blocks, Lian builds the
  tallest tower of the whole morning.
- **Characters:** Lian (a girl of about two and a half); her mother; Teddy.
- **Setting and season:** home; spring; morning.
- **Activity, "Dress Teddy, Undress Teddy":** pull a pair of loose, elastic-waist trousers down and up on a big teddy,
  then on the child: down to the knees, up to the tummy, by themselves as far as they can. *Materials:* a big soft toy,
  loose trousers with an elastic waist.
- **Activity goal:** Practise pulling clothes down and up without help.
- **Film can show:** the inside feeling made visible (a small glowing ripple in the tummy) and the comic dash through
  the house.

### A13 · Where Is Little Fox?
- **Kind:** skill (language)
- **Teaching goal:** Understand the position words in, on, under and behind.
- **Why this age:** words for position (in, on, under) are a goal at 2 to 3 [S, ages §J1.21; Development Matters].
- **Also fits:** group B, as a guessing game for younger four-year-olds.
- **Plot:** The hot day is over, and the desert is cool and full of stars. Little Fox wants to play hide-and-seek.
  Mother Fox closes her eyes. Where is Little Fox? Under the bush? No, that's a beetle. Behind the rock? No, that's an
  owl, blinking. On the dune? No, that's only a shadow. Mother Fox turns her big ears this way and that. A tiny giggle...
  In the den! Little Fox is in the den, curled up like a ball. Mother Fox curls around him. "Found you." The owl hoots
  goodnight.
- **Characters:** Little Fox (a fennec cub); Mother Fox; a beetle; an owl.
- **Setting and season:** the desert; summer; night.
- **Activity, "The Box Tunnel":** open both ends of a big cardboard box; the child crawls through it, sits in it, walks
  around it and climbs over a cushion while you say the word for each. *Materials:* a large cardboard box, a cushion.
- **Activity goal:** Move the whole body through, around and over things.
- **Film can show:** the desert night alive (turning stars, blinking eyes, big ears swivelling) and a giggle whose
  direction we hear before we see.

### A14 · Everything Has a Home
- **Kind:** value (helping)
- **Teaching goal:** Help tidy up by putting each thing back in its place.
- **Why this age:** tidying up with a parent is a goal we derive from Development Matters ("we're tidying up") and
  CDC's simple chores [D, ages §J1.29].
- **Plot:** The hedgehog family has a picnic in the pine woods. Then it is time to go home. But everything is
  everywhere! "The cups go home in the basket," says Grandma Hedgehog. Little Hedgehog puts the cups in. "The ball goes
  home in the bag." In! "The blanket goes home..." Little Hedgehog rolls it up, and rolls himself up inside it too.
  Everyone laughs. Last, the apple cores go home in the bin by the path. The grass is clean, a jay sings from a branch,
  and the hedgehogs trot home, everything in its home.
- **Characters:** Little Hedgehog; Grandma Hedgehog; a cousin hedgehog; a jay.
- **Setting and season:** pine woods; spring; daytime.
- **Activity, "Big Box, Little Box":** set out one big box and one small box; gather safe things from around the home
  (a shoe, a sock, a pot, a spoon); the child decides which box each goes in. *Materials:* two boxes of clearly
  different sizes, household objects that pass the tube test.
- **Activity goal:** Compare and sort things by size: big and small.
- **Film can show:** each object "walking home" into its place in a satisfying rhythm; sunlight through the pines.

### A15 · My Turn, Your Turn
- **Kind:** value (with others)
- **Teaching goal:** Take turns with a toy: my turn, then your turn.
- **Why this age:** joining other children's play is a goal by 3 [S, ages §J1.8]; at this age sharing works as
  turn-taking, since real fairness comes later [D, ages §J1.30 and §F8].
- **Plot:** On the beach there is one red bucket and two children. Lea has the bucket. Amir wants the bucket. Pull!
  Pull! Sand everywhere, and both of them cry. A little crab peeps out of its hole. Lea's mother smooths the sand. "Lea
  scoops one. Amir scoops one." Lea scoops. Amir scoops. Lea, Amir, Lea, Amir. The bucket fills up. Flip it over... a
  sandcastle! The crab climbs to the very top. Lea and Amir clap. "Again! My turn, your turn!"
- **Characters:** Lea and Amir (two children of about three who have just met); Lea's mother; a crab.
- **Setting and season:** a sandy beach; autumn; morning.
- **Activity, "Carry It Together":** lay a teddy on a towel; the child and a partner each hold two corners and carry it
  across the room without dropping it. *Materials:* a towel, a soft toy.
- **Activity goal:** Move in step with a partner to carry something together.
- **Film can show:** the rhythm of turns as music (scoop, scoop, scoop), the crab's climb and the waves washing in.

## Group B: ages 4 to 5

Balance: 8 value stories, 7 skill stories. Seasons: summer 4, winter 4, autumn 3, spring 4. Night or dusk: B01, B10,
B11.

### B01 · Ten Goats Home ★ numbers
- **Kind:** skill (numbers)
- **Teaching goal:** Count to ten and know that the last number counted tells how many there are.
- **Why this age:** counting one item per number, beyond five toward ten, and knowing that the last number counted is
  the total are goals at 4 to 5 [S, ages §J2.26; Development Matters].
- **Plot:** Every autumn evening Rafi helps his aunt bring the goats down from the hills. At the gate he counts them
  in: one, two, three... nine. Nine? "We have ten goats," says Auntie. Rafi counts again, slowly, touching each warm
  back. Nine. Where is Speckle, the smallest? They walk back up the path in the purple light. From behind the old water
  trough comes a tiny "meh". Speckle is stuck between two stones, munching a thistle. Rafi lifts her out. At the gate he
  counts once more, and the last one in is Speckle. "Ten!" Ten goats home.
- **Characters:** Rafi (a boy of five); his aunt, a shepherd; Speckle, the smallest goat; the other nine goats.
- **Setting and season:** hills above a village; autumn; dusk.
- **Activity, "Ten in the Pen, Then One Less":** draw ten squares with chalk or on paper and put a pinecone in each;
  count them; the child closes their eyes while you take one away; count again: how many now? Swap roles.
  *Materials:* chalk or paper, ten pinecones or stones.
- **Activity goal:** See that taking one away makes one less (ten, then nine).
- **Film can show:** the flock flowing down the hill, each goat jumping through the gate on a counting beat (the
  numbers are heard, never written in the picture); the search in the dusk.

### B02 · The Shell That Sings ★ respect for classmates
- **Kind:** value
- **Teaching goal:** Respect a classmate by listening quietly while they speak.
- **Why this age:** building "constructive and respectful relationships" and learning how and why to listen are
  Reception goals [S, ages §J2.10 and §D1]. Respect for classmates as such has no specific trusted source [D, ages §M].
- **Plot:** It is "bring something" day, and rain drums on the kindergarten roof. Everyone has something loud: a toy
  drum, a squeaky duck, a whistle. Dalia has a shell. When her turn comes, her voice is very small, and the room is very
  loud. Nobody hears. Dalia closes her hand around the shell. Then a boy at the back puts a finger to his lips: shhh.
  The girl beside him does too. Shhh, shhh, around the circle, until only the rain is left. Dalia lifts the shell to
  her ear. "If you are quiet," she whispers, "it sings the sea." One by one, they all listen.
- **Characters:** Dalia (a quiet girl of five); her classmates; their teacher.
- **Setting and season:** a kindergarten; winter; a rainy morning.
- **Activity, "The Talking Stick":** sit in a circle with a stick from the garden; only the person holding it speaks;
  before saying their own thing, the next holder repeats one thing they heard from the last. *Materials:* a stick or a
  wooden spoon.
- **Activity goal:** Repeat back what another person said (active listening).
- **Film can show:** sound as the story: the loud room fading out, child by child, until only rain is left, and then
  the faint sound of the sea from the shell.

### B03 · The Chalk Sun
- **Kind:** value (feelings)
- **Teaching goal:** When you are angry, say what is wrong with words, not with your hands.
- **Why this age:** handling anger with words and asking for help instead of hitting is a goal at 3 to 5 [S, ages
  §J2.2; Development Matters; CDC 3 to 5].
- **Plot:** Rami spends the whole morning drawing a giant chalk sun on the pavement. Then Nina rides her scooter
  straight across it. Swish: a stripe through the middle. Rami's face gets hot. His hands make fists. He wants to push
  her scooter over. Instead he stamps his foot and shouts the truest thing he has: "I'M ANGRY! You rode over my sun!"
  Nina stops and looks down. "I didn't see it. Sorry." Rami stares at the stripe. It looks a bit like a sunbeam. Nina
  picks up the yellow chalk. Together they draw beams all round, until the sun is bigger than before.
- **Characters:** Rami (a boy of five); Nina (a girl of five on a scooter); Rami's mother, watching from the balcony.
- **Setting and season:** a city pavement outside a block of flats; summer; morning.
- **Activity, "How Big Is My Feeling?":** draw a ladder of three steps on a strip of paper (small, medium, big) and clip
  a clothes peg on it; tell little "what if" moments ("your tower falls", "a friend takes your seat") and the child moves
  the peg to show how big the feeling would be. *Materials:* a strip of paper, a crayon, a clothes peg.
- **Activity goal:** Show how big a feeling is on a three-step scale.
- **Film can show:** anger as colour and heat rising through the picture and cooling again; the chalk sun growing in
  fast motion.

### B04 · The Clay Bird
- **Kind:** value (honesty)
- **Teaching goal:** Tell the truth after a mistake, and see that it makes things better.
- **Why this age:** in a study of 268 children aged 3 to 7, a story where honesty was praised increased truth-telling,
  while stories that punished lying did not [S, ages §F7 and §J2.9; one laboratory study].
- **Also fits:** group C (the study covered ages up to 7).
- **Plot:** At the spring market Grandma stops to buy herbs, and Lara wanders to the potter's stall. Clay birds stand
  in a row; when you blow into their tails, they sing. Lara picks up the smallest, and it slips. Crack! A wing breaks
  off. Nobody saw. Lara's heart thumps. She could put it back and walk away. Instead she carries the bird and the wing
  to the potter. "I dropped it. I'm sorry." The old potter looks at her a long moment, then smiles. "Thank you for
  telling me. Come, let's mend it." Together they press wet clay into the crack, and the mended bird sings a crooked,
  happy song.
- **Characters:** Lara (a girl of five); her grandmother; the old potter.
- **Setting and season:** a market; spring; late morning.
- **Activity, "The Fix-It Table":** the adult tears an old drawing or a magazine page into four or five big pieces; the
  child puts it back together like a puzzle and tapes the cracks. *Materials:* an old drawing or magazine page, sticky
  tape.
- **Activity goal:** Repair something broken, step by step.
- **Film can show:** the clay bird's song (sound), the slow-motion fall, and the potter's hands working wet clay.

### B05 · The Basket Moved
- **Kind:** value (thinking about others)
- **Teaching goal:** Understand that a friend does not know what they did not see, and tell them.
- **Why this age:** at about age 4 children begin to understand that others can believe something that is not true
  (a meta-analysis of 178 studies) [S, ages §F9 and §J2.11].
- **Also fits:** group C.
- **Plot:** At the oasis it is date-picking time. Yasmin hides a basket of dates under the big palm, a surprise for the
  grown-ups when their work is done. Then she runs off to help. Her little cousin finds the basket sitting in the sun.
  "The dates will get hot," he thinks, and carries it to the shade by the well. Later Yasmin runs back to the big palm.
  No basket! She searches and searches. Her cousin watches, puzzled... then he understands. "She didn't see me move
  it!" He takes her hand and leads her to the well. The surprise is safe, the dates are cool, and the tired grown-ups
  cheer.
- **Characters:** Yasmin (a girl of five); her little cousin (four); the grown-ups picking dates; a camel resting by
  the well.
- **Setting and season:** a desert oasis; autumn; afternoon.
- **Activity, "Find It by My Words":** one player hides a toy while the other waits outside the room; then guide them
  to it with words only: "behind the chair", "under the cushion", "next to the door". Swap. *Materials:* one toy.
- **Activity goal:** Give clear spoken directions so someone else can find a hidden thing.
- **Film can show:** what one character sees and the other does not, by cutting between them: the viewer watches the
  basket move while Yasmin is away.

### B06 · Stand Still, Wave High
- **Kind:** skill (safety)
- **Teaching goal:** If you are lost, stand still and ask a worker or a parent with children for help.
- **Why this age:** stranger safety is a goal at 3 to 5 [S, ages §J2.15; CDC 3 to 5]; the "stand still and ask a
  worker" advice is our choice of what to teach, not a sourced rule [D].
- **Plot:** The beach is crowded and bright. Sam chases the waves, and when he turns round, every umbrella looks the
  same. Where is Papa's green one? Sam's tummy goes tight. He remembers what Papa told him: stand still. So he stands
  still, right where the sand is wet, and looks for a helper. The lifeguard on her tower! Sam waves both arms, high and
  slow. The lifeguard waves back and comes down. Sam tells her his full name and Papa's name, just as he practised.
  Her voice booms through the loudspeaker, and soon Papa comes running, laughing and hugging, his green umbrella under
  his arm.
- **Characters:** Sam (a boy of five); his father; the lifeguard.
- **Setting and season:** a crowded beach; summer; midday.
- **Activity, "My Name, Your Name":** play helper: the adult asks "What's your name? Who are you with?"; the child
  answers with their full name and the full name of their grown-up; swap roles. *Materials:* none.
- **Activity goal:** Say one's own full name and a grown-up's full name clearly.
- **Film can show:** the crowd from a small child's height (umbrellas towering), then the camera rising to the tower to
  show Sam as one still, waving figure.

### B07 · Snow Day
- **Kind:** value (with others)
- **Teaching goal:** Say "Stop, I don't like that" clearly, and stop when someone says it to you.
- **Why this age:** being assertive in fitting ways, saying "stop" and "I don't like that", is a goal at 3 to 4 [S, ages
  §J2.4; Development Matters].
- **Plot:** Snow! It almost never snows on their hill, and everyone is outside. Alma and Yousef throw snowballs at the
  big tree. Then Yousef throws one at Alma's face. Cold! Then again, and again, laughing. Alma's cheeks sting. She wants
  to cry, but she plants her feet and holds up her hand. "Stop. I don't like that." Yousef freezes, a snowball in his
  hand. He looks at her red cheeks. He drops it. "Only the tree?" Alma nods. Then they build a snow lady with a
  pinecone nose, and Yousef's grandmother calls them in for warm milk.
- **Characters:** Alma (a girl of five); Yousef (a boy of five); Yousef's grandmother.
- **Setting and season:** a hill town in a rare snowfall; winter; morning.
- **Activity, "My Bubble":** each player stands inside a circle of cushions (or a chalk circle); to come into someone
  else's circle you must ask "Can I come in?" and wait for the answer; practise both "yes" and "no, thank you".
  *Materials:* cushions or chalk.
- **Activity goal:** Understand personal space and ask before entering it.
- **Film can show:** falling snow in slow motion, breath clouds, and the snowball stopping in mid-air on "Stop".

### B08 · The Soap Song
- **Kind:** skill (hygiene)
- **Teaching goal:** Wash hands with soap, every part of them, before eating and after playing outside.
- **Why this age:** washing and drying hands is a goal at 3 to 4 [S, ages §J2.17; Development Matters]; handwashing
  after outdoor play and animals is CDC advice [S, safety §1.11].
- **Plot:** Nadia spends the morning in Grandpa's garden, digging with a spoon, planting onions, scratching the donkey
  behind the ears. At lunchtime there is warm bread on the table, and her hands reach straight for it. Grandpa catches
  her fingers. "First, the soap song!" At the outside tap they sing: palms rub, backs rub, fingers knot, thumbs turn,
  nails scratch. The water runs brown, then grey, then clear. Back at the table, Grandpa reaches for the bread with his
  own muddy hands, and Nadia catches his fingers. "Grandpa! The soap song!" Laughing, they go back to the tap together.
- **Characters:** Nadia (a girl of four); her grandfather; a donkey.
- **Setting and season:** a village garden; spring; noon.
- **Activity, "Pepper Runs Away":** pour a little water into a plate; the adult sprinkles ground pepper on top; the
  child touches the middle with a dry finger (nothing happens), then with a finger dipped in soap: the pepper rushes to
  the edges. *Materials:* a plate, water, ground pepper (adult only, kept away from eyes), liquid soap.
- **Activity goal:** See how soap pushes dirt away (a first experiment).
- **Film can show:** the hand-washing steps danced to the song's rhythm, and the water changing colour as the dirt
  goes.

### B09 · The Kite That Kept Falling
- **Kind:** value (effort)
- **Teaching goal:** Keep trying when something is hard, and try a new way.
- **Why this age:** resilience and perseverance are Reception goals [S, ages §J2.12; Development Matters].
- **Plot:** The first windy day of autumn comes, and Rita runs up the hill with the kite she made with her uncle. She
  runs; the kite drags along the grass. She runs again; it flips and crashes. Again; it tangles in a bush. Rita sits
  down hard. "It's a bad kite." Her uncle sits beside her and says nothing. They watch the long grass bend. Rita
  notices: the grass bends away from the sea. The wind comes from there! She turns to face it, holds the kite up high
  and lets go, without running at all. The kite climbs, wobbles... and flies.
- **Characters:** Rita (a girl of five); her uncle.
- **Setting and season:** a grassy hill in a city park; autumn; a windy afternoon.
- **Activity, "The Cup Tower Test":** build the tallest tower you can from paper cups; when it falls, change one thing
  (a wider base, cups upside down, fewer per row) and try again; count the tries. *Materials:* 10 to 20 paper cups.
- **Activity goal:** Improve a build by changing one thing after each try.
- **Film can show:** the wind made visible (grass waves, racing clouds) and the kite's dives and final climb.

### B10 · The Big Sister Song
- **Kind:** value (family)
- **Teaching goal:** Feel loved and find a helping role when a new baby comes.

### B11 · Drum My Name
- **Kind:** skill (letters and sounds)
- **Teaching goal:** Hear the beats (syllables) in a word by clapping them.
- **Why this age:** counting and clapping syllables is a goal at 3 to 4 [S, ages §J2.24; Development Matters].
- **Plot:** Natalia has just moved to the city and knows nobody. In the square, an old woman plays a big drum every
  evening, and children sit round her. "Give me a word, and I'll drum it." "Cat!" Boom. "Pigeon!" Boom-boom.
  "Watermelon!" Boom-boom-boom-boom. The children clap along. Natalia whispers her own name. The drummer leans close,
  listens, and drums it, beat by beat. "The longest name in the square!" Everyone claps it with her, again and again.
  Walking home under the street lamps, Natalia claps everything she sees.
- **Characters:** Natalia (a girl of five, new in town); the old drummer; the children of the square; pigeons.
- **Setting and season:** a city square; spring; evening.
- **Note for writers:** each language chooses its own words with one, two, three and four beats; the name must be the
  longest word drummed in that language's text.
- **Activity, "Name Drum":** turn a pot upside down; drum the names of everyone in the family, one beat for each part;
  whose name has the most beats? *Materials:* a pot, a wooden spoon.
- **Activity goal:** Compare which word is longer by counting its beats.
- **Film can show:** the drumbeats themselves. Because beat counts differ between languages, the drum track is laid
  per language with the voice (Stage 10), not baked into the shared picture.

### B12 · Ball, Box, Tube, Cone
- **Kind:** skill (shapes)
- **Teaching goal:** Tell solid shapes apart: ball, cube, cylinder and cone.
- **Why this age:** talking about flat and solid shapes is a goal at 3 to 5 [S, ages §J2.28; Development Matters].
- **Plot:** Dana's mother is building a house at the edge of the village, and today Dana may come, in her own yellow
  helmet. Everything has a shape. Bricks like boxes, stacked high. Pipes like tubes. Orange cones in a row. Dana has
  brought her ball. Then a pipe starts rolling down the slope toward the builders' lunch. "Boxes don't roll!" shouts
  Dana, and her mother wedges a brick in front of it just in time. The builders cheer. At lunch, Dana builds a tiny
  house from a bread box, a cup and a cone-shaped paper hat, and the builders say she is hired.
- **Characters:** Dana (a girl of five); her mother, the builder; two builders.
- **Setting and season:** a building site in a village; summer; morning to noon.
- **Activity, "Roll or Stack?":** collect things from the home (a ball, a box, a tube, a cup, a funnel); test each:
  does it roll? does it stack? Sort them into "rolls", "stacks" and "both". *Materials:* household objects.
- **Activity goal:** Test which solid shapes roll and which stack.
- **Film can show:** shapes doing what their shape lets them do (rolling, stacking, turning), which a still picture can
  only hint at.

### B13 · The Blue-White Path
- **Kind:** skill (patterns)
- **Teaching goal:** Continue an AB pattern, and spot a mistake in it.
- **Why this age:** extending ABAB patterns and spotting a mistake in a pattern are goals at 3 to 5 [S, ages §J2.29;

### B15 · The Seed That Took Its Time
- **Kind:** skill (nature)
- **Teaching goal:** Know that a seed needs soil, water, light and time to grow.
- **Why this age:** investigating living things, plants and weather is a goal at 3 to 5 [S, ages §J2.33; Development
  Matters; the Danish nature theme].
- **Plot:** Every child in the kindergarten plants one chickpea in a cup of soil and sets it on the windowsill. Every
  day they water it and look. Day two: nothing. Day four: nothing. One cup has been pushed into the dark corner behind
  the curtain. Then, one morning, little green hooks push up in the sunny cups, and the children cheer. But the corner
  cup stays bare. They carry it into the light, water it, and wait. Days later a pale shoot appears, leans toward the
  window, and slowly turns green. On the last day of spring, all the seedlings go out into the garden together.
- **Characters:** the children of one kindergarten class; their teacher; a garden snail.
- **Setting and season:** a kindergarten and its garden; spring; several weeks.
- **Activity, "Watch It Grow":** the adult lays a few dry chickpeas on damp kitchen paper inside a clear cup (the adult
  handles the dry seeds: they are a choking risk and never for eating); keep it damp on a windowsill; each day the child
  draws what they see on a strip of paper. *Materials:* a clear plastic cup, kitchen paper, a few dry chickpeas,
  paper, crayons.
- **Activity goal:** Observe a change and draw it every day (an observation diary).
- **Film can show:** time-lapse growth: weeks pressed into seconds, the shoot bending toward the light.

### C01 · Ten Pomegranates, Two Baskets ★ numbers
- **Kind:** skill (numbers)
- **Teaching goal:** Split ten into two parts in different ways (the number pairs that make ten).
- **Why this age:** number bonds and numbers beyond ten are maths goals from Reception onward; school curricula for 6 to
  8 were not opened, so the exact year is our judgement [D, ages §J3.28].
- **Plot:** At the autumn market, Sofia and Liam help the pomegranate seller, a woman with a big laugh. Ten
  pomegranates are left, and two baskets. "The baker wants some, the doctor wants some." Liam makes five and five. Then
  the baker hurries over: "Seven for my juice!" Sofia moves two across: seven and three. The doctor calls: "Only two
  today!" Eight and two? The baker frowns: "Then I'll take eight." Back and forth the pomegranates go, and the children
  notice something: however they move them, there are always ten. When both customers leave happy, the seller takes
  one more from her apron, cracks it open, and shares the shining seeds.
- **Characters:** Sofia (a girl of seven); Liam (a boy of seven); the pomegranate seller; the baker; the doctor.
- **Setting and season:** a market; autumn; afternoon.
- **Activity, "Bowling with Ten":** stand ten cardboard tubes up as skittles; roll a ball; count how many fell and how
  many stand; play two rounds and add your two scores. *Materials:* ten cardboard tubes, a ball, paper and a pencil.
- **Activity goal:** Add two scores together to find a total.
- **Film can show:** pomegranates juggled between baskets on a musical beat, so the viewer feels the total stay the
  same; the jewel-like seeds when the fruit cracks open.

### C02 · The Slow Round ★ respect for classmates
- **Kind:** value
- **Teaching goal:** Invite a classmate who is left out into the game.
- **Why this age:** friendship, teamwork and including others matter more and more at 6 to 8 [S, ages §J3.9; CDC 6 to
  8]; kindness to classmates and no exclusion is our derived goal [D, ages §J3.14].
- **Plot:** Every break, the jump-rope game in the corner of the playground goes faster and faster. Jonas always trips,
  and the others groan, so one day he stops coming and sits on the bench instead. Mira notices. She
  remembers her own first week, when nobody let her turn the rope. She walks over. "Want to turn with me?" Jonas turns
  slowly, then slower, and slow is just right for the little first-graders, who hop in for the very first time. Soon
  there is a new rule: every third round is a Slow Round, for anyone. Soon the Slow Round has the longest
  line in the playground.
- **Characters:** Mira (a girl of eight); Jonas (a boy of eight); the jump-rope players; the first-graders.
- **Setting and season:** a school playground; spring; break time.
- **Activity, "Clap the Rope Rhyme":** learn a short skipping or clapping chant (one the children already
  know, or a new one they make up); the group keeps the beat with claps, then one pair turns a long rope slowly to the
  beat while others jump in one at a time. *Materials:* a long rope, kept on the ground when not turned, never around a
  neck.
- **Activity goal:** Keep one shared rhythm as a group.
- **Film can show:** speed as feeling: the fast rope as a blur and the slow rope as a wide, visible arc; a lonely bench
  that ends up crowded.

- **Kind:** value

### C06 · One Note at a Time
- **Kind:** value (effort)

### C07 · The Crossing Leader
- **Kind:** skill (safety)

  the first way again", and the pictures follow traffic as it drives in Israel.
- **Activity, "Map My Way":** walk a short route together (to the shop, the park or school); at home the child draws a
  map of it and marks each crossing; then walk it again, map in hand. *Materials:* paper, crayons.
- **Activity goal:** Draw and read a simple map.
- **Film can show:** traffic in real time: the timing of cars and lights, the stopping car, the beep of the crossing.

### C08 · The Ball the Sea Took
- **Kind:** skill (safety)
- **Teaching goal:** Never chase anything into the water; swim only where a lifeguard and an adult are watching.
- **Why this age:** water safety is a CDC goal at 6 to 8 [S, ages §J3.18]; young children can drown in very little
  water, quickly and silently [S, safety §1.7, AAP].
- **Plot:** It is early morning at the beach, before the lifeguards arrive, and the sea is wavy. Miriam and her father
  play ball on the sand. A gust lifts the ball and drops it into the water, and the waves begin to carry it away.
  Miriam runs to the edge and stops, toes in the foam. The lifeguard tower is empty. She watches the ball bob out,
  farther and farther. "Papa!" Papa looks, and takes her hand. "Let it go." At nine the lifeguard arrives, laughing,
  with a ball under her arm: the sea has washed it up by her tower. Then Miriam and Papa swim, between the flags,
  together.
- **Characters:** Miriam (a girl of seven); her father; the lifeguard.
- **Setting and season:** the sea; summer; early morning.
- **Activity, "Boat Test":** fold a small boat from aluminium foil and float it in a basin of shallow water; add pebbles
  one at a time; how many before it sinks? Change the shape and try again. An adult watches the water the whole time.
  *Materials:* foil, a basin with a few centimetres of water, pebbles.
- **Activity goal:** Test how shape and weight change what floats.
- **Film can show:** the sea's pull: the ball drifting farther with each wave, measured against a still girl on the
  shore.

### C09 · Next Episode in 5, 4, 3...
- **Kind:** skill (screen habits)
- **Teaching goal:** Decide yourself when a screen show ends, instead of letting "next" decide.
- **Why this age:** the AAP warns about autoplay and endless scroll and advises clear family rules for screens at 6 to
  12 [S, ages §J3.23, §J3.20 and §C].
- **Also fits:** group B (sensible amounts of screen time is a Reception goal, ages §J2.20).
- **Plot:** It is the long summer holiday, and Max is watching a cartoon. When it ends, a little clock appears: next
  episode in 5, 4, 3... and the next one starts by itself. Then the next. Outside, the light turns orange. Max's legs
  have gone to sleep. He had planned to finish his cardboard rocket today. When the next countdown starts, Max does
  something new: he presses stop before it reaches one. The room is suddenly very quiet. He stretches, finds the rocket
  under the bed, and by evening it stands in the garden, painted silver, with the first star right above its tip.
- **Characters:** Max (a boy of seven); his mother; the cartoon characters on the screen.
- **Setting and season:** home and garden; summer; afternoon into evening.
- **Activity, "The Off-Screen Cube":** cover a small box with paper; on each of its six sides draw something you love
  to do away from screens; when a show ends, roll the cube and do what comes up. *Materials:* a small box, paper,
  crayons, tape.
- **Activity goal:** Make a tool for choosing off-screen play.
- **Film can show:** a screen inside the screen: the countdown, and the light outside changing in fast motion while
  Max does not move. (StoryTime's own player has no autoplay, so the film practises what it preaches.)

### C10 · Long-Legs Flies South
- **Kind:** skill (nature)
- **Teaching goal:** Learn that many birds migrate far every autumn and spring, and why.
- **Why this age:** nature and science are goals at 6 to 8 [D, ages §J3.29; Danish nature theme; the Israeli science
  programme exists but was not opened].
- **Fact check before writing:** the stork facts below (thermal soaring, autumn passage over Israel to Africa, return in
  spring, resting at valley lakes) are general knowledge, not in our research files [U]. Check each one against a
  bird-migration source before the text is written (project rule 4).
- **Plot:** Long-Legs is a young stork. The air is turning cool, and all around him storks are gathering. "Why must we
  go?" he asks. "Where we are going, there will be food when the cold comes here," says his mother. They fly for days,
  riding the warm air that rises from the ground, circling higher and higher so they hardly need to flap. Over a green
  valley with a lake, thousands of storks drop down to rest and eat. On a hill, a girl and her grandfather watch them
  through binoculars. Then the warm air rises again, and up goes Long-Legs, circling, south.
- **Characters:** Long-Legs (a young stork); his mother; a girl and her grandfather, bird-watching.
- **Setting and season:** the sky, and a valley with a lake; autumn; days of flight.
- **Activity, "Sky Tally":** sit outside for 15 minutes in the shade, at a cool hour; each time a bird flies past, make
  a tally mark under small, medium or big; at the end, count the tallies in fives. *Materials:* paper, a pencil.
- **Activity goal:** Count with tally marks while observing nature.
- **Film can show:** what the book cannot: thermals made visible as slowly turning towers of birds, the scale of
  thousands, the land changing below.

### C11 · Meet Me at Four
- **Kind:** skill (time)
- **Teaching goal:** Read a clock to the hour and the half hour.
- **Why this age:** simple time-telling belongs to the 6 to 8 maths goals; school curricula were not opened, so the
  exact level is our judgement [D, ages §J3.28].
- **Plot:** Laura is always late, because she can't read the big clock in the square. Her grandfather winds that clock
  every week, and one day he takes her up the narrow stairs inside the tower. Behind the giant face, among the turning
  wheels, he shows her: the short hand points to the hour; when the long hand points straight up, it's o'clock; straight
  down, half past. Laura practises all week. Then her friend says, "Meet me at the fountain at four." At half past three
  Laura looks up: half an hour to go. When the bells ring four, she is already waiting at the fountain.
- **Characters:** Laura (a girl of seven); her grandfather, the clock keeper; her friend.
- **Setting and season:** a city square with a clock tower; spring; afternoon.
- **Note for writers:** in Danish "halv fire" means half past three; the text and the clock in the picture must agree.
- **Activity, "Shadow Clock":** on a sunny morning, stand a stick upright in a pot of sand or soil; every hour, mark the
  tip of the shadow with a stone and note the time; watch the shadow move and shorten, then lengthen. *Materials:* a
  stick, a pot of sand or soil, stones, paper, a pencil.
- **Activity goal:** See how the sun's shadow moves through the day.
- **Film can show:** time itself moving: hands sweeping in fast motion, gears turning inside the tower, the bells
  counting four.

### C12 · Words and Pictures
- **Kind:** value (reading together)
- **Teaching goal:** Enjoy reading aloud to someone, taking turns with the words and the pictures.
- **Why this age:** CDC advises reading together and taking turns once the child reads [S, ages §J3.26].
- **Plot:** Tamara's little brother broke his leg falling off a swing, and now he lies in a hospital bed with a white
  cast and a grumpy face. Tamara has just learned to read. She brings her favourite book and reads it to him, slowly,
  her finger under each word. She gets stuck on a long word, and her brother, who knows the story by heart, shouts it
  out. So they make a deal: she reads the words, he reads the pictures, telling what everyone in them is doing. The boy
  in the next bed listens. Then the nurse. By evening a small crowd sits around the bed, and nobody is grumpy.
- **Characters:** Tamara (a girl of six); her little brother (four); their mother; a nurse; the boy in the next bed.
- **Setting and season:** a children's ward in a city hospital; autumn; afternoon into evening.
- **Activity, "Fold-a-Book":** fold one sheet of paper into an eight-page mini-book (the adult shows the folds and
  checks the one cut); the child writes and draws a story with a beginning, a middle and an end, then reads it aloud.
  *Materials:* one sheet of A4 paper, child scissors, pencils.
- **Activity goal:** Make a book with a beginning, a middle and an end.
- **Film can show:** the pictures in Tamara's book coming alive as her brother tells them, a story inside the story.

### C13 · After the Storm
- **Kind:** value (helping)
- **Teaching goal:** Notice people in need and help them as a team.
- **Why this age:** helping people in need and joining groups or volunteering are CDC goals at 6 to 8 [S, ages §J3.1
  and §J3.8].
- **Plot:** A winter storm blows all night. In the morning the village street is covered in branches, and the old woman
  at the end of the street can't open her gate: a big branch lies across it, and her hens are waiting for breakfast.
  Diana sees her waving from the window. Diana pushes the branch. It doesn't move. So she knocks on doors: the twins,
  the boy from the bakery, the girl with the wagon. Five children, one branch. "One, two, three, lift!" It moves.
  Someone feeds the hens, someone sweeps. By noon the whole street is clear, and the old woman brings out warm bread for
  five.
- **Characters:** Diana (a girl of eight); the old woman at the end of the street; four children from the street; the
  old woman's hens.
- **Setting and season:** a village street; winter; the morning after a storm.
- **Activity, "Thank-You Cards":** think of people who help everyone (the street cleaner, the bus driver, the school
  guard, the nurse); write or draw a short thank-you card for one of them and deliver it together. *Materials:*
  paper, pencils, crayons.
- **Activity goal:** Write a short message for a real reader.
- **Film can show:** the storm's power at night through sound and moving shadows, the still morning after, and the
  group lift in one rhythm.

### C14 · Two Ideas, One Raft
- **Kind:** value (with others)
- **Teaching goal:** Settle a disagreement with a friend by listening and making a plan you both accept.
- **Why this age:** solving small disagreements on their own is a CDC goal at 6 to 8 [S, ages §J3.5].
- **Plot:** By a shallow stream in the woods, Celine and Rani decide to build a raft for their toy animals, while
  Rani's father reads on the bank. Celine wants a tall sail. Rani wants a wide, flat floor. They argue, then each
  builds alone, crossly. Celine's tall raft tips over at once. Rani's flat raft floats but won't move. They sit on the
  bank, not talking. Then Rani says, "Tell me about your sail." "The wind pushes it." "My floor doesn't tip." Together
  they build a flat raft with a small sail, and the breeze carries a toy hedgehog all the way across the stream.
- **Characters:** Celine (a girl of seven); Rani (a boy of seven); Rani's father; dragonflies.
- **Setting and season:** woods with a shallow stream; summer; afternoon.
- **Activity, "The Paper Bridge":** lay a sheet of paper between two books as a bridge; add coins or pebbles until it
  sags; then fold the paper into a zigzag and test again; pairs must agree on one design before testing. *Materials:*
  paper, two books, pebbles or coins (counted back afterwards).
- **Activity goal:** Discover that folding paper makes it stronger.
- **Film can show:** physics in motion: the tall raft tipping, the flat raft stuck, the combined raft sailing on the
  breeze.

### C15 · The Bottle That Looked Like Juice
- **Kind:** skill (safety)
- **Teaching goal:** Never taste or open an unknown bottle or pill; leave it and ask an adult.
- **Why this age:** keeping harmful products out of reach is a CDC goal at 6 to 8; most poisonings happen when
  caregivers are home but not paying attention [S, ages §J3.19; safety §1.3].
- **Plot:** It is spring-cleaning day at Grandma's house, and Rose is helping in the dusty storeroom. She is hot and
  thirsty. Behind a box she finds a bottle of bright orange liquid. It looks exactly like juice. Her hand goes to the
  cap, and stops. Who keeps juice in a storeroom? She sets the bottle down and calls Grandma. Grandma reads the faded
  label: it is cleaner for the oven. Her face goes pale, and then she hugs Rose very tight. Together they move every
  bottle to the top shelf, and Rose draws a big red hand for the door: "Ask first." Then they share real orange juice
  on the step.
- **Characters:** Rose (a girl of seven); her grandmother.
- **Setting and season:** Grandma's house in a village; spring; a cleaning day.
- **Activity, "Warning-Sign Detectives":** the adult takes a few closed cleaning products down and holds them while the
  child finds the warning symbols on the labels (the child never holds or opens them); then the child draws a "Stop,
  ask a grown-up" sign for the cupboard. *Materials:* the household's own closed products (adult only), paper,
  crayons.
- **Activity goal:** Recognise the warning symbols on labels.
- **Film can show:** temptation and its pause: a shaft of light making the bottle glow like juice, the hand slowing in
  close-up, the music stopping when Rose stops.
