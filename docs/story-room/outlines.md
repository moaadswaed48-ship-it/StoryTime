# The 45 story outlines

Fifteen stories per age group: **A01 to A15** for ages 2 to 3, **B01 to B15** for ages 4 to 5, **C01 to C15** for
ages 6 to 8. Each outline is the brief a writer, the Studio and the activity designer work from. Titles are working
titles in English; each language names its own book.

**Read with:** the age brief for the group (`age-2-3.md`, `age-4-5.md`, `age-6-8.md`) and the style guide for the
language. Research references: `ages §J1.24` means `docs/research/ages-raw.md`, section J1, goal 24; `safety §1.5`
means `docs/research/activities-safety-raw.md`, section 1.5. Tags: [S] the source states it, [D] our judgement from
the nearest source, [U] unverified.

**Revised 22 Nov 2026 after an editorial review.** 41 of the 45 outlines were changed: 11 rewritten in full, 15 with a
real change of plot, cast or activity, 15 with smaller fixes; every activity gained a class version. Every change, and
the reason for it, is in `REVIEW.md`.

**Marks used below**
- ★ a lesson the owner named (respect for the elderly, respect for classmates, being good to one's mother, numbers).
- **Kind:** *value* (behaviour, feelings, relationships) or *skill* (numbers, colours, shapes, letters, time, nature,
  body, safety, hygiene).
- **Also fits:** the lesson suits a second group; the story may be offered there too (master plan §3.2).
- **In a class:** how a kindergarten or school teacher runs the same activity with a group (master plan §3.6).

**Rules every outline follows:** one teaching goal, unique across all 45; a beginning, a turn and a warm ending; no
moral spoken at the end; no lesson taught by punishment (honesty and kindness are shown with a good result, as the
research advises, ages §F7); no character appears in two stories; no two stories share a plot shape, a main setting
and a feeling at once (see the shape table in `REVIEW.md`); settings and seasons spread across home, kindergarten,
school, market, sea, desert, hills, woods, city and village, day and night, all four seasons; nothing resembles the old
autumn story of a boy and falling leaves (no falling leaves appear in any story). Every activity uses free or
near-free materials, passes the red list in its group's brief, works for one parent with one child and for a teacher
with a group, and can be shown in four or five simple pictures.

**Drawing rule for every outline:** pictures and films are paper-cut puppets drawn in code. No crowds (at most eight
figures in one picture; a "full" bus or class is shown by a few figures and repeated shapes), no detailed machines,
water as flat layered bands, flocks as one repeated silhouette. No digits or words inside any picture: clock faces
carry twelve marks, countdowns are shrinking dots (master plan §7: one picture serves four languages).

## Group A: ages 2 to 3

Balance: 7 value stories, 8 skill stories. Seasons: summer 4, autumn 4, winter 3, spring 4. Night, dusk or dawn:
A06, A07, A09, A11, A13.

### A01 · One, Two, Three, Wheee! ★ numbers
- **Kind:** skill (numbers)
- **Teaching goal:** Count three things, saying one number for each thing.
- **Why this age:** counting objects to about three to five and finger-number rhymes are goals for ages 2 to 3 [S, ages
  §J1.24; Development Matters maths and Reach Out and Read 24 to 30 months].
- **Plot:** Every morning, before the day gets hot, Adam and Papa go to the little playground. The little slide has
  three steps. Adam climbs. "One! Two! Three!" Wheee! Again: one, two, three, wheee! Then Papa wants a turn. One, two,
  three... Papa sits at the top with his knees up by his ears. Wheee? No. Papa is stuck! Adam pushes Papa's back.
  "One, two, THREE!" Pop! Papa slides all the way down, laughing. On the way home, three pigeons sit on a wall. Adam
  points: one, two, three. The pigeons fly away: wheee!
- **Characters:** Adam (a boy of about two and a half); his father; three pigeons.
- **Setting and season:** a small playground between city flats; summer; early morning.
- **Activity, "One Stone, One Cup":** set out three cups in a row; the child drops one big stone into each while you
  count together, then tips them out and starts again. *Materials:* three paper or plastic cups, three smooth stones
  bigger than the child's fist (tube test, safety §1.1). *In a class:* each child has three cups and three stones; the
  teacher counts aloud and the children drop on each number.
- **Activity goal:** Put one object in each place (one-to-one matching).
- **Film can show:** the three steps as three rising notes, Papa's comic squeeze and pop, the pigeons lifting off in
  three flaps.

### A02 · The Sparrow's Warm Nest
- **Kind:** skill (colours)
- **Teaching goal:** Name four colours: red, yellow, green and blue.
- **Why this age:** pointing to and naming colours is a goal at 24 to 30 months [S, ages §J1.23; Reach Out and Read].
- **Plot:** On a cold winter morning a little sparrow wakes on the market roof. Her nest is grey and thin. She hops
  down to the stalls. Yellow lemons: too heavy. Green cucumbers: too cold. Red tomatoes: too squishy. "Not yellow, not
  green, not red." At the very last stall an old man is knitting, and little bits of wool fall around his feet: red,
  yellow, green and one long blue piece. The sparrow carries them up, one by one. Now her nest is the warmest and
  brightest on the roof. She sits in the middle, on the blue, and sings down to the old man. He waves his needles.
- **Characters:** the sparrow; the old man who knits at the wool stall; three sellers in the background.
- **Setting and season:** a city market; winter; early morning.
- **Activity, "Sock Pairs":** tip a basket of clean socks onto the floor; the child finds two that are exactly the
  same and lays them together; you name the colour as each pair is found. *Materials:* clean socks, a basket. *In a
  class:* each child gets one sock and walks round to find the child holding its twin; together they say its colour.
- **Activity goal:** Find and match pairs that are exactly the same.
- **Film can show:** four stalls only, the colours waking up as the morning light rises; the sparrow's hops; the wool
  drifting down.

### A03 · Thank You, Mama ★ being good to one's mother
- **Kind:** value
- **Teaching goal:** Say thank you to Mama, and give back by doing a small thing for her.
- **Why this age:** noticing when someone needs help and responding kindly is a goal by 2 [S, ages §J1.6; CDC by 2];
  helping with small tasks is a goal we derive from Development Matters and CDC simple chores [D, ages §J1.29]. Being
  good to one's mother has no specific trusted source as a goal [D, ages §M].
- **Plot:** Mama comes to fetch Lina from kindergarten. Mama finds Lina's hat. "Thank you, Mama." Mama ties Lina's
  shoe. "Thank you, Mama." Mama gives her water from the bottle. "Thank you, Mama." On the way home Mama carries
  Lina's scooter, her own work bag and Lina's big painting. Her arms are full. Clink! Mama drops her keys. Lina picks
  them up. "Here you are, Mama!" At the door, Lina holds the painting. "Here you are, Mama!" Inside, Mama sits down.
  Lina brings her slippers. "Here you are, Mama!" Mama opens her arms wide. "Thank you, Lina." One big hug.
- **Characters:** Lina (a girl of about three); her mother.
- **Setting and season:** the walk home from kindergarten to a city flat; summer; afternoon.
- **Activity, "Here You Are, Thank You":** the child carries a cushion or an empty cup to someone in the family (or to
  you), holds it out with both hands and says "here you are"; the other says "thank you"; swap roles and carry
  something else. *Materials:* a cushion, a plastic cup. *In a class:* children sit in a circle and pass a cushion
  round, each giving with "here you are" and taking with "thank you".
- **Activity goal:** Use the giving words "here you are" and "thank you" in play.
- **Film can show:** the two refrains as two little tunes; Mama's arms filling up one thing at a time; the keys falling
  in slow motion and the small hand reaching for them.

### A04 · Slow, Slow, Together ★ respect for the elderly
- **Kind:** value
- **Teaching goal:** Slow down and hold hands to walk at an older person's pace.
- **Why this age:** waiting and holding back an impulse ("effortful control") is a goal at 2 to 3 [S, ages §J1.3];
  applying it to a grandparent is our choice, since no trusted source names respect for elders as a goal [D, ages §M].
- **Plot:** Grandpa promised Sami the red flowers at the top of the hill. Sami runs. Run, run, run! He looks back.
  Grandpa is far below, leaning on his stick. "Come, Grandpa!" Grandpa waves and walks: step... step... Sami runs back
  down and takes Grandpa's hand. Slow, slow, together. Now Sami sees what he missed: a butterfly, a ladybird, a bee in a
  flower. At the top the whole hill is red with anemones. They sit on a warm rock, and the ladybird walks over both
  their hands.
- **Characters:** Sami (a boy of about three); his grandfather, who walks with a stick; a ladybird.
- **Setting and season:** green hills; spring; morning.
- **Activity, "The Looking Frame":** the adult tears or cuts the middle out of a paper plate to make a frame. Walk
  slowly outside, hand in hand; stop, hold up the frame and name what is inside it: an ant, a flower, a stone, a cloud.
  Look only: no picking, no touching insects. Hands washed after. *Materials:* a paper plate. *In a class:* pairs
  share one frame and walk at the teacher's pace; the teacher calls "stop and look".
- **Activity goal:** Look closely and name small things in nature (new words).
- **Film can show:** two speeds side by side, the boy's dash and the grandfather's steps, then the matched pace; small
  creatures that appear only when the camera slows down with them.

### A05 · Little Camel Shakes the Sand
- **Kind:** skill (body)
- **Teaching goal:** Name and point to six body parts: head, ears, nose, back, knees and feet.
- **Why this age:** pointing to body parts is a milestone by 2 and naming them a goal [S, ages §J1.15; CDC by 2].
- **Plot:** A warm wind blows over the desert, and sand gets everywhere. Little Camel has sand on his head. Shake,
  shake! Sand in his ears. Flap, flap! Sand on his nose. Achoo! Sand on his back, on his knobbly knees, on his big flat
  feet. Shake, flap, achoo, wiggle, stomp! A small lizard on a rock laughs and laughs. Mother Camel kneels beside him
  and blows softly: whoo. The last sand flies away. Little Camel tucks his head under her neck, and they rest in the
  shade of a rock.
- **Characters:** Little Camel (a calf); Mother Camel; a lizard.
- **Setting and season:** the desert; autumn; midday.
- **Activity, "Scarf on Your Knee":** give one short instruction at a time with a short scarf ("Put the scarf on your
  knee", "Put the scarf on your head"); the child does it, then gives you one. *Materials:* a short scarf or a small
  towel. *In a class:* each child has a scarf; the teacher calls, the group follows, then one child calls.
- **Activity goal:** Follow a spoken instruction with two key words.
- **Film can show:** sand blowing and being shaken from each body part in turn, a slow-motion sneeze, the wind's
  sound: comedy that lives in movement.

### A06 · Little Hippo's Big Yawn
- **Kind:** skill (hygiene)
- **Teaching goal:** Brush the top teeth and the bottom teeth as part of going to bed.
- **Why this age:** tooth-brushing and bedtime routine books are recommended from 15 to 18 months; routines are a
  goal at 2 to 3 [S, ages §J1.13; Reach Out and Read; AAP choosing books].
- **Plot:** Night is coming to the river. Papa Hippo opens his mouth very wide: "Brush time!" Little Hippo opens her
  mouth too, just a little. Papa's brush is as big as a broom. Little Hippo's brush is tiny. Brush, brush, top teeth.
  Brush, brush, bottom teeth. Little Hippo giggles: the brush tickles! "Wider!" says Papa. Little Hippo opens wider...
  and wider... and gives the biggest yawn on the whole river. Papa yawns too, even bigger. Swish, spit, splash. Two
  clean smiles in the moonlight, and one sleepy Little Hippo leans on Papa's big warm side.
- **Characters:** Little Hippo (a calf, she); Papa Hippo.
- **Setting and season:** a river; autumn; dusk into night.
- **Activity, "Brush to the Song":** brush your teeth side by side in front of a mirror while you sing one short song
  twice; stop when the song stops. *Materials:* toothbrushes and toothpaste as your dentist or nurse advises. *In a
  class:* no shared brushes; the children "brush" with a clean finger to the song, and real brushing stays at home.
- **Activity goal:** Keep going at a task until a song ends (a song as a timer).
- **Film can show:** the river turning from dusk to moonlight, the broom-sized brush beside the tiny one, the giant
  comic yawns.

### A07 · Boom, Says the Sky
- **Kind:** value (feelings)
- **Teaching goal:** Let a cuddle and a favourite soft toy calm a scared feeling.
- **Why this age:** calming down with a comforting adult and finding ways to soothe oneself is a goal at 2 to 3; stories
  let children "safely explore emotions beyond their normal range" [S, ages §J1.2 and §D1].
- **Plot:** Yara is sleeping at Grandma's house in the village. Rain taps on the window. Then: BOOM! The sky shouts.
  Yara sits up, eyes wide. Grandma comes with a little lamp. "Big noise," she says, and holds Yara close. Flash...
  BOOM! Yara squeezes her toy lamb. Grandma breathes slowly: in... out... Yara breathes too: in... out... The next boom
  is smaller. Then only rain. Tap, tap, tap. Grandma taps her fingers on the blanket with the rain: tap, tap, tap. Yara
  taps too, softer and softer, until her eyes close.
- **Characters:** Yara (a girl of about three); her grandmother; Yara's toy lamb.
- **Setting and season:** Grandma's house in a village; winter; a stormy night.
- **Activity, "Blow the Paper Ball":** crumple a whole sheet of newspaper into a big ball; lie on the floor facing each
  other and blow it slowly across: one long breath out, "whoooo". *Materials:* one sheet of newspaper. *In a class:*
  pairs lie facing each other on a mat, one ball per pair; the teacher counts a slow "whoooo" for everyone.
- **Activity goal:** Practise long, slow breaths out through play.
- **Film can show:** the storm's flashes and rain rhythm, gentle and never startling (each boom is softened and comes
  after a warning flash); the breathing shown as the lamb rising and falling; fingers tapping with the rain.

### A08 · After the Nap
- **Kind:** value (feelings)
- **Teaching goal:** Cope with goodbye by knowing that the parent comes back after a known moment of the day.
- **Why this age:** by 3 most children calm within ten minutes after a parent leaves them at childcare; managing
  separation is a goal [S, ages §J1.9; CDC by 3; Development Matters].
- **Plot:** At the kindergarten gate Papa kneels down. "After the nap, I come back." Elias holds his sleeve. Then Papa
  is gone, and Elias cries a little. The teacher shows him the goldfish. After the fish, a song. After the song, a
  snack. After the snack, the sandpit. Each time, Elias looks at the door. After the sandpit: the nap. Elias closes his
  eyes on his little mat. When he opens them, there is a sleeve at the door, a sleeve he knows. "After the nap!"
  shouts Elias, and runs.
- **Characters:** Elias (a boy of about two and a half); his father; the kindergarten teacher; the class goldfish.
- **Setting and season:** a kindergarten; autumn; morning to afternoon.
- **Activity, "My Day in Three Pictures":** draw three simple pictures together (breakfast, playing, going home); mix
  them up; the child lays them in order on the floor: first, then, last. *Materials:* three pieces of paper, crayons.
  *In a class:* the teacher draws the class's own day in three big pictures; children take turns to lay them in order.
- **Activity goal:** Put three moments of the day in order.
- **Film can show:** time passing in a way a page cannot: the sunlight moving across the kindergarten floor between
  each "after".

### A09 · Hot, Hot, Hands Back!
- **Kind:** skill (safety)
- **Teaching goal:** Pull your hands back and wait when a grown-up says "Hot!".
- **Why this age:** by 3 children avoid touching hot objects when warned; accepting the warning is a goal [S, ages
  §J1.11; CDC by 3].
- **Plot:** Before the sun is up, Dina helps her uncle in the village bakery. Dina pats the dough: pat, pat. Dina
  sprinkles flour: puff! Then Uncle opens the big oven, and warm air rolls out. Dina reaches toward the shining
  loaves. "Hot!" says Uncle. Dina's hands fly back. Hands back, hands on the table. Uncle lays the round loaves on the
  shelf to cool. Dina waits. She blows: whoo, whoo. At last Uncle touches one. "Warm now." Dina holds a warm piece in
  both hands as the first neighbours come in from the cold.
- **Characters:** Dina (a girl of about three); her uncle, the baker; the first neighbours of the morning (background).
- **Setting and season:** a village bakery; winter; before dawn.
- **Activity, "Little Loaves":** tear a slice of soft bread into pieces in a bowl, add a spoon of water, and squeeze,
  roll and pat it into small round "loaves" on a plate. It is real, baked bread, so a small taste is fine, sitting
  down. Never raw flour: the CDC advises that children should not play with or eat raw-flour dough, including craft
  dough [S, CDC "Raw Flour and Dough"]. *Materials:* two slices of soft bread (gluten-free bread if there is a wheat
  allergy), a bowl, a plate, a spoon of water. Hands washed before and after. *In a class:* one slice per child, one
  adult per four children.
- **Activity goal:** Strengthen the hands by squeezing, rolling and patting.
- **Film can show:** heat made visible (shimmering air, steam, the oven's glow) and the dawn light growing over the
  village roofs.

### A10 · Soft Hand, Slow Hand
- **Kind:** value (nature)
- **Teaching goal:** Touch an animal gently, and let it go when it walks away.
- **Why this age:** being gentle with living things is a goal we derive from Development Matters ("living things") and
  the Danish nature theme [D, ages §J1.31].
- **Plot:** A grey cat called Cloud lives in the courtyard. Every afternoon she walks along the wall past the step
  where Ella sits with her big brother. Today Cloud jumps down and comes close. Ella wants to squeeze her tight! Her
  brother holds out one finger, low and still. Cloud sniffs it. "Soft hand, slow hand," he whispers. Ella holds out
  one finger too. Sniff, sniff. Tickly whiskers! Ella strokes Cloud's back, the way the fur goes. Soft hand, slow hand.
  Purr. Then Cloud stands up and walks away to the sunny corner. "Bye, Cloud," says Ella, and lets her go. They wash
  their hands at the tap, and Cloud washes her face in the sun.
- **Characters:** Ella (a girl of about two and a half); her big brother (about nine); Cloud, the courtyard cat.
- **Setting and season:** the courtyard of a block of flats; spring; afternoon.
- **Activity, "The Feel Bag":** put four big things in a cloth bag (a woolly hat, a large pinecone, a smooth stone, a
  kitchen sponge); the child reaches in, feels one, and you find the word together: soft, rough, smooth, squishy.
  *Materials:* a cloth bag and the four objects, all bigger than the tube test. *In a class:* the bag goes round the
  circle; each child feels, says a word, and pulls the object out to check.
- **Activity goal:** Name how things feel: soft, rough, smooth.
- **Film can show:** the cat's real behaviour (the sniff, the tail held up, a purr shown as a gentle shiver, the calm
  walk away) and the direction of a gentle stroke.

### A11 · Round and Round
- **Kind:** skill (shapes)
- **Teaching goal:** Spot circles in everyday things.
- **Why this age:** by 3 a child draws a circle when shown; the circle is the first shape [S, ages §J1.27; CDC by 3].
- **Plot:** Daniel and his uncle wait at the big station for the afternoon train to the sea. Daniel finds a round
  thing: the clock on the wall! Another: the light that turns green! Round, round, the buttons on Uncle's shirt.
  "Round!" says Daniel, and draws a circle in the air. Then: whoo-whooo! The train comes in, and under it the wheels
  turn round and round. Daniel and Uncle climb aboard. Through the round window the city slides away, and at the end of
  the line the sun, the biggest round of all, is going down into the sea.
- **Characters:** Daniel (a boy of about three); his uncle; the train driver, who waves.
- **Setting and season:** a city train station and a train to the sea; summer; afternoon to sunset.
- **Note for writers and the Studio:** the sea lies to the west in our picture world, so the sun sets into it; it
  never rises over it.
- **Activity, "Water Circles":** on a warm, not hot, pavement or wall (touch it first with the back of your hand),
  dip a brush in a cup of water; you paint a big circle, the child copies it; watch the circles dry and paint again.
  *Materials:* a paintbrush or sponge, a cup of water. *In a class:* each child has a brush and a patch of wall; the
  teacher paints one circle at a time to copy.
- **Activity goal:** Copy a circle drawn by an adult (early drawing).
- **Film can show:** circles in motion (turning wheels, the clock's sweeping hand on a face of twelve marks, a circle
  drawn in the air that leaves a trail of light).

### A12 · Teddy Needs a Wee Too
- **Kind:** skill (body)
- **Teaching goal:** Notice the body's "I need a wee" feeling and go to the potty in time.
- **Why this age:** toilet learning, first with help and then alone, is a goal at 2 to 3 [S, ages §J1.5; Development
  Matters].
- **Plot:** Nadia is building with blocks, and Teddy is helping. Suddenly Nadia stops. A little feeling, low in her
  tummy. A wee feeling! "Teddy needs a wee too!" Nadia grabs Teddy and runs: past the blocks, past the sofa, into the
  bathroom. Teddy sits on the edge of the bath. Nadia sits on the potty. Wait... wait... tinkle! "I did it!" Wipe,
  pants up, wash hands with Mama: splash, bubbles. Mama claps. Nadia makes Teddy clap. Back to the blocks, Nadia
  builds the tallest tower of the whole morning.
- **Characters:** Nadia (a girl of about two and a half); her mother; Teddy.
- **Setting and season:** home; spring; morning.
- **Activity, "Dress Teddy, Undress Teddy":** pull a pair of loose, elastic-waist trousers down and up on a big teddy,
  then on the child: down to the knees, up to the tummy, by themselves as far as they can. *Materials:* a big soft toy,
  loose trousers with an elastic waist. *In a class:* practise only on big soft toys or dolls, never on the children.
- **Activity goal:** Practise pulling clothes down and up without help.
- **Film can show:** the inside feeling made visible (a small glowing ripple in the tummy) and the comic dash through
  the house.

### A13 · Where Is Little Hedgehog?
- **Kind:** skill (language)
- **Teaching goal:** Understand the position words in, on, under and behind.
- **Why this age:** words for position (in, on, under) are a goal at 2 to 3 [S, ages §J1.21; Development Matters].
- **Also fits:** group B, as a guessing game for younger four-year-olds.
- **Fact checked:** hedgehogs in Israel are active at night and live in gardens and parks [S: the southern
  white-breasted hedgehog, Wikipedia species page; a three-year field study in a Tel Aviv suburb, *Mammalia* 1985].
- **Plot:** The hot day is over, and the garden is cool and full of stars. Little Hedgehog wants to play
  hide-and-seek. Mama Hedgehog closes her eyes. Where is Little Hedgehog? Under the bench? No, that's a beetle. Behind
  the watering can? No, that's a snail. On the wall? No, that's an owl, blinking. Mama Hedgehog sniffs this way and
  that. Sniff, sniff... a tiny giggle... In the old boot! Little Hedgehog is in the boot, curled up tight, round and
  prickly. "Found you!" Mama Hedgehog curls up beside the boot, and the owl hoots goodnight.
- **Characters:** Little Hedgehog (he); Mama Hedgehog; a beetle, a snail and an owl in the background.
- **Setting and season:** a garden behind a house; summer; night.
- **Activity, "The Box Tunnel":** open both ends of a big cardboard box (take out any staples and tape the edges); the
  child crawls through it, sits in it, walks around it and climbs over a cushion while you say the word for each.
  *Materials:* a large cardboard box, a cushion. *In a class:* two or three boxes in a line as a little course; one
  child at a time; the group calls the word.
- **Activity goal:** Move the whole body through, around and over things.
- **Film can show:** the garden at night alive (turning stars, blinking eyes, a sniffing nose) and a giggle whose
  direction we hear before we see.

### A14 · Everything Has a Home
- **Kind:** value (helping)
- **Teaching goal:** Help tidy up by putting each thing back in its place.
- **Why this age:** tidying up with a parent is a goal we derive from Development Matters ("we're tidying up") and
  CDC's simple chores [D, ages §J1.29].
- **Plot:** Rayan's family has a picnic in the pine woods. Then it is time to go home. But everything is everywhere!
  "The cups go home in the basket," says Papa. Rayan puts the cups in. "The ball goes home in the bag." In! "The
  blanket goes home..." Rayan's big sister rolls it up, and Rayan rolls himself up inside it too, with only his face
  peeping out. Everyone laughs. Last, the orange peels go home in the bin by the path. The ground is clean, a jay sings
  from a branch, and the family walks home, everything in its home.
- **Characters:** Rayan (a boy of about three); his father; his big sister; a jay.
- **Setting and season:** pine woods; spring; daytime.
- **Activity, "Big Box, Little Box":** set out one big box and one small box; gather safe things from around the home
  (a shoe, a sock, a pot, a spoon); the child decides which box each goes in. *Materials:* two boxes of clearly
  different sizes, household objects that pass the tube test. *In a class:* each child fetches one thing from the
  room and puts it in the big or the little box; the group says "big!" or "little!".
- **Activity goal:** Compare and sort things by size: big and small.
- **Film can show:** each object "walking home" into its place in a satisfying rhythm; sunlight through the pines.

### A15 · My Turn, Your Turn ★ respect for classmates
- **Kind:** value (with others)
- **Teaching goal:** Take turns with a toy: my turn, then your turn.
- **Why this age:** joining other children's play is a goal by 3 [S, ages §J1.8]; at this age sharing works as
  turn-taking, since real fairness comes later [D, ages §J1.30 and §F8]. Respect for classmates has no specific
  trusted source as a goal [D, ages §M].
- **Plot:** Maya and Amir go to the same kindergarten, and today they meet on the beach. There is one red bucket and
  two children. Maya has the bucket. Amir wants the bucket. Pull! Pull! Sand everywhere, and both of them cry. A little
  crab peeps out of its hole. Maya's mother smooths the sand. "Maya scoops one. Amir scoops one." Maya scoops. Amir
  scoops. Maya, Amir, Maya, Amir. The bucket fills up. Flip it over... a sandcastle! The crab climbs to the very top.
  Maya and Amir clap. "Again! My turn, your turn!"
- **Characters:** Maya and Amir (two children of about three from the same kindergarten class); Maya's mother; a crab.
- **Setting and season:** a sandy beach; autumn; morning.
- **Activity, "Carry It Together":** lay a teddy on a towel; the child and a partner each hold two corners and carry it
  across the room without dropping it. *Materials:* a towel, a soft toy. *In a class:* pairs carry their towel to a
  "home" mat and back; then the pair swaps who leads.
- **Activity goal:** Move in step with a partner to carry something together.
- **Film can show:** the rhythm of turns as music (scoop, scoop, scoop), the crab's climb and the waves washing in as
  flat layered bands.

## Group B: ages 4 to 5

Balance: 8 value stories, 7 skill stories. Seasons: summer 4, winter 4, autumn 3, spring 4. Night or dusk: B01, B10,
B11.

### B01 · Ten Goats Home ★ numbers
- **Kind:** skill (numbers)
- **Teaching goal:** Count to ten and know that the last number counted tells how many there are.
- **Why this age:** counting one item per number, beyond five toward ten, and knowing that the last number counted is
  the total are goals at 4 to 5 [S, ages §J2.26; Development Matters].
- **Plot:** Every autumn evening Rafi helps his aunt bring the goats down from the hills. At the gate he counts them

- **Plot:** Tamir spends the morning in Grandma's garden, digging with a spoon, planting onions, scratching the donkey

- **Characters:** Mira (a girl of eight); Adrian (a boy of eight); the jump-rope players; the first-graders.
- **Setting and season:** a school playground; spring; break time.
- **Activity, "Clap the Rope Rhyme":** learn a short skipping or clapping chant (one the children already know, or a
  new one they make up); the group keeps the beat with claps, then one pair turns a long rope slowly to the beat while
  others jump in one at a time. *Materials:* a long rope, kept on the ground when not turned, never around a neck. *In
  a class:* groups of six; the turners change every round so everyone turns once.
- **Activity goal:** Keep one shared rhythm as a group.
- **Film can show:** speed as feeling: the fast rope as a blur and the slow rope as a wide, visible arc; a lonely bench
  that ends up empty. Six jumpers at most in any picture.

### C03 · The Quiet Morning ★ being good to one's mother
- **Kind:** value
- **Teaching goal:** Help Mother without being asked, by noticing what needs doing.
- **Why this age:** household tasks and helping others are goals at 6 to 8 [S, ages §J3.4 and §J3.1; CDC 6 to 8].
- **Plot:** Mama is a doctor, and last night she worked at the hospital until morning. Now she is asleep, and the flat
  is very quiet. Emil wakes his little sister with a finger on his lips. They tiptoe. Emil looks round: what would Mama
  do now? He pours cereal and cold milk for two. He finds his sister's lost sock (in the bread basket!). He packs both
  school bags and writes a note in his best letters: "Mama, we ate, we went to school, we love you." At the door the
  neighbour from upstairs is waiting to walk them. At noon Mama finds the note, propped against her favourite cup.
- **Characters:** Emil (a boy of eight); his little sister (six); their mother, a doctor; the neighbour from upstairs.
- **Setting and season:** a city flat; winter; early morning.
- **Activity, "Cold Breakfast Tray":** the child plans and lays a cold breakfast tray for someone at home: bread, a
  spread put on with a butter knife, cucumber cut by the adult, a cup of water; then carries it with two hands. No
  stove, no kettle, no sharp knife. *Materials:* a tray, bread, a spread with no nuts or sesame (for example soft
  cheese or jam), cucumber, a butter knife. Check allergies first. *In a class:* pairs lay a tray for a school helper
  (the cleaner, the gardener, the guard) and carry it to them together.
- **Activity goal:** Prepare a simple cold breakfast safely, step by step.
- **Film can show:** silence made funny: every small noise suddenly loud, near misses with a spoon and a door, the
  winter light slowly filling the flat.

### C04 · Face to Face ★ respect for the elderly
- **Kind:** value
- **Teaching goal:** Be patient with an older person who hears less: come close, face them, speak clearly and say it
  another way.
- **Why this age:** patience and respecting others are CDC goals at 6 to 8 [S, ages §J3.2 and §J3.1]; respect for elders
  specifically has no trusted source [D, ages §J3.15 and §M].
- **Fact checked:** face the person, move closer, cut background noise, speak clearly and a little slower without
  shouting, and rephrase rather than repeat [S: St George's University Hospitals NHS "Communicating with people with a
  hearing loss"; ASHA "Tips for communicating with someone who has hearing loss"; Cleveland Clinic].
- **Plot:** Leo's grandfather hears less than he used to. When Leo calls from the kitchen, Grandpa answers the wrong
  question, and Leo rolls his eyes. "Never mind!" Grandpa goes quiet and looks at the hills. That summer evening, on the
  porch, Leo watches Grandpa's face while the cousins laugh at a joke he didn't catch. Leo moves his chair so they sit
  face to face. He speaks slowly and clearly, not loudly. When Grandpa misses a word, Leo says it another way, with his
  hands. Grandpa laughs at the joke, a big laugh. Then he tells his own, from his shepherd days, until the stars come
  out.
- **Characters:** Leo (a boy of eight); his grandfather; the cousins.
- **Setting and season:** a village house with a porch; summer; evening.
- **Activity, "Silent Messages":** in pairs, one person says a word with no sound, using only lips, face and hands; the
  other guesses; start with easy words (cat, water, sleep), then try short sentences. *Materials:* none. *In a class:*
  pairs, then one child "says" a word to the whole class.
- **Activity goal:** Communicate without sound, using face, lips and hands.
- **Film can show:** the world as Grandpa hears it: the sound track goes muffled from his side of the porch, then turns
  clear when Leo faces him.

### C05 · Oranges for Everyone
- **Kind:** value (fairness)
- **Teaching goal:** Share fairly, also with children who are not in my group.
- **Why this age:** most 7- and 8-year-olds prefer fair splits but still favour their own group [S, ages §F8 and
  §J3.13]; the step to fairness beyond one's group is our teaching choice [D].
- **Plot:** On a spring sports morning, two classes from two schools meet at the big sports ground. At snack time
  Julia's class opens a crate of oranges from their school garden: eleven big oranges. Alex's class looks for their
  snack box. It is still on the bus, and the bus has gone. Alex's class sits on the grass, very quiet. "They're ours,"
  someone mutters. "We picked them." Julia looks at the crate, then at the other class. She counts: twelve of us, ten
  of them. Twenty-two children, eleven oranges. "If every orange is cut in half..." Her teacher cuts, and Julia carries
  the halves round, one to each child in both classes, until everybody has the same. In the afternoon relay, the two
  classes run as one mixed team.
- **Characters:** Julia (a girl of eight); Alex (a boy of eight); the two classes; Julia's teacher.
- **Setting and season:** a city sports ground; spring; morning.
- **Activity, "Fair Shares":** share 12 pebbles equally between 2 people, then 3, then 4; what happens with 5?
  *Materials:* 12 pebbles or dry pasta pieces, small bowls. *In a class:* groups of five; each round, a different child
  does the sharing.
- **Activity goal:** Divide a set into equal groups (first steps to division).
- **Film can show:** two colours of T-shirt that are mixed by the end; orange halves passed hand to hand on a rhythm.
  Each class shown as four or five figures.

### C06 · One Note at a Time
- **Kind:** value (effort)
- **Teaching goal:** Reach a goal by practising a little every day.
- **Why this age:** setting small achievable goals and taking pride in them is a CDC goal at 6 to 8; praise effort, not
  "being smart" [S, ages §J3.7 and §J3.6].
- **Plot:** The school concert is thirty days away, and Lily wants to play a song on the old wooden flute that was her
  grandmother's. But she can play only one squeaky note. Thirty days. She draws thirty boxes on a paper and makes a plan:
  ten minutes of practice every day, even on rainy days, even when her little brother covers his ears. Some days the
  notes squeak; she ticks the box anyway. Day twelve: the first line of the song. Day twenty-five: the whole song,
  slowly. On concert night her hands shake, but her fingers know the way. In the front row, her grandmother closes her
  eyes and smiles.
- **Characters:** Lily (a girl of eight); her grandmother; her little brother; the music teacher.
- **Setting and season:** home and the school hall; winter; a month, ending on a concert night.
- **Activity, "Bottle Music":** fill four plastic bottles with different amounts of water; blow across the top of each;
  line them up from the lowest note to the highest and play a simple tune. *Materials:* four plastic bottles, water,
  a jug. *In a class:* each child blows only their own bottle; groups of four make one "band" of four notes.
- **Activity goal:** Discover how the amount of water changes a note's pitch.
- **Film can show:** progress you can hear: the squeak turning, day by day, into a melody, while the chart's boxes fill
  up with ticks (boxes, not numbers).

### C07 · The Crossing Leader
- **Kind:** skill (safety)
- **Teaching goal:** Cross the street safely: stop at the kerb, look both ways every time, even when you hear nothing,
  wait for the green light, walk.
- **Why this age:** traffic safety and walking to school are CDC goals at 6 to 8, and not running into the street is
  CDC advice from age 3 [S, ages §J3.17 and §B]. Electric bicycles and scooters are close to silent, so listening alone
  is not enough [D].
- **Plot:** In the first week of school, Noah walks with his big cousin, and his cousin has a game: at every crossing,
  Noah is the leader. Stop at the kerb. Look one way, the other way, then the first way again. Wait for the green
  light. Walk, don't run. At the little crossing by the bakery there is no light. Noah stops and listens: no engines.
  "Nothing coming!" He lifts his foot. "Look too," says his cousin. Noah looks. A bicycle with a small electric motor
  glides past, quick and silent, just where his foot would have been. The rider calls "Sorry!" Noah takes a breath at
  the kerb: stop, look, look, look, walk. At the school gate, his cousin grins. "Tomorrow you lead the whole way."
- **Characters:** Noah (a boy of six); his older cousin (ten); a rider on an electric bicycle.
- **Setting and season:** city streets; autumn; morning.
- **Note for writers:** the direction to look first differs by country; the text says "one way, the other way, then
  the first way again", and the pictures follow traffic as it drives in Israel.
- **Activity, "Map My Way":** walk a short route together (to the shop, the park or school); at home the child draws a
  map of it and marks each crossing; then walk it again, map in hand. *Materials:* paper, crayons. *In a class:* the
  class walks once round the school block with the teacher; groups of four draw it and mark the crossings.
- **Activity goal:** Draw and read a simple map.
- **Film can show:** the street in real time: the light changing, the silent bicycle that we see but cannot hear, the
  beep of the crossing. Two or three simple vehicles, never traffic jams.

### C08 · The Ball the Sea Took
- **Kind:** skill (safety)
- **Teaching goal:** Never chase anything into the water; swim only when a lifeguard is on duty and an adult is
  watching.
- **Why this age:** water safety is a CDC goal at 6 to 8 [S, ages §J3.18]; young children can drown in very little
  water, quickly and silently [S, safety §1.7, AAP].
- **Fact checked:** beach flag colours differ between countries, so the story uses none; Israeli safety advice is not
  to swim outside lifeguard hours or at beaches without a lifeguard [S: ynetnews "How to avoid drowning hazards when
  enjoying the sea"].
- **Plot:** It is early morning at the beach, before the lifeguard arrives, and the sea is wavy. Miriam and her father
  play ball on the sand. A gust lifts the ball and drops it into the water, and the waves begin to carry it away.
  Miriam runs to the edge and stops, toes in the foam. The lifeguard tower is empty. She watches the ball bob out,
  farther and farther. "Papa!" Papa looks, and takes her hand. "Let it go." Later the lifeguard arrives, laughing, with a
  ball under her arm: the sea has washed it up by her tower. Then Miriam and Papa swim together, close to the tower,
  where the lifeguard can see them.
- **Characters:** Miriam (a girl of seven); her father; the lifeguard.
- **Setting and season:** the sea; summer; early morning.
- **Activity, "Boat Test":** fold a small boat from aluminium foil and float it in a basin of shallow water; add pebbles
  one at a time; how many before it sinks? Change the shape and try again. An adult watches the water the whole time.
  *Materials:* foil, a basin with a few centimetres of water, pebbles. *In a class:* one basin per group of four, one
  adult per eight children.
- **Activity goal:** Test how shape and weight change what floats.
- **Film can show:** the sea's pull: the ball drifting farther with each wave (flat bands of water), measured against
  a still girl on the shore.

### C09 · Next Episode in 5, 4, 3...
- **Kind:** skill (screen habits)
- **Teaching goal:** Decide yourself when a screen show ends, instead of letting "next" decide.
- **Why this age:** the AAP warns about autoplay and endless scroll and advises clear family rules for screens at 6 to
  12 [S, ages §J3.23, §J3.20 and §C].
- **Plot:** It is the long summer holiday, and Max is watching a cartoon. When it ends, a little countdown appears:
  next episode in five, four, three... and the next one starts by itself. Then the next. Outside, the light turns
  orange. Max's legs have gone to sleep. He had planned to finish his cardboard rocket today. When the next countdown
  starts, Max does something new: he presses stop before it ends. The room is suddenly very quiet. He stretches, finds
  the rocket under the bed, and by evening it stands in the garden, painted silver, with the first star right above its
  tip.
- **Characters:** Max (a boy of seven); his mother; the cartoon characters on the screen.
- **Setting and season:** home and garden; summer; afternoon into evening.
- **Note for the Studio:** the countdown on the screen-inside-the-screen is drawn as a ring of shrinking dots; no
  digits in the picture.
- **Activity, "The Off-Screen Cube":** cover a small box with paper; on each of its six sides draw something you love
  to do away from screens; when a show ends, roll the cube and do what comes up. *Materials:* a small box, paper,
  crayons, tape. *In a class:* each child draws one side; groups of six build one cube and use it at break.
- **Activity goal:** Make a tool for choosing off-screen play.
- **Film can show:** a screen inside the screen: the countdown dots, and the light outside changing in fast motion
  while Max does not move. (StoryTime's own player has no autoplay, so the film practises what it preaches.)

### C10 · Long-Legs Flies South
- **Kind:** skill (nature)
- **Teaching goal:** Learn that many birds fly far between their summer and winter homes, and why.
- **Why this age:** nature and science are goals at 6 to 8 [D, ages §J3.29; Danish nature theme; the Israeli science
  programme exists but was not opened].
- **Fact checked (22 Nov 2026):** white storks soar on rising warm air (thermals) and so avoid the open sea, where
  thermals do not form; they funnel over the land bridge of Israel and fly only by day; autumn passage begins in early
  August; the valleys with fishponds in the north of Israel are major rest stops [S: ynetnews "A bird's Aliyah: white
  storks converge in southern Israel"; JNS "Thousands of white storks rest-stop in Galilee Panhandle"; Shamoun-Baranes
  et al. 2006 on autumn soaring routes; counts in press reports are U]. Young storks can make their first migration
  without their parents [S: PNAS 2024 study reported by EurekAlert and the University of Wyoming], so Long-Legs
  travels with the flock, not with his mother. They fly south because food is scarce in the northern winter [D,
  general knowledge].
- **Plot:** Long-Legs is a young stork, only three months old. The air in the north is turning cool, and storks are
  gathering into a great circling flock. "Why must we go?" he asks a big stork beside him. "Where we are going, there
  will be food when the cold comes here." Long-Legs cannot flap all day. So each morning the flock waits until the sun
  warms the ground, then rides the warm air rising from it, circling up and up, and glides south to the next warm
  column. They never cross the open sea, where no warm air rises; they follow the land. Each evening they come down to
  rest. Over a green valley full of fishponds, thousands of storks drop down to eat. On a hill, a girl and her
  grandfather watch them through binoculars and count. In the morning, when the ground is warm again, up goes
  Long-Legs, circling, south. In spring he will come back the same way.
- **Characters:** Long-Legs (a young stork); a big stork in the flock; a girl and her grandfather, bird-watching.
- **Setting and season:** the sky, and a valley with fishponds; late summer into autumn; days of flight.
- **Activity, "Sky Tally":** sit outside for 15 minutes in the shade, at a cool hour; each time a bird flies past, make
  a tally mark under small, medium or big; at the end, count the tallies in fives. *Materials:* paper, a pencil. *In a
  class:* groups of four each watch one part of the sky and add their tallies together at the end.
- **Activity goal:** Count with tally marks while observing nature.
- **Film can show:** what the book cannot: thermals made visible as slowly turning towers of birds (one stork
  silhouette repeated in a spiral, not thousands of separate puppets), the land changing below, day and rest by night.

### C11 · Meet Me at Four
- **Kind:** skill (time)
- **Teaching goal:** Read a clock to the hour and the half hour.
- **Why this age:** simple time-telling belongs to the 6 to 8 maths goals; school curricula were not opened, so the
  exact level is our judgement [D, ages §J3.28].
- **Plot:** Felix is always late, because he can't read the big clock in the square. His grandfather winds that clock
  every week, and one day he takes Felix up the narrow stairs inside the tower. Behind the giant face, where a few big
  wheels turn slowly, he shows him: the short hand tells the hour; when the long hand points straight up, it's o'clock;
  straight down, half past, and then the short hand sits halfway between two hours. Felix practises all week. Then his
  friend says, "Meet me at the fountain at four." At half past three Felix looks up: half an hour to go. When the clock
  strikes four, bong, bong, bong, bong, he is already waiting at the fountain.
- **Characters:** Felix (a boy of seven); his grandfather, the clock keeper; his friend.
- **Setting and season:** a city square with a clock tower; spring; afternoon.
- **Note for writers and the Studio:** the clock face carries twelve marks, no numerals, so one picture serves Arabic,
  Hebrew and Latin readers (master plan §7). In Danish "halv fire" means half past three; the text and the clock in the
  picture must agree. The clock strikes; it has no church-like bells.
- **Activity, "How Long Is a Minute?":** with a kitchen timer or a phone, the child closes their eyes and says "now"
  when they think one minute has passed; check how close it was. Then try counting slowly ("one-and-two-and...") and
  guess again. Last, see what fits into one minute: how many hops, how many names of fruit. *Materials:* a timer or a
  phone, paper and a pencil for the guesses. *In a class:* everyone closes their eyes and stands up when they think the
  minute is over; the teacher calls the real minute.
- **Activity goal:** Feel and estimate how long one minute lasts.
- **Film can show:** time itself moving: hands sweeping in fast motion, a few big wheels turning inside the tower, the
  clock striking four.

### C12 · Words and Pictures
- **Kind:** value (reading together)
- **Teaching goal:** Enjoy reading aloud to someone, taking turns with the words and the pictures.
- **Why this age:** CDC advises reading together and taking turns once the child reads [S, ages §J3.26].
- **Plot:** Tamara's little brother broke his leg falling off a swing, and now he lies in a hospital bed with a white
  cast and a grumpy face. Tamara has just learned to read. She brings her favourite book and reads it to him, slowly,
  her finger under each word. She gets stuck on a long word, and her brother, who knows the story by heart, shouts it
  out. So they make a deal: she reads the words, he reads the pictures, telling what everyone in them is doing. The boy
  in the next bed listens. Then his mother. Then the nurse. By evening three listeners sit around the bed, and nobody is
  grumpy.
- **Characters:** Tamara (a girl of six); her little brother (four); their mother; a nurse; the boy in the next bed.
- **Setting and season:** a children's ward in a city hospital; autumn; afternoon into evening.
- **Activity, "Fold-a-Book":** fold two sheets of paper in half, one inside the other, and tape along the fold: an
  eight-page book. The child writes and draws a story in it, then reads it aloud to someone, taking turns: the child
  reads the words, the listener tells the pictures. *Materials:* two sheets of paper, sticky tape, pencils, crayons.
  *In a class:* pairs swap books and read each other's aloud.
- **Activity goal:** Tell a story in pictures and words, and read it aloud to a listener.
- **Film can show:** the pictures in Tamara's book coming alive as her brother tells them, a story inside the story.

### C13 · After the Storm
- **Kind:** value (helping)
- **Teaching goal:** Notice people in need and help them as a team.
- **Why this age:** helping people in need and joining groups or volunteering are CDC goals at 6 to 8 [S, ages §J3.1
  and §J3.8].
- **Plot:** A winter wind blows all night. In the morning the village street is covered in branches, and a big branch
  lies across the gate of the little kindergarten at the end of the street. The little ones and their teacher wait
  outside; they cannot get in. Diana sees them from her window. She runs out and pushes the branch. It doesn't move. So
  she knocks on doors: the twins, the boy from the bakery, the girl from the corner house, who rolls out in her
  wheelchair. Diana's mother looks up and down the street: no wires down. "Go ahead." Five children, one branch. The
  girl in the wheelchair calls it: "One, two, three, lift!" It moves. Someone sweeps the path, someone stacks the small
  branches. The gate opens, and the little ones march in. The next morning, five drawings hang on the kindergarten
  gate, one for each helper.
- **Characters:** Diana (a girl of eight); the girl from the corner house, who uses a wheelchair; the twins; the boy
  from the bakery; Diana's mother; the kindergarten teacher and the little ones.
- **Setting and season:** a village street; winter; the morning after a storm.
- **Activity, "Thank-You Cards":** think of people who help everyone (the street cleaner, the bus driver, the school
  guard, the nurse); write or draw a short thank-you card for one of them and deliver it together. *Materials:*
  paper, pencils, crayons. *In a class:* each group chooses one helper of the school and delivers its cards together.
- **Activity goal:** Write a short message for a real reader.
- **Film can show:** the storm's power at night through sound and moving shadows, the still morning after, and the
  group lift in one rhythm.

### C14 · Two Ideas, One Raft
- **Kind:** value (with others)
- **Teaching goal:** Settle a disagreement with a friend by listening and making a plan you both accept.
- **Why this age:** solving small disagreements on their own is a CDC goal at 6 to 8 [S, ages §J3.5].
- **Plot:** By a shallow stream in the woods, Celine and Rani decide to build a raft for their toy animals, while
  Rani's father reads on the bank. Celine wants a tall sail. Rani wants a wide, flat floor. They argue, then each
  builds alone, crossly. Celine's tall raft tips over at once. Rani's flat raft floats but won't move. They sit on the
  bank, not talking. Then Rani says, "Tell me about your sail." "The wind pushes it." "My floor doesn't tip." Together
  they build a flat raft with a small sail, and the breeze carries a toy hedgehog all the way across the stream.
- **Characters:** Celine (a girl of seven); Rani (a boy of seven); Rani's father; dragonflies.
- **Setting and season:** woods with a shallow stream; summer; afternoon.
- **Activity, "The Paper Bridge":** lay a sheet of paper between two books as a bridge; add coins or pebbles until it
  sags; then fold the paper into a zigzag and test again; pairs must agree on one design before testing. *Materials:*
  paper, two books, pebbles or coins (counted back afterwards). *In a class:* pairs, one bridge each; the class
  compares the zigzag bridges at the end.
- **Activity goal:** Discover that folding paper makes it stronger.
- **Film can show:** physics in motion: the tall raft tipping, the flat raft stuck, the combined raft sailing on the
  breeze; the stream as flat moving bands.

### C15 · The Bottle That Looked Like Juice
- **Kind:** skill (safety)
- **Teaching goal:** Never taste or open an unknown bottle or pill; leave it and ask an adult.
- **Why this age:** keeping harmful products out of reach is a CDC goal at 6 to 8; most poisonings happen when
  caregivers are home but not paying attention [S, ages §J3.19; safety §1.3].
- **Plot:** Grandma is clearing out her old storeroom, and Rose and her little cousin are helping. It is hot and
  dusty. Behind a box, the little cousin finds a bottle of bright orange liquid. "Juice!" He reaches for the cap.
  "Stop! Leave it," says Rose, and catches his hand. It looks exactly like juice. But who keeps juice in a storeroom,
  next to the brushes, far from the fridge? Rose doesn't touch it. "Grandma! Come and look." Grandma reads the faded
  label: it is cleaner for the oven. Her face goes pale, and then she hugs them both very tight. Together they move
  every bottle to the top shelf, and Rose draws a big red hand for the door: "Ask first." Then they share real orange
  juice on the step.
- **Characters:** Rose (a girl of seven); her little cousin (four); her grandmother.
- **Setting and season:** Grandma's house in a village; spring; a clearing-out day.
- **Activity, "Warning-Sign Detectives":** the adult takes a few closed cleaning products down and holds them while the
  child finds the warning symbols on the labels (the child never holds or opens them); then the child draws a "Stop,
  ask a grown-up" sign for the cupboard. *Materials:* the household's own closed products (adult only), paper,
  crayons. *In a class:* the teacher shows photographs of warning symbols, never real products.
- **Activity goal:** Recognise the warning symbols on labels.
- **Film can show:** temptation and its pause: a shaft of light making the bottle glow like juice, the small hand
  stopped in close-up, the music stopping when Rose says "Stop".
