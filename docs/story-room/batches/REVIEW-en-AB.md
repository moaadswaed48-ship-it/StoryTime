**Result.** The English of 28 stories (A01 to A03, A05 to A15, B01, B03 to B15) was read aloud page by page, film line
by line and activity by activity, against `style-en.md`, the two age briefs, the outlines and the pilot review. About

errors (`storytool.py check` on all 28 ids, run after the last edit; the only error left in the run is A06 Arabic,

## 2. Decisions

1. **Mama, Papa, Mum, Dad.** The guide prefers Mum and Dad, but A01, A03, A06, A08 and A13 use Papa or Mama, as the
   outlines and the ledger do. Kept: they are what toddlers call their parents in Arabic- and Hebrew-speaking homes,
   English readers everywhere understand them, and each family in our library may use its own word. Inside each story
   the word is consistent.
2. **"Snow lady" (B07), "Whoo-whooo" (A11), "Wheee" (A01), "Night-night" (A06), "pants" and "wee" (A12)**: kept.
   "Pants" is British for underwear, which is what Nadia pulls up, and the book is British by our rule.
3. **Capitals for shouts** ("I’M ANGRY!" B03, "BOOM!" A07, "WAAAH!" B10): kept, one per story, as in the pilot.
4. **Exclamation marks** checked by script: no B spread has more than one.
5. **Semicolons removed** from every adult text (eight), as the pilot review did, because they read badly on a phone.

## 3. Things I did not change, for the coordinator

1. **B13: the door is green.** With green tiles, the last tile (green) sits next to Ilan's green door, and a child
   checking the pattern may read the door as one more green. I recommend changing the door to red in all four
   languages and in `story.json` (p1, p11, f13 and the art). I did not do it: it goes beyond the decision and the Arabic
   and Hebrew texts also name the green door.
2. **"Yellow-Green" in the English title** can also be read as one colour (a greenish yellow). The decision names the
   title; "The Yellow and Green Path" would avoid the doubt. Owner's call.
3. **A15 still has a crab** (p5, p10, f15 and the art). The reason the B14 crab became a seagull (the Hebrew and Arabic
   word also means cancer) applies here too; the Arabic writer used سلطعون, but Hebrew has only סרטן. Worth a decision.
4. **A13**: hedgehogs are awake at night; the story puts them to sleep at nightfall. Picture-book licence; I left it.
5. **B10 activity** tucks the doll's blanket "no higher than the shoulders"; the AAP statement now cited in the B2 notes
   advises no soft bedding for real babies. Harmless for a doll; the Danish and English texts say the same.
