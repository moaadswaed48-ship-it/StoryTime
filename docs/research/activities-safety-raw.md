# Activities and safety: raw research (StoryTime)

Status: work in progress, appended in sections. Every point carries its source URL. "[unconfirmed]" = could not verify directly.
Educational note: this is product research, not medical advice.

## 1. Safety rules per age group

### 1.1 Choking (food and objects)
- Keep high-risk foods away until about age 4 or older (depending on development): hot dogs/sausages, chunks of meat/cheese/string cheese, hard/gooey/sticky candy, marshmallows, gum, nuts and seeds, whole grapes, raw carrot sticks, raw apple chunks, popcorn, thick nut-butter chunks. Round firm foods only if chopped tiny. Source: https://www.healthychildren.org/English/health-issues/injuries-emergencies/Pages/Choking-Prevention.aspx
- Cut food for young children into pieces no larger than 1/2 inch; children sit to eat, never run/play/lie down with food in mouth. Same source.
- Most choking deaths are under age 5; two thirds of these are babies under 1. Same source.
- Household items to keep from young children: balloons, coins, marbles, toys with small parts, toys that fit entirely in the mouth when squeezed, small balls, pen/marker caps, button/lithium coin batteries, medicine syringes. Same source.
- Balloons: choking risk listed up to age 8 (latex pieces and uninflated/broken balloons). Same source.
- CDC list of foods to avoid (toddlers): whole corn kernels, uncut cherry/grape tomatoes, hard raw veg/fruit, uncut grapes/berries/cherries/melon balls, raisins, whole/chopped nuts and seeds, spoonfuls of nut butter, hot dogs, large cheese chunks, whole beans, cookies/granola bars, chips, pretzels, popcorn, round/hard candy, gummy candy, chewing gum, marshmallows. https://www.cdc.gov/nutrition/infantandtoddlernutrition/foods-and-drinks/choking-hazards.html
- Small-parts rule (US): toys/children's products intended for children under 3 may not contain parts that fit entirely inside the small-parts cylinder (models a child under 3's fully expanded throat), in any orientation, without compression (16 CFR part 1501). Cylinder dimensions are in 16 CFR 1501.4 (page itself gives none). https://www.cpsc.gov/Business--Manufacturing/Business-Education/Business-Guidance/Small-Parts-for-Toys-and-Childrens-Products
- Home test: if an item fits through a toilet-paper tube it is a choking hazard for babies/young children (practical stand-in for the cylinder; roughly 1.75 in / 4.4 cm across [approximate, unconfirmed]). Sources: https://myhealth.alberta.ca/Health/pages/conditions.aspx?hwId=ue5222 ; https://www.seattlechildrens.org/health-safety/injury-prevention/choking/ ; https://healthy.kaiserpermanente.org/health-wellness/health-encyclopedia/he.preventing-choking-in-small-children.ue5222
- Balloons are excluded from the small-parts ban but must carry a choking warning label (16 CFR 1500.19). CPSC page above. Seattle Children's and AboutKidsHealth recommend foil/Mylar over latex: https://www.aboutkidshealth.ca/preventing-choking-in-toddlers-and-preschoolers

### 1.2 Button batteries, magnets (and first aid)
- Button/lithium coin batteries: found in remotes, thermometers, toys, key fobs, light-up shoes, flameless candles, greeting cards. If swallowed: go to ER / emergency number immediately; X-ray. Honey (2 tsp, up to 6 doses 10 min apart) only if over 12 months and able to swallow liquids; do not delay care to find honey; no vomiting induction. Hotline 800-498-8666 (US). Tape battery compartments shut, store out of reach. https://www.healthychildren.org/English/safety-prevention/at-home/Pages/what-parents-need-to-know-about-button-and-lithium-coin-batteries.aspx
- Honey time window varies between sources (12 h vs 2 h) - verify with local poison centre: https://www.poison.org/battery/guideline ; https://drgreene.com/swallowed-button-batteries
- Strong (neodymium) magnets: more than one swallowed can attach through intestinal walls; go to ER. Overview: https://www.seattlechildrens.org/health-safety/injury-prevention/dangers-button-batteries-magnets/ ; https://www.safekids.org/fully-charged
- Product rule derived: no magnets and no battery-powered items in any activity for under 6; for 6-8 only large craft/fridge-type magnets, adult-held, never loose small magnets.

### 1.3 Poisons, plants
- Keep household products locked/out of reach; detergent packets never handled by children; "most poisonings occur when caregivers are home but not paying attention". Houseplants: call Poison Help 1-800-222-1222 for lists. https://www.healthychildren.org/English/safety-prevention/at-home/Pages/Childproofing-Your-Home.aspx
- Keep all plants, berries, seeds and bulbs out of reach of young children; a child may choke on any plant even if non-toxic. https://www.aboutkidshealth.ca/plant-safety

### 1.4 Supervision ratios (groups)
- NAEYC accreditation-standard ratios (secondary reporting; NAEYC's own PDF returned HTTP 403 so exact table unconfirmed): 2.5-3 yr groups up to 1:9 (1:6 if any child under 2.5); 4-5 yr max group 20 at 1:10; if most are 3-year-olds max 18 at 1:9. Sources: https://naeyc.org/sites/default/files/globally-shared/staff-to-child-ratio-class-size-2025.pdf (not readable here), https://www.unlv.edu/sites/default/files/page_files/1768/NAEYCTeacherChildRatio.pdf , https://childcare.gov/consumer-education/ratios-and-group-sizes
- Product rule (my recommendation, not a source claim): for kindergarten group use, require 1 adult per 6 children for ages 2-3 and per 8 for 3-5 for any activity with scissors, water or food; mark ratio on activity card.

### 1.5 Scissors and cutting
- Typical progression (OT sources, ages vary): about 2 = single snips with help; 3 = snips forward along a thick line; 4 = cuts long straight and curved lines, rough circle; 5 = squares and triangles. Use child-size, blunt-tip scissors and always supervise. Pre-skills: tearing paper, squeezing, tongs. Sources: https://pathways.org/when-will-child-use-scissors ; https://www.somersetft.nhs.uk/children-and-young-peoples-therapy-service/scissor-skills/ ; https://www.chrichmond.org/services/therapy-services/developmental-milestones/fine-motor-skills-2-to-5-years ; https://www.pbs.org/parents/thrive/how-to-help-young-children-learn-to-use-scissors
- Product rule: 2-3 = tearing only (adult does all cutting); 3-5 = blunt child scissors on thick paper/dough/playdough strips, adult in arm's reach; 6-8 = child scissors plus adult-checked.

### 1.6 Glue, paint, dough
- ACMI seals: children up to grade 6 should only use products with the AP (Approved Product) seal; never CL-seal products. Smell is not a safety signal. Conformance to ASTM D-4236 signals compliance with US art-material labelling. https://acmiart.org/acmi-seals ; https://www.acmiart.org/classroom-art-materials-safety
- AP seal addresses toxicity, not food allergens: check labels. [Allergen-specific guidance for wheat dough/nuts/latex not found in a trusted source in this pass: unverified.] Practical rule (own): homemade dough uses flour (wheat/gluten) so offer a gluten-free (rice/cornflour) version or salt-free alternative when allergy is known; never use nut shells or peanut-butter craft for groups; avoid latex gloves and latex balloons.
- Edible-play is not "safe to eat in bulk": salt dough is a high-salt hazard if eaten [own reasoning; unverified source].

### 1.7 Water play
- AAP: young children can drown in an inch or two of water, quickly and silently; adult within arm's reach ("touch supervision") for infants, toddlers, weak swimmers; never leave alone even briefly; assign a "water watcher" at group events; child should not bathe alone until about age 6. https://www.healthychildren.org/English/safety-prevention/at-play/Pages/Water-Safety-and-Young-Children.aspx ; https://www.aap.org/en/patient-care/early-childhood/early-childhood-health-and-development/safe-environments/drowning
- Beterem (Israel) via press: eyes directly on child in water even in kiddie pools; under 5 can drown in a few cm of water. https://www.jpost.com/israel/report-most-child-injuries-occur-in-the-home (secondary; Beterem's own site beterem.org not retrieved)
- Product rule: water play only in a shallow tray (a few cm), adult hands-on for ages 2-3, empty immediately after.

### 1.8 Strings, cords
- CPSC: cordless window coverings are the safe choice; strangulation can occur in under a minute; never tie cords together. https://www.cpsc.gov/Newsroom/News-Releases/2021/Go-Cordless-with-Window-Coverings-to-Avoid-Risk-of-Strangulation-to-Infants-and-Children ; https://www.cpsc.gov/gocordless
- Numeric limits for necklace/toy strings (e.g. 7 in / 18 cm) were NOT verified in this pass. Product rule (own): no string, yarn or ribbon longer than a hand span (about 15 cm) for under 3; no necklaces/loops around the neck for under 5 (use bracelets); no string tied to anything a child can pull; no free cords near window.

### 1.9 Heat and kitchen
- Keep children away while pouring/drinking hot liquids; pot handles to back; steam scalds; don't let children microwave unsupervised; test food temperature. https://www.chkd.org/patient-family-resources/health-library/safety-and-injury-prevention/keep-your-child-safe-from-burns/ (note: Safe Kids page itself not retrieved)
- Product rule: no heat in any activity for ages 2-5 other than adult-only pre-preparation; for 6-8 cold or lukewarm only; no knives except butter knife / soft food with adult.

### 1.10 Allergens and food in activities
- Common allergens in craft: nuts and peanut butter, wheat in dough, eggs in egg cartons (shell/ carton contamination), latex balloons/gloves, milk in some paints/glues [categories listed from general knowledge; no single trusted source retrieved]. Rule: ask parents/teacher about allergies first; label every activity "contains: ..." or "free of the common allergens".
- Foods used as materials: only those safe for the age (see 1.1); dry beans, rice, lentils and pasta used for sensory play are choking and ear/nose-insertion hazards under 3 (rule of own derivation from choking sources).

### 1.11 Outdoor and nature
- Plants: keep all plants, berries, seeds, bulbs out of reach of young children; teach "we never put plants in our mouths". https://www.aboutkidshealth.ca/plant-safety ; Beterem advice (via press): keep plants away from small children and teach children not to chew unfamiliar plants https://www.israelnationalnews.com/news/212519
- Israel: Arum palaestinum is among the most common paediatric plant poisonings reported to the Israel Poison Information Centre (2017 data, no severe cases); oleander (Nerium) is highly toxic (heart/nerves); castor bean seeds are very toxic (NSW factsheet). Sources: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00431-020-03648-x ; https://www.israelnationalnews.com/news/212519 ; https://www.poisonsinfo.nsw.gov.au/Factsheets/Poisonous-Plants-Factsheet.aspx
- Israel Poison Information Centre (Rambam): 80.3% of paediatric exposures in 2007 were children under 6. https://ima.org.il/FilesUploadPublic/IMAJ/0/40/20182.pdf (search summary; verify number)
- Denmark: yew (taks, red berries, seeds toxic), laburnum (guldregn, seeds resemble peas), colourful berries; Giftlinjen 82 12 12 12, most calls are children aged 1-4. https://www.sundhed.dk/borger/sygdomme-a-aa/akutte-sygdomme/foerstehjaelp/forgiftninger/giftige-planter/ ; https://www.bolius.dk/faa-styr-paa-dine-giftige-planter-11843 ; https://lex.dk/guldregn
- Other Europe/Israel common risks [from general knowledge, unverified here]: laurel/Prunus berries, daphne, lily-of-the-valley, foxglove, monkshood, privet berries, hemlock, mistletoe, mushrooms (never taste), jimsonweed (Datura), Ricinus.
- Product rule: collect only leaves, sticks, stones (larger than a toilet-paper-tube opening), pinecones, dry grass, shells; never berries, seeds, mushrooms, flowers unfamiliar; no tasting; no pulling plants from garden beds.
- Animals/insects/hygiene: keep children away from snakes, rodents, stray animals; outdoor play raises bug-bite risk; wash hands with soap and water after outdoor play and after touching animals/animal items; reptiles, amphibians, poultry, rodents unsuitable around under 5s. https://www.cdc.gov/earlycare/resources/outdoor-play-and-safety.html ; https://cdc.gov/clean-hands/prevention/about-hand-hygiene-in-schools-and-early-care-and-education-settings.html ; https://www.cdc.gov/healthy-pets/schools-daycares/index.html
- Sun: infants under 6 months in shade; clothing, brimmed hat; sunscreen SPF15+ from 6 months on uncovered skin. https://www.aap.org/en/news-room/news-releases/health--safety-tips/american-academy-of-pediatrics-top-safety-tips-for-preventing-heat-sun-related-illnesses-in-children-this-summer/ . Israel summer: product rule outdoor collecting before 10:00 or after 16:00 [own judgment].

### 1.12 Poison control numbers
- US Poison Help 1-800-222-1222; Battery hotline 800-498-8666; Denmark Giftlinjen 82 12 12 12; Israel poison centre (Rambam) phone [not confirmed here; check ministry site]; Israel emergency 101 (Magen David Adom) [from general knowledge, not source-checked].

## 2. Master list of safe, free or near-free materials (per age)
Derived from the safety sources above; each item's source is the rule it respects (sections 1.1, 1.5-1.11).
- 2-3 yrs (adult hands-on, all pieces larger than a toilet-paper tube opening, nothing mouthable): paper bags, large cardboard boxes, empty cardboard tubes (kitchen-roll size), paper plates, cloth scraps (washed, no loops), plastic tubs with lids (no small lids), wooden spoons, large plastic cups, washable non-toxic paint/finger paint (AP-seal), water in shallow tray, shaving-foam-free (use mashed soft fruit only if no allergy), large leaves, large pinecones, big smooth stones (palm-sized, larger than child's fist), sand/soil (washed hands after), torn newspaper, crayons (chunky), cardboard-box tunnels, pots and pans as drums, scarves (short), plain flour+water paint only if no wheat allergy.
- 3-5 yrs (adds): blunt child scissors (age 3-4+), washable glue stick/white glue (AP seal), cardboard offcuts, egg cartons (rinsed; check egg allergy), yoghurt pots, paper cups, leaves/sticks/pebbles (fist-size or bigger) collected under supervision, homemade playdough (flour/salt; allergy notes), washable markers (ventilated cap), cotton wool (not for under 3 mouths), pasta only dry large shapes for older 4-5 with adult, plasticine alternatives, sponges, spoons/funnels, chalk, coloured tape, string up to hand-span length (3-5 only for tying with adult).
- 6-8 yrs (adds): child scissors, paper folding, cardboard construction, split pins? (sharp: adult only), recycled bottles (caps removed or large), rubber bands (single, no sling play), tape, threads/yarn with adult (not necklaces), seeds for planting only in sealed pots with handwash (non-edible seeds not for tasting), magnifier [cheap; may require purchase], torch/mirror (no battery-opening), compass (free app not recommended: keep tool-free).
- Age-limit notes: all items must pass the toilet-paper-tube test for under 3 (see 1.1).

## 3. Red list (never include)
All ages: button/coin batteries and anything with them; loose small magnets and magnet sets (swallowing can cause serious injury: https://www.seattlechildrens.org/health-safety/injury-prevention/dangers-button-batteries-magnets/); heat, flame, candles, hot glue gun, matches; knives, sharp blades, needles, pins, staples, craft knives; unknown berries/seeds/mushrooms/wild plants tasted or crushed; oleander, castor, arum, laburnum, yew, datura; household chemicals (bleach, detergent pods; https://www.healthychildren.org/English/safety-prevention/at-home/Pages/Childproofing-Your-Home.aspx ), medicines, e-cigarette items; solvent glue, spray paint, permanent markers with strong solvent, non-AP/CL-seal art products (https://acmiart.org/acmi-seals); water deeper than a few cm without hands-on supervision (1.7); plastic bags over head; animals not suited to children (1.11).
Under 3: anything fitting the small-parts cylinder (1.1); round foods (whole grapes, nuts, popcorn, hard candy, whole beans/dry beans/lentils); balloons (latex) and balloon pieces; strings/cords longer than a hand span, loops, necklaces; scissors; glitter/beads/sequins/googly eyes/buttons/pom-poms (small); play with dry rice/pasta unattended; coins, marbles, bottle caps, pen caps; cotton balls pulled apart in mouth; uncooked flour-dough eaten in quantity.
Under 5: same food list (hot dogs, nuts, whole grapes, popcorn, raw carrot/apple chunks, sticky candy; AAP says until about 4 or older); latex balloons (AAP: risk until about 8); necklaces and neck-length loops; small beads; free scissors (without supervision); dyes/ink that stain mouth; seeds for tasting.
Under 8 (balloons): latex balloons for under 8 per AAP (https://www.healthychildren.org/English/health-issues/injuries-emergencies/Pages/Choking-Prevention.aspx); no stove, no knives, no hot glue; no water play without watcher; no tools like hammers/saws.

## 4. What good learning activities look like by age
Evidence found in this pass (thin; most numbers from secondary sources):
- Play is the central teaching practice; mix of self-directed play, guided play and direct instruction; small-group teacher-guided experiences from age 3; challenge must stay above ease. NAEYC DAP: https://naeyc.org/resources/position-statements/dap/principles ; https://www.naeyc.org/sites/default/files/globally-shared/downloads/PDFs/resources/pubs/dap-focus-preschool-intro.pdf (text via search summary; fetch not done).
- Attention span: sustained attention of 2-8 min in preschoolers (Ruff and Capozzoli 2003, cited in https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10643-025-01985-w ); blog rules of thumb: age 2 ~4-6 min, 3 ~6-8, 4 ~8-12, 5 ~10-14 (low-quality sources; e.g. https://www.melissaanddoug.com/blogs/living-playfully/activities-toys-to-help-development-of-preschoolers-attention-span). Recommended product plan (own, based on this): 2-3 yrs: core activity 5-10 min, with 1 step; 3-5: 10-20 min, 2-3 steps; 6-8: 20-30 min, 3-5 steps.
- Following directions: two-step about 2.5-3 yrs, three-step about 3-5 yrs, 3-5 steps by ages 4-5 (secondary sources; https://earlylearningprogressions.fpg.unc.edu/age/15/1017.html ; https://beaminghealth.com/article/when-should-my-child-be-able-to-follow-simple-directions). CDC milestone pages not retrieved: unconfirmed.
- Adult role: 2-3 = hands-on partner, models every step, narrates and names things; 3-5 = guide with questions ("what do you think will happen?"), child leads some choices; 6-8 = facilitator, child plans and explains. (Synthesis of NAEYC DAP themes; own wording.)
- Group vs one-to-one: 2-3 yrs one-to-one or tiny group (ratios 1:6 to 1:9 are for care not for hands-on activities); 3-5: small groups of 4-6 with an adult; 6-8: pairs and groups of 4. (Own recommendation, supported by NAEYC statement that small groups allow individual participation.)

## 5. Twenty activity types per age group (raw inspiration)
Note: drawn from standard early-childhood practice (sensory, fine-motor, early maths, language, nature, movement, music); not individually sourced; no curriculum document was retrieved in this pass. Format: what / teaches / materials.

### 2-3 years
1. Fill and empty containers with big leaves / water scoops - cause-effect, in/out - tray, cups.
2. Sock or cloth matching by colour - colour matching - washed socks.
3. Cardboard box tunnel crawl - spatial words (in, through) - big box.
4. Paper tearing collage - hand strength - newspaper, paper plate, glue stick.
5. Finger painting on a tray - colour, sensory - AP-seal paint, tray.
6. Pot drum band - rhythm, listening - pots, wooden spoons.
7. Sorting big vs small stones (palm-sized) - size comparison - large stones, two bowls.
8. Washing a doll or plastic animals (shallow tub) - care, vocabulary - tub, cloth, adult hands-on.
9. Animal sounds and movements with story - language, imitation - none.
10. Stacking cardboard boxes/cups - balance, counting 1-3 - paper cups.
11. Playdough squish and roll - fine motor - homemade dough (allergy check).
12. Scarf peek-a-boo and dance - object permanence, body awareness - scarf.
13. Bean-bag (cloth sock filled with soft rice sealed shut, adult sewn) toss into box - hand-eye - sealed socks.
14. Nature walk "find something big/soft/rough" - adjectives - pocket-free walk.
15. Sticky paper (contact paper) leaf mural - texture - contact paper, leaves.
16. Matching shapes cut from cardboard to holes in box lid - shapes - box.
17. Mirror faces (emotions) - emotion words - hand mirror (unbreakable) or reflective foil.
18. Water painting on a wall/pavement with a brush - mark making - water, brush.
19. Shoe/bag "home" matching (big/small shoes) - sizes - shoes.
20. Songs with actions (finger rhymes) tied to the story - memory, language - none.

### 3-5 years
1. Leaf-and-stick collage of the story character - design, colour names - leaves, glue, paper.
2. Paper-plate puppets retell story - sequencing, language - plates, sticks.
3. Counting stones into egg-carton cups - 1-10 counting - egg cartons (egg allergy check), stones.
4. Mix colours with water and food colouring? (food colouring stains; AP paint safer) - colour mixing - paint.
5. Sink or float with household objects (shallow tub) - prediction - tub, objects larger than tube opening.
6. Blunt scissors cut along thick lines to make a path - fine motor - paper, scissors.
7. Build a den/bridge from cardboard - engineering, cooperation - boxes.
8. Obstacle course following 3 instructions - sequencing, gross motor - cushions, rope on floor (not elevated).
9. Seed-free planting: plant a bean in a cup (non-edible, wash hands) - growth, patience - cup, soil (note: dry beans are choking hazard; supervise).
10. Shadow tracing with a sunny wall - light and shadow - chalk.
11. Texture rubbings of leaves/bark with crayon - observation - paper, crayon.
12. Pattern bracelets with paper strips and tape - AB patterns - paper, tape.
13. Story-sound hunt: find things that start with the story's first sound - phonological awareness - household items.
14. Playdough letters/shapes - letter recognition - dough.
15. Weather chart for the story's setting - vocabulary, observation - paper, crayon.
16. Mirror-art symmetry: fold paint blot - symmetry - paper, paint.
17. Sorting recyclables by material - classification, sustainability - bin items (clean).
18. Make and shake a simple instrument (sealed cardboard tube with rice, taped by adult) - sound - tube, tape.
19. "Feelings faces" cards - emotional literacy - paper plates.
20. Measuring with hand spans/footsteps - non-standard units - none.

### 6-8 years
1. Design a map of the story's world - spatial thinking - paper, pencil.
2. Build a bridge from paper that holds a weight - structure - paper, tape, coins (coins only for 6+; count & return).
3. Write and illustrate an alternative ending - writing - paper.
4. Nature journal with sketches and labels - observation, classification - notebook.
5. Plant and measure growth over 2 weeks - data, measuring - cup, soil, ruler.
6. Paper-fold animals/boats (origami) - sequencing, geometry - paper.
7. Make a shadow puppet theatre - light, storytelling - cardboard, torch (no battery opening) / sun.
8. Weather/rain gauge from a bottle - measurement - clean bottle, ruler.
9. Code-by-cards: give a friend a path using arrow cards - algorithmic thinking - paper.
10. Recycled-materials vehicle with wheels from bottle caps (large caps only, adult-checked) - design - cardboard, caps.
11. Interview a grandparent/neighbour about the story theme - language, culture - notebook.
12. Cooking-free "recipe" writing for a story character - procedural writing - paper.
13. Balance mobile from sticks and string (adult tied, hung out of reach) - balance - sticks, string.
14. Sound experiment: rubber band on box (single band, adult present) - pitch - box, band.
15. Sorting and graphing found objects - data - objects, paper.
16. Compare floating/sinking with predictions and chart - scientific method - tub, items.
17. Dramatise scene in groups - cooperation, speaking - none.
18. Newspaper-fold hat for character - geometry - newspaper.
19. Geometric pattern tiles from cardboard - symmetry - cardboard, ruler, scissors.
20. Seasons/time line of the story - sequencing, time - paper strips.

## 10-line summary
1. Choking: keep round/hard foods away until about age 4 (AAP), pieces at most 1/2 inch; latex balloons are a risk until about age 8.
2. Toy and material test: anything that fits through a toilet-paper tube is unsafe for under 3 (US small-parts rule, under-3 products).
3. No batteries and no loose magnets in any activity; swallowing is an emergency (battery hotline 800-498-8666 in US).
4. Water: touch supervision, a few cm depth only, empty after; children can drown in an inch or two (AAP; Beterem via press).
5. Cutting: tearing only for 2-3; blunt scissors from about 3-4 with an adult; this is a developmental range, not a rule.
6. Use AP-seal art materials; check allergens (wheat dough, nuts, eggs, latex); wash hands after outdoor play and animals.
7. Plants: collect only leaves, sticks, stones; never berries or seeds; Israel: arum, oleander, castor; Denmark: yew, laburnum.
8. Group ratios (NAEYC, secondary): about 1:9 at 2.5-3, 1:10 at 4-5; I recommend tighter ratios (1:6) for hands-on craft, scissors, water, or food.
9. Learning: play-based, short (about 5-10 min core for 2-3 yrs; 10-20 for 3-5; 20-30 for 6-8), one step for 2-3, two to three steps for 3-5, up to five for 6-8.
10. 60 activity ideas (20 per age band) are listed above; all use household or nature items.
