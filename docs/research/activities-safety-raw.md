# Activities and safety: raw research (StoryTime)

Status: work in progress, appended in sections. Every point carries its source URL. "[unconfirmed]" = could not verify directly.
Educational note: this is product research, not medical advice.

## 1. Safety rules per age group

### 1.1 Choking (food and objects)
- Keep high-risk foods away until about age 4 or older (depending on development): hot dogs/sausages, chunks of meat/cheese/string cheese, hard/gooey/sticky candy, marshmallows, gum, nuts and seeds, whole grapes, raw carrot sticks, raw apple chunks, popcorn, thick nut-butter chunks. Round firm foods only if chopped tiny. Source: https://www.healthychildren.org/English/health-issues/injuries-emergencies/Pages/Choking-Prevention.aspx
- Cut food for young children into pieces no larger than 1/2 inch; children sit to eat, never run/play/lie down with food in mouth. Same source.
- Most choking deaths are under age 5; two thirds of these are babies under 1. Same source.
- Household items to keep from young children: balloons, coins, marbles, toys with small parts, toys that fit entirely in the mouth when squeezed, small balls, pen/marker caps, button/lithium coin batteries, medicine syringes. Same source.
- Balloons: choking risk listed up to age 8 (latex pieces and uninflated/broken balloons). Same source.
- CDC list of foods to avoid (toddlers): whole corn kernels, uncut cherry/grape tomatoes, hard raw veg/fruit, uncut grapes/berries/cherries/melon balls, raisins, whole/chopped nuts and seeds, spoonfuls of nut butter, hot dogs, large cheese chunks, whole beans, cookies/granola bars, chips, pretzels, popcorn, round/hard candy, gummy candy, chewing gum, marshmallows. https://www.cdc.gov/nutrition/infantandtoddlernutrition/foods-and-drinks/choking-hazards.html
- Small-parts rule (US): toys/children's products intended for children under 3 may not contain parts that fit entirely inside the small-parts cylinder (models a child under 3's fully expanded throat), in any orientation, without compression (16 CFR part 1501). Cylinder dimensions are in 16 CFR 1501.4 (page itself gives none). https://www.cpsc.gov/Business--Manufacturing/Business-Education/Business-Guidance/Small-Parts-for-Toys-and-Childrens-Products
- Home test: if an item fits through a toilet-paper tube it is a choking hazard for babies/young children (practical stand-in for the cylinder; roughly 1.75 in / 4.4 cm across [approximate, unconfirmed]). Sources: https://myhealth.alberta.ca/Health/pages/conditions.aspx?hwId=ue5222 ; https://www.seattlechildrens.org/health-safety/injury-prevention/choking/ ; https://healthy.kaiserpermanente.org/health-wellness/health-encyclopedia/he.preventing-choking-in-small-children.ue5222
- Balloons are excluded from the small-parts ban but must carry a choking warning label (16 CFR 1500.19). CPSC page above. Seattle Children's and AboutKidsHealth recommend foil/Mylar over latex: https://www.aboutkidshealth.ca/preventing-choking-in-toddlers-and-preschoolers

### 1.2 Button batteries, magnets (and first aid)
- Button/lithium coin batteries: found in remotes, thermometers, toys, key fobs, light-up shoes, flameless candles, greeting cards. If swallowed: go to ER / emergency number immediately; X-ray. Honey (2 tsp, up to 6 doses 10 min apart) only if over 12 months and able to swallow liquids; do not delay care to find honey; no vomiting induction. Hotline 800-498-8666 (US). Tape battery compartments shut, store out of reach. https://www.healthychildren.org/English/safety-prevention/at-home/Pages/what-parents-need-to-know-about-button-and-lithium-coin-batteries.aspx
- Honey time window varies between sources (12 h vs 2 h) - verify with local poison centre: https://www.poison.org/battery/guideline ; https://drgreene.com/swallowed-button-batteries
- Strong (neodymium) magnets: more than one swallowed can attach through intestinal walls; go to ER. Overview: https://www.seattlechildrens.org/health-safety/injury-prevention/dangers-button-batteries-magnets/ ; https://www.safekids.org/fully-charged
- Product rule derived: no magnets and no battery-powered items in any activity for under 6; for 6-8 only large craft/fridge-type magnets, adult-held, never loose small magnets.

### 1.3 Poisons, plants
- Keep household products locked/out of reach; detergent packets never handled by children; "most poisonings occur when caregivers are home but not paying attention". Houseplants: call Poison Help 1-800-222-1222 for lists. https://www.healthychildren.org/English/safety-prevention/at-home/Pages/Childproofing-Your-Home.aspx
- Keep all plants, berries, seeds and bulbs out of reach of young children; a child may choke on any plant even if non-toxic. https://www.aboutkidshealth.ca/plant-safety

### 1.4 Supervision ratios (groups)
- NAEYC accreditation-standard ratios (secondary reporting; NAEYC's own PDF returned HTTP 403 so exact table unconfirmed): 2.5-3 yr groups up to 1:9 (1:6 if any child under 2.5); 4-5 yr max group 20 at 1:10; if most are 3-year-olds max 18 at 1:9. Sources: https://naeyc.org/sites/default/files/globally-shared/staff-to-child-ratio-class-size-2025.pdf (not readable here), https://www.unlv.edu/sites/default/files/page_files/1768/NAEYCTeacherChildRatio.pdf , https://childcare.gov/consumer-education/ratios-and-group-sizes
- Product rule (my recommendation, not a source claim): for kindergarten group use, require 1 adult per 6 children for ages 2-3 and per 8 for 3-5 for any activity with scissors, water or food; mark ratio on activity card.
