## 1. Consumer law for online (distance) sales

Main law: Consumer Protection Law, 1981 (חוק הגנת הצרכן, התשמ"א-1981). Text read via the Hebrew Wikisource copy (which reproduces the consolidated Nevo/Knesset text and shows amendments up to 2026, e.g. 2026-adjusted sanction amounts) - https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/חוק_הגנת_הצרכן **[SECONDARY copy of the statute]**. A lawyer must check against Nevo (https://www.nevo.co.il) or the Knesset database.

### 1.1 Who is a "distance sale" seller and what must be disclosed
- Section 14ג(א): in distance marketing the trader must disclose at least: (1) name, **ID number** and address of the trader (in Israel and abroad); (2) main features of the product/service; (3) price and possible payment terms; (4) time and manner of supply; (5) the period the offer is valid; (6) warranty details; (7) the consumer's right to cancel. **[SECONDARY copy of statute]**
- Section 14ג(ב): the trader must give the consumer a **written document** (the "transaction document", no later than delivery/supply) containing the 14ג(א) details, plus how to cancel, the manufacturer/country (for goods), warranty and any other terms. **The document must be in Hebrew or in the language in which the marketing was done.** For a four-language site this means the confirmation/terms should be provided in the language the buyer used. **[SECONDARY copy of statute]**
- Section 4(ב) (as summarised from the page): a trader must disclose to the consumer their name and ID number (on offers, contracts, receipts/tax invoices). **[SECONDARY, from a first-pass read of the Wikisource page; confirm the exact wording of section 4 on Nevo]**
- Consumer Authority enforcement reports state that the duty to disclose cancellation terms applies at the marketing stage as well, and that missing cancellation information or no cancellation link on the site was fined (annual reports 2018, 2020, 2024 on gov.il) **[SNIPPET]** - https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/reports/pfta_annual_reports/he/ (reports by year).

### 1.2 Price display, VAT
- Chapter on price display, sections 17א-17ז: "total price" (המחיר הכולל) means the price including all payments and **all taxes** applicable (section 17א). Price must be shown in Israeli currency, in a visible place, in clear legible digits (sections 17ב(ב), 17ג). Section 17ד: a trader may not publish or state, expressly or by implication, a price of a product or service offered to a consumer in a way that departs from this (text truncated in what I read). **[SECONDARY copy of statute]**
- Practical meaning for a website: the price shown to a consumer in Israel must include VAT. In the Agoda and Booking.com matters the courts and the Consumer Authority required VAT-inclusive prices for Israeli buyers (ice.co.il, bizportal, 2023 reports) **[SECONDARY]**. Whether non-Israeli buyers may be shown prices without VAT is not settled in what I read (Booking proposed IP-based display to the Authority) - ask the lawyer.
- **Current VAT rate: 18%**, in force since 1 January 2025 (raised from 17%). Sources: Bizportal 2026 VAT guide/calculator https://www.bizportal.co.il/additives/301/vat-calculator-2026-.html and https://www.bizportal.co.il/guides/news/article/20041450 **[SECONDARY]**; a 2026 English guide also gives 18% as the standard rate. I did **not** open an official Tax Authority page stating the 2026 rate (gov.il blocked). Check: https://www.gov.il/he/departments/israel_tax_authority.

### 1.3 Cancellation rights and deadlines (distance sale)
- Section 14ג(ג)(1) - **goods**: cancel from the transaction until 14 days after receiving the goods or the 14ג(ב) document, whichever is later.
- Section 14ג(ג)(2) - **services**: the consumer may cancel within **14 days** from the transaction date or from receipt of the 14ג(ב) document, whichever is later. For **ongoing services** this applies whether or not the service has started. For a **one-time service**, cancellation must be made at least **two non-rest days before the service is to begin**. **[SECONDARY copy of statute]**
- **I found no digital-content exception in the statute text I read.** The Consumer Protection Regulations (Cancellation of Transaction), 2010 (תקנות הגנת הצרכן (ביטול עסקה), התשע"א-2010) list some exceptions (e.g. certain goods); I could not open the regulation text, so whether any exception covers access to online digital content is **not verified**. Treat it as: the 14-day right applies to a paid access pass sold online. Ask the lawyer. Related page: https://www.kolzchut.org.il/he/ביטול_עסקת_מכר_מרחוק (blocked to me; found via search) **[SNIPPET]**.
- Extended right: section 14ג1(ג): a consumer who is a person with a disability, a senior (65+) or a new immigrant (within 5 years of the immigrant certificate) may cancel a distance sale within **4 months**, if the deal included a conversation between trader and consumer (phone/chat). Not relevant for a pure website purchase with no conversation, but relevant if support chat/phone is used in the sale. **[SECONDARY copy of statute]**

### 1.4 Refund timing and allowed fees
- Section 14ה(ב): when the consumer cancels under 14ג(ג) for reasons other than a trader fault, the trader must **refund within 14 days** of receiving the cancellation notice, cancel the charge, and give written confirmation. The trader may keep a **cancellation fee of up to the lower of 5% of the price or 100 NIS**. Summaries by Kol Zchut, Bizportal and the Chamber of Commerce say the same **[SECONDARY]**.
- Section 14ה(ב1): for an ongoing service already provided, the consumer pays the pro-rated value of the service received (my paraphrase of the page text; check exact wording). **[SECONDARY]**
- Section 14ה(א): if cancellation is because of a defect, non-conformity, late delivery or other breach by the trader: refund within 14 days, **no** cancellation fee.

### 1.5 Duty to allow cancellation online
- Section 14ט(א): the trader must allow cancellation by: phone or oral notice (unless the law demands writing), registered mail, email, fax (if the trader has one), **the internet** (where the deal can be made online), or another way set by the minister. **[SECONDARY copy of statute]**
- Section 14ט(ב): a trader selling online must place a **prominent, clearly labelled cancellation link on the site's home page**.
- Section 14ט(ד)-(ו): the trader must disclose in writing the cancellation methods and contact details no later than when the product/service is provided; the same information must appear on invoices/receipts/payment notices and on the home page next to the cancellation link, in emphasised, clear lettering.
- Enforcement example: Consumer Authority reports describe fines for missing a dedicated cancellation link on the home page (2020 annual report) **[SNIPPET]**.

### 1.6 Free trials and one-time passes with no auto-renewal
- I found **no specific statutory rule on "free trial" periods** in the text I read, and the search did not turn up one **[not verified]**. The general rules still apply: no misleading (section 2), full disclosure (14ג), price clarity (17), and consent to any payment.
- Section 13א (contracts for a fixed term) says a contract term that continues the relationship automatically after the end date is void, renewal needs the consumer's agreement, and the trader must notify the end date: in the invoices/receipts sent in the last three months, and by SMS 21 days before the end if a mobile number was given (13א(ב), (ג), (ה): burden of proof on the trader). **Because your passes do not renew automatically, the safe design is: the free trial must not roll into a paid pass without a fresh, explicit purchase.** **[SECONDARY copy of statute]**
- Whether 13א applies to a 1/3/12-month pass is a question for the lawyer (it concerns "עסקה לתקופה קצובה"); the end-date notice is also a good-practice expiry reminder.

### 1.7 Receipts and confirmation after purchase
- Consumer law: the transaction document under 14ג(ב) (see 1.1) must be provided no later than supply, and tax-law receipts/invoices are described in section 2 below. A sent confirmation email carrying: business name, ID number, address, price including VAT, period of the pass, cancellation method and contact details covers the consumer-law side. **[my synthesis from sources above; lawyer to confirm]**

### 1.8 Penalties
- Monetary sanctions section 22ג (amounts adjusted to 2026 on the Wikisource copy): 25,950 NIS (corporation) / 8,260 NIS (individual) for breaches listed in 22ג(א); 53,070 / 29,490 NIS for 22ג(ב); 75,620 / 42,030 NIS for unfair influence (22ג(ב1)); aggravating circumstances (e.g. offences aimed at minors) multiply by 1.5 (22ד). **[SECONDARY copy of statute; amounts are updated yearly]**

## 2. Tax and invoicing basics (not an accountant's advice)

Sources are mostly commercial guides plus gov.il service page titles seen in search results; I could not open gov.il pages. **[SECONDARY / SNIPPET]**

- **Osek patur** (exempt dealer, עוסק פטור): no VAT charged or reclaimed; turnover ceiling for **2026 is 122,833 NIS** per the gov.il service pages as summarised by the search tool (2025: 120,000 NIS). Pages: https://www.gov.il/he/service/request-open-exempt-dealer-via-internet and https://www.gov.il/he/service/request-transfer-to-micro-business-owner **[SNIPPET of official page; not opened]**. Status depends on turnover, not profit; some professions cannot be osek patur.
- **Osek murshe** (licensed dealer, עוסק מורשה): charges **18% VAT** on sales, issues tax invoices, files periodic VAT reports (monthly or bi-monthly) **[SECONDARY]** (Bizportal https://www.bizportal.co.il/guides/news/article/20039167).
- Documents: an osek patur issues **receipts** (not tax invoices); an osek murshe issues tax invoices and, when payment is received, tax-invoice/receipt or receipt **[SECONDARY; confirm with an accountant]**.
- **Israel Invoice (חשבונית ישראל) / allocation number**: for a tax invoice above a threshold the issuer must request an allocation number from the Tax Authority, otherwise the buyer cannot deduct input VAT or claim the expense. Thresholds: 25,000 NIS (May 2024), 20,000 (Jan 2025), 10,000 (1 Jan 2026), **5,000 NIS before VAT from 1 June 2026** (Greeninvoice, Capitax, gov.il request page title) **[SECONDARY]** - https://www.gov.il/he/service/request-assignment-number-for-tax-invoice, https://www.greeninvoice.co.il/israel-invoice/. Commentary says osek patur dealers are outside this regime **[SECONDARY]**. **Matters for kindergartens/teachers who buy as a business and want to deduct VAT**: a 12-month pass may exceed the 5,000 threshold only if priced very high, so probably not an issue, but the invoicing software must support it.
- Digital invoices: invoicing software that is approved by the Tax Authority is the practical route; I did **not** verify the formal rules on software approval/"approved invoicing programs" - ask the accountant.
- Online sales do not remove the duty to issue a receipt/invoice on every payment received **[SECONDARY; I did not find the exact section: Income Tax Regulations (Book-keeping) / VAT Law section 47 are the usual references - NOT verified]**.

## 3. Privacy

Primary law: Protection of Privacy Law, 1981 (חוק הגנת הפרטיות, התשמ"א-1981), as amended by **Amendment 13 (תיקון מס' 13, התשפ"ד-2024)**, published 14 Aug 2024 (Sefer HaChukim 3287) and in force **14 August 2025** (section 74(a)) - Source: dpoas.co.il guide https://dpoas.co.il/blog/amendment-13-complete-guide/ and Shibolet client update https://www.shibolet.com/עדכון-לקוחות-פעילות-הרשות-להגנת-הפרטי/ **[SECONDARY]**.

### 3.1 What the Authority itself says (read in full text)
- Privacy Protection Authority opinion on consent, 25 Feb 2026 **[PRIMARY, PDF read]** - https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/legalinfo/consent-2026/he/cpncent-2025.pdf :
  - Consent must be informed ("מדעת"), clear and understandable; section 1 of the Law ("no one shall violate another's privacy without consent"), section 11 (duty to inform).
  - The controller must list **all** types of data collected and **all** purposes; partial lists such as "etc." or "among other things" do not satisfy informed consent. Recipients can be described by type (e.g. "advertising companies") but not too generically.
  - Avoid blanket consent for the service; let users choose which data and purposes; if you use data beyond what is normal for that service, highlight it and get explicit, separate consent. The paper gives the example of parents who consent to children's location data for a child-protection app not expecting marketing use: a **separate** parental consent is needed.
  - Where a service is mainly aimed at minors or other special groups, the content and presentation of consent should be adapted to them.
- The Authority's guidance on minors: 2018-19 cross-sector inspection report on minors' data (https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/dynamiccollectorresultitem/ppa-report18/he/Minors_report.pdf) - transparency to minors must suit their age; and the Justice Ministry item "prohibition to collect personal information from minors without parental consent, also online" (https://www.justice.gov.il/Pubilcations/ArticlesOld/Pages/HaskamaHorit.aspx) **[SNIPPET, not opened]**. **I did not find a statutory minimum age for a child's own consent in Israeli privacy law** - the general approach is that a minor's consent needs a parent/guardian for data collection from minors; confirm with the lawyer. Your design (adult account holder, child = first name/nickname + age group) keeps the controller relationship with the adult.
- Guidance on children/sharenting (Nov 2025) and Daycare cameras guideline 1/26 (Feb 2026), age verification draft (June 2026) exist but concern other situations (https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/reports/sharenting_for_parents1/he/SharintingPost13Access.pdf).

### 3.2 Database registration and notification (after Amendment 13)
- Registration of databases was **abolished for most businesses**; a **30-day notification duty (section 8A(b))** applies to large databases containing specially sensitive information; the exact "large" threshold was **not confirmed** in the sources I read (secondary sources gave 100,000 data subjects; one older secondary source mentions 10,000 for data brokers). **[SECONDARY, conflicting - verify]** - dpoas.co.il guide; official page to check: https://www.gov.il/he/service/notice-obligation (blocked to me).
- A small story site with parents' emails and children's first names is very unlikely to be near these thresholds - but the lawyer should confirm "specially sensitive" does not apply (children's names + age group are not in the sensitive list in the Security Regulations schedule, see 3.4).

### 3.3 Privacy officer (ממונה הגנת פרטיות), section 17ב1
- Mandatory only for: (1) public bodies; (2) data brokers with data on more than 10,000 people; (3) bodies whose main activity is systematic monitoring of people at large scale; (4) bodies whose main activity is processing specially sensitive data at large scale. **A small business outside these four categories has no obligation** (Melnik law firm https://www.melnik.org.il/en/israel-privacy-officer-amendment-13/ **[SECONDARY]**; dpoas.co.il **[SECONDARY]**).

### 3.4 Data security regulations
- Protection of Privacy Regulations (Data Security), 5777-2017 - official English translation **[PRIMARY, read]**: https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/legalinfo/data_security_regulation/en/5777%E2%80%932017.pdf. In force one year after publication (regulation 22). Three levels: basic, medium, high. Medium: data-broker databases, public bodies, or databases with listed sensitive categories (intimate life, medical, genetic, political/religious views, criminal records, telecom data, biometric, financial situation, consumption habits that reveal those). A database with none of these, and not a data-broker or public body, is **basic level**. High: medium-type databases with 100,000+ people or 100+ authorised users.
- All databases must keep a **database definitions document** (regulation 2: description of collection and use, purposes, data types, **transfer outside Israel with country and recipient**, processors).
- **Breach notification** (regulation 11(d)): for a severe security incident (defined only for **high** and **medium** level databases) the controller must immediately notify the Registrar (now the Authority) and report the measures taken; the Authority may order notice to affected data subjects. Basic-level databases are not in the "severe incident" definition, but the general duty to secure data still applies. **[PRIMARY]**
- Amendment 13 era enforcement: Authority launched cross-sector inspections from Dec 2025 including **online retail companies and popular apps** (Shibolet update, 23 Dec 2025 **[SECONDARY]**). Reported sanctions of 12,000 NIS (a karting business, section 11 breach) up to 256,000 NIS (health fund, failure to report breach) (dpoas.co.il **[SECONDARY]**).

### 3.5 Notice, consent, rights, privacy policy
- Section 11 of the Law: whoever asks a person for personal data must tell them: whether they must provide it or it is voluntary, the purpose, and to whom and for what purpose it will be handed over (statement from the Authority's consent opinion refers to "duty to inform in section 11"; the full wording of section 11 after Amendment 13 I did **not** read) **[PRIMARY pointer, text not read]**.
- A privacy policy should therefore list: who you are and contact; each type of data (account email/name, payment reference, child's nickname and age group, usage data, cookies); each purpose; recipients by type (payment company, hosting, email sender); where data is stored (country); retention; how to ask for access/correction/deletion; whether providing data is mandatory; how to complain. **[my synthesis from the Authority's consent opinion + section 11; lawyer to finalise]**
- Rights: section 13 (access) and 14 (correction/deletion) of the Law; the content/deadlines after Amendment 13 were **not verified** by me in primary text.
- Statutory damages without proof of harm up to 10,000 NIS per claimant (section 29א) - per dpoas.co.il **[SECONDARY, unverified]**.
- Administrative fine ranges: base 150,000 NIS, per-person formulas of 2-8 NIS, overall cap of 5% of annual turnover - per dpoas.co.il and an IAPP summary **[SECONDARY, unverified]**. Fifth Schedule reductions exist.

### 3.6 Data stored abroad (e.g. EU hosting)
- Protection of Privacy Regulations (Transfer of Data to Databases Abroad), 2001: transfer allowed to a country with a level of protection no lower than Israel's; the regulator treats transfers to **EU member states** as adequate; a contract route exists (regulation 2(4)) **[SECONDARY]** - Baker McKenzie https://resourcehub.bakermckenzie.com/en/resources/global-data-and-cyber-handbook/emea/israel/topics/international-data-transfer. Practical: hosting in the EU is allowed; the transfer must be written in the database definitions document (regulation 2(a)(4) of the Security Regulations, read above).
- EU has recognised Israel as adequate (reaffirmed 15 Jan 2024; Chambers 2026 guide says it still stands; civil society asked for review in 2025) **[SECONDARY]**.

## 4. Accessibility (websites)

Source read in full: Equal Rights for Persons with Disabilities Regulations (Service Accessibility Adjustments), 5773-2013 - consolidated text PDF on gov.il **[PRIMARY, but the text layer of the PDF garbled digits, so I cross-checked with search results]** - https://www.gov.il/BlobFolder/guide/accommodating_service_providing_rules/he/sitedocs_service_acessibility_regulations.pdf (the consolidated text I downloaded says it includes amendments up to the last one in 2020 - **check there is no newer amendment**).

- Chapter on internet services (regulations numbered 35א-35ו; the headings in my text extraction showed garbled numbers but regulation 35ג is cross-referenced in the text and the search result confirmed 35ה for the statement):
  - **Standard**: Israeli Standard **ת"י 5568** (Web Content Accessibility Guidelines, based on WCAG; the regulation requires **level AA**), excluding apps (separate rules) and time-based media. Regulation 35א(א).
  - Accessible documents (e.g. PDFs) must follow the standard (35א(ג)).
  - Deviations are not a violation until the provider was notified and failed to fix within a reasonable time, **no more than 60 days** (35א(ד), digit shown garbled as "61" - **verify**).
  - Third-party content: provider is not responsible for adjusting content it did not edit or commission, but must offer an accessible infrastructure (35ב).
  - **Accessibility statement** (35ה): must be shown in a **prominent place** on the site (and app), per the standard's guidelines, and include the adjustments made, the **accessibility coordinator's** details and how to contact them **if required to appoint one**, and contact details to report missing adjustments or request an adjustment.
  - **Exemptions** (35ו): (ז) an **osek patur** (exempt dealer as defined in section 1 of the VAT Law) **or** a provider whose **average annual turnover does not exceed 100,000 NIS** is exempt from the internet-service adjustments. (The text showed "111,111" because zeros were garbled; the search-result summary independently says 100,000 NIS.) (ה) content of a service accessible only to **pre-registered users with at most 500 simultaneous participants** is exempt, unless a person with a disability asks for the adjustment (garbled digits; **verify**) - this could apply to a subscription site. (ט) a provider with average turnover up to 1 million NIS is exempt for sites started before the regulations came into force (not applicable to a new site).
  - Video content: captions required only for a provider whose average turnover exceeds 5 million NIS that edits/produces recorded video (35ד).
- **Accessibility coordinator**: a service provider that **employs at least 25 employees** must appoint one from the employees (regulation titled "רכז נגישות", section 19מב of the law; my extraction numbered it 90 but numbers were garbled). A business with fewer than 25 employees has no duty to appoint a coordinator, but the statement still has to give a contact point.
- Which regulator: Commission for Equal Rights of Persons with Disabilities, Ministry of Justice (https://www.gov.il/he/departments/the_commission_for_equal_rights_of_persons_with_disabilities) **[page not opened]**.
- Practical meaning: a brand-new business that is osek patur or under 100,000 NIS average turnover is legally exempt from the website adjustments, but must check the exemption conditions on the day it passes the limit; building to WCAG AA is the safe default, particularly for four languages and RTL (Arabic, Hebrew).

## 5. Marketing messages ("spam law")

Law: Communications Law (Bezeq and Broadcasting), 1982, **section 30א**, as replaced by Amendment 40 (2008) - I read the 2008 amendment text from the Knesset (Sefer HaChukim 2153, 1.6.2008) **[PRIMARY as enacted in 2008; later amendments not checked]** - https://fs.knesset.gov.il//17/law/17_lsr_299991.pdf
- 30א(א): "advertisement" (דבר פרסומת) = a message distributed commercially whose purpose is to encourage buying a product/service or spending money in another way. Covers fax, automatic dialling, **email**, **SMS**.
- 30א(ב): no advertisement by those channels **without the recipient's explicit prior consent in writing** (consent can be given in an electronic message or recorded call).
- 30א(ג): exception for **existing customers**: allowed without consent if all of: (1) the recipient gave details during a purchase or negotiation for a product/service **and the advertiser told them the details would be used to send advertisements**; (2) the advertiser gave the recipient a chance to refuse and the recipient did not; (3) the ad relates to a **similar** product/service.
- 30א(ד): the recipient may at any time withdraw consent / refuse; refusal must be free of charge (except message cost), by the channel the ad was sent or the recipient's choice.
- 30א(ה): required content, prominent and clear: (1) that it is an advertisement: the word **"פרסומת"** at the **beginning** of the ad, and for an email **in the subject line**; (2) advertiser's name, address and contact details; (3) the right to refuse and a simple way to do so, and for email a **valid email address** for refusals. For SMS only name and contact method for refusal.
- 30א(ו): criminal fines (a fine under section 61(א)(3)-(4) of the Penal Law); 30א(ח) officers' duty to supervise; 30א(ט)-(י): civil wrong, with **compensation without proof of damage of up to 1,000 NIS per advertisement** received (the Chamber of Commerce and several court reports confirm it is claimed per message) **[PRIMARY text; secondary confirmation]**.
- **Service message vs advertisement**: the definition turns on the message's purpose being to encourage a purchase. A receipt, order confirmation, password reset, or a pure account notice (your pass expires on X date) is not an "advertisement" as such, but adding "renew now with 20% off" inside it can turn it into one. The statute text I read does not list examples; the distinction is from the definition. A court-case summary I saw did not settle where a renewal reminder falls **[not verified - ask lawyer]**.
- Note: the existing-customer exception requires telling the buyer at purchase time that the details will be used for ads. For a "pass is ending" reminder with a renewal offer, a separate tick-box (unticked) at sign-up is the safe route.

## 6. Advertising and children; unproven claims

- Consumer Protection Law section 2(א) (no misleading on a material matter) lists subjects of deception, including (4) the use, benefit and risks of the service, (10) sponsorship/endorsement, (11) conformity to a standard, (14) professional opinions or test results **[SECONDARY copy of statute]**. A claim like "improves children's behaviour/intelligence" is a claim about benefit/results and must be provable.
- Section 7א: advertising or marketing aimed at **minors** must not mislead a minor, exploit age, naivety or inexperience, or encourage activity that could harm physical or mental health; the minister may set regulations (7א(ב)) **[SECONDARY copy of statute]**. Offences aimed at minors: section 23(א)(4); aggravated circumstances for offences aimed at minors (22ד, 23א(ב)(4)) **[SECONDARY]**. Since the buyers are parents/teachers, not children, the main exposure is truthful claims to adults and anything directly marketed to children on the site.
- I did **not** find, in what I read, a specific Israeli rule on "educational/developmental" claims for children's content; Consumer Authority guidance on this was not found **[not verified]**. Practical: describe what the stories are, avoid "proven"/"improves" wording unless a source exists, and keep the evidence on file.
- Cross-border: Danish/EU rules on marketing to children are not covered here.

## 7. Copyright basics (selling stories; transferring a site and its content)

- Law: Copyright Law, 2007 (חוק זכות יוצרים, התשס"ח-2007), in force 25 May 2008, replacing the 1911 law (search summary; WIPO Lex page https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/legislation/details/5016 **[SNIPPET]**).
- Term: generally the author's life plus 70 years **[SNIPPET]**; exceptions e.g. works made for the State.
- Moral rights are personal (section 45 heading in the table of contents of the law) **[SNIPPET]**. Moral rights (attribution, integrity) stay with the author even if economic rights are sold - meaning the stories' authors and illustrators must be named/credited as agreed.
- **Work done by freelancers**: Israeli law gives first ownership to the author; an employer owns an employee's work only for work made in the course of employment (section on works made in employment - **not verified**). For a site's stories, art, audio and translations (four languages!), get **written assignment or licence agreements** from every author, illustrator, translator, voice actor and developer. **[general rule; section numbers not verified]**
- **Transfer of the site to a buyer**: the buyer needs (a) an assignment of the economic rights in the stories/art/code, in writing (the Israeli law requires a **written** document for assignment/exclusive licence - section number **not verified**, my search result and the earlier guess of "section 23" were wrong: 23 is about public-place works), (b) the domain, accounts, customer data (privacy law applies to handing the customer database to the buyer: tell users or rely on a lawful basis), (c) third-party licences (fonts, AI tools, music) being transferable.
- Check that AI-generated images/text and fonts/music you used are licensed for commercial use and transferable.
- Source to check: full text at https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/text/202010 (English) and the Knesset/Nevo consolidated text.

## 8. For later: EU / Denmark essentials (short)

- GDPR applies to a non-EU business that offers goods/services to people in the EU (Article 3(2)); expect: an EU representative (Article 27) for non-EU controllers, a lawful basis, records, breach notice within 72 hours - general GDPR knowledge, not re-verified here.
- **Child consent age**: the GDPR default for information-society services is 16, member states may lower to 13 (Article 8). **Denmark: 13** under the Danish Data Protection Act (section 6(2) or (3) - sources disagree on the subsection); one Linklaters page says 15 and a 2025 news item (dig.watch) says Denmark planned to raise it - **check the current Danish text** **[SECONDARY, conflicting]**. Your accounts are adult-held, so parental consent is not the main route, but a Danish parent entering a child's nickname is still data about a child.

## Ten-line plain summary

1. The Consumer Protection Law treats your site as a distance seller: show your name, **ID number**, address, price, what is sold, how to cancel.
2. Prices shown to Israeli consumers must **include VAT (18%)**; the confirmation document must be in Hebrew or the language of the sale.
3. Buyers can cancel within **14 days**; you may keep at most the lower of **5% or 100 NIS**, and must refund within **14 days**.
4. You must put a **cancellation link on the home page** (section 14ט) and allow cancelling by internet/email; missing it is fined.
5. No special free-trial law was found; do not let the trial turn into a payment without a new explicit purchase.
6. Osek patur ceiling is **122,833 NIS in 2026** (no VAT, receipts); above that or by choice you are osek murshe (18% VAT, tax invoices; allocation number above 5,000 NIS before VAT).
7. Privacy: Amendment 13 has been in force since **14 Aug 2025**; most small businesses no longer register a database and need no privacy officer, but need a clear notice, security and a breach plan; the Authority is auditing online retail.
8. Websites: standard **5568 / WCAG AA** and an accessibility statement - but an osek patur or a business under about **100,000 NIS** turnover is exempt; coordinator only at 25+ employees.
9. Marketing email/SMS needs **prior explicit consent**, "**פרסומת**" in the subject, sender details and an easy unsubscribe; up to **1,000 NIS per message** compensation.
10. Authors keep moral rights; get written assignments from every writer/illustrator/translator before selling the site; EU/Denmark selling later brings GDPR, cookies and OSS VAT.
