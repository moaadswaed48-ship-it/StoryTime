## A. CDC milestone checklists (revised 2022 CDC/AAP, "75% of children can do this by this age")

### By 2 years  https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/milestones/2-years.html
- Social/emotional: notices when others are hurt or upset (pauses or looks sad when someone cries); looks at your face to see how to react in a new situation.
- Language: points to things in a book when asked ("Where is the bear?"); says at least two words together ("More milk"); points to at least two body parts; uses more gestures than waving/pointing (blows a kiss, nods yes).
- Cognitive: holds something in one hand while using the other; tries switches/knobs/buttons; plays with more than one toy at once (toy food on a plate).
- Motor: kicks a ball; runs; walks up a few stairs; eats with a spoon.
### By 3 years  https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/milestones/3-years.html
- Social/emotional: calms within 10 minutes after caregiver leaves (childcare drop-off); notices other children and joins them to play.
- Language: conversation of at least two back-and-forth exchanges; asks who/what/where/why; says what action is happening in a picture or book ("running", "eating"); says first name; talks well enough for others to understand most of the time.
- Cognitive: draws a circle when shown; avoids touching hot objects when warned.
- Motor: strings large beads; puts on some clothes by himself (loose pants, jacket); uses a fork.

## B. CDC "Positive Parenting Tips" stages (the owner's hypothesis source)
- CDC publishes stages: Infants, Toddlers (1-2), Toddlers (2-3), Preschoolers (3-5), Middle childhood (6-8), (9-11), Teens. Note "2-3" and "3-5" share the age-3 boundary; the CDC pages for 2-3 returned 404 on the current URL (page moved) so the 2-3 stage text is unverified here, but the CDC milestone checklists (above) are by 2, 3, 4, 5 years.
- Preschoolers 3-5 (mirror of CDC page): https://www.restoredcdc.org/www.cdc.gov/child-development/positive-parenting-tips/preschooler-3-5-years.html (live: https://www.cdc.gov/child-development/positive-parenting-tips/preschooler-3-5-years.html)
  - Children should be able to help dress/undress, recall part of a story, sing songs; play with other children, ride a tricycle, use safety scissors; world opens up, they ask about things; focus more on adults and children outside the family.
  - Tips: keep reading, library/bookstore; simple chores; clear consistent discipline, say what to do instead of "no"; speak in complete sentences and "grown up" words; help through problem-solving steps when upset; limited simple choices (what to wear, snack); encourage play with other children "to learn the value of sharing and friendship".
  - Safety: why to stay out of traffic; do not play in the street or run after stray balls; tricycle on sidewalk with helmet; watch at all times outside; stranger safety. Healthy habits: eat meals together; keep TV out of the bedroom.
- Middle childhood 6-8 (mirror): https://www.restoredcdc.org/www.cdc.gov/child-development/positive-parenting-tips/middle-childhood-6-8-years.html (live URL: https://www.cdc.gov/child-development/positive-parenting-tips/middle-childhood-6-8-years.html)
  - Dress themselves, catch a ball with hands only, tie shoes; independence from family more important; starting school brings contact with the larger world; friendships more and more important; critical time to build confidence (friends, schoolwork, sports).
  - Emotional/social: more independence; start to think about the future; understand more about their place in the world; attention to friendships and teamwork; want to be liked and accepted.
  - Thinking: rapid growth of mental skills; better ways to describe experiences and talk about thoughts and feelings; less focus on self, more concern for others.
  - Tips: talk about respecting others and helping people in need; keep reading to the child, take turns reading to each other once the child reads; encourage solving small disagreements on their own; join groups, volunteering.
  - Responsibility: affection, recognise accomplishments; household tasks (set the table); learn patience by letting others go first or finishing a task before play; think about consequences; clear rules (TV time, bedtime); discipline to guide not to shame; praise effort ("you worked hard") over traits ("you are smart"); set own achievable goals.
  - Safety: traffic/walking to school/bike; water safety; supervise climbing; how to ask for help; harmful products and firearms out of reach; car seats.

## C. Screen time and video (question 4)
- AAP 2026 policy "Digital Ecosystems, Children, and Adolescents", Pediatrics Feb 2026 (online Jan 20 2026), doi:10.1542/peds.2025-075320 (cited by https://www.contemporarypediatrics.com/view/aap-calls-for-system-level-changes-to-improve-children-s-digital-media-environments ; DOI itself not opened = unverified). Family version: https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/Media/Pages/Helping-Kids-Thrive-in-a-Digital-World-AAP-Policy-Explained.aspx
  - Shift from strict screen-time limits to "quality, context and conversation" (healthychildren page above, fetched).
  - Early childhood 0-5: heavy solo use can slow language and social development; linked to poor sleep, more angry outbursts, less patience and self-control; best way is to watch together; one shared family device is easier than personal devices; under-18-months learn best from real-world interaction; keep screens out of bedrooms and the hour before bed. (same URL)
  - School age 6-12: misuse linked to poorer school performance, weaker attention control, less exercise; watch/play alongside, delay personal tablets; choose content that builds social-emotional skills, supports reading and math, or encourages free play; be wary of autoplay, endless scroll, targeted ads. (same URL)
- AAP numeric limit: 2016 statement kept "1 hour or less a day of high-quality programming" for ages 2-5, none under 18 months except video chat, co-view for 18-24 months. Source for wording: Harvard Health summary https://www.harvardhealth.org/blog/new-expert-recommendations-on-media-use-and-children-2016102510564 (fetched). CHOC (hospital, secondary) says the Feb 2026 AAP guidance keeps "one hour of high-quality content daily for ages 2-5" with co-viewing: https://health.choc.org/updated-aap-recommendations-for-screen-time/ . Whether the 2026 policy text itself keeps the 1-hour figure is UNVERIFIED (I read only the explainer, which gives no number).
- WHO 2019 (children under 5): under 1 year screen time not recommended; age 1 sedentary screen time not recommended; age 2 no more than 1 hour, "less is better"; ages 3-4 "no more than 1 hour; less is better"; "When sedentary, engaging in reading and storytelling with a caregiver is encouraged"; not restrained or sitting for extended periods more than 1 hour at a time; sleep 11-14 h (1-2 y), 10-13 h (3-4 y); 3-4 y 180 min physical activity incl. 60 min moderate-vigorous. Sources: https://www.who.int/news/item/24-04-2019-to-grow-up-healthy-children-need-to-sit-less-and-play-more (fetched) ; guideline https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789241550536
- Implication for film length (my inference, not a source claim): the 1-hour daily cap is for ALL screen use, so a single film should be a small fraction of it. Short episodes (about 5-10 min for 2-3, up to about 10-15 for 3-5) with a natural end, no autoplay, a built-in "stop" moment and a parent prompt for co-viewing. 6-8 can handle longer but AAP gives no numeric cap for them.

## D. National curricula (borders and teaching goals)

### D1. England EYFS "Development Matters" (DfE non-statutory guidance, updated Sep 2023, 128 pp, fetched and read as PDF)
URL page: https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/development-matters--2 ; PDF: https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/64e6002a20ae890014f26cbc/DfE_Development_Matters_Report_Sep2023.pdf (cited below as DM, p. = section). Statutory EYFS 2025 framework (birth-5; 3 prime areas + 4 specific areas; 17 early learning goals): https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/687105a381dd8f70f5de3ea9/EYFS_framework_for_group_and_school_based_providers_.pdf (returned HTTP 410 to my fetch; facts from search summary = secondary).
- BORDERS: Development Matters uses bands "Birth to three", "3 and 4-year-olds", "Reception (age 4-5)". It does NOT have a separate 2-3 band, and treats 3-4 as one band and 4-5 as another. (DM, all sections)
- Language (DM, Communication and Language):
  - By about 2: up to 50 words, starting to join two or three words ("more milk"); by about 3: around 300 words including words for time, space, function; links up to 5 words; uses pronouns, plurals, prepositions; follows instructions with three key words ("Can you wash dolly's face?").
  - 2-3 y: "Listen to simple stories and understand what is happening, with the help of the pictures."; shares picture books daily; wordless books encourage conversation; "four comments to every question".
  - 3-4 y: sings a large repertoire of songs; knows many rhymes, can talk about familiar books and "tell a long story"; sentences of four to six words by around 4; sentences joined with "because", "or", "and"; uses past and future tense; answers simple "why" questions; may still say "swimmed", "runned"; mispronounces r, j, th, ch, sh and long words like "hippopotamus"; can express a point of view and debate with words, hold conversation for many turns.
  - Reception (4-5): understand how and why to listen; learn new vocabulary and use it through the day; connect ideas with connectives; describe events in detail with sequence; engage in storytimes (at least one a day); retell story in own words after deep familiarity; listen to rhymes paying attention to how they sound.
  - Attention (DM): at 2-3 children "generally focus on an activity of their own choice and find it difficult to be directed by an adult" and "can easily be distracted"; around age 3 can shift from one task to another if you get attention with their name.
- Personal, social, emotional (DM, PSED):
  - 2-3: "effortful control", e.g. waiting for a turn, resisting grabbing; "me do it" independence and tantrums; say feelings in words ("I'm sad because..."); notice differences (skin colour, hair, gender, disability, religion); "Safely explore emotions beyond their normal range through play and stories"; toilet learning; manage separation from parent.
  - 3-4: play with others, elaborate play; find solutions to conflicts and rivalries; increasingly follow rules and understand why; remember rules without reminders; be assertive in appropriate ways; talk about feelings with words happy, sad, angry, worried; understand gradually how others feel; sense of responsibility and membership of a community; more independence in tooth-brushing, toilet, hand-washing; make healthy choices about food, drink, activity.
  - Reception: see self as valuable; build constructive, respectful relationships; express feelings and consider feelings of others; resilience and perseverance; moderate own feelings; think about others' perspectives; personal hygiene; know factors for health: physical activity, healthy eating, toothbrushing, "sensible amounts of screen time", sleep routine, being a safe pedestrian.
- Literacy / maths (DM): 3-4: spot and suggest rhymes, count/clap syllables, same initial sounds, know some letters of own name; Reception: read letters by sound, blend sounds into short words, write name and some letters, read simple phrases. Maths: 2-3 finger rhymes with numbers, compare "lots/more/same", notice and arrange patterns; 3-4: say one number per item 1-5, know last number counted is the total, subitise, finger numbers to 5, compare quantities "more than/fewer than", talk about 2D and 3D shapes, extend ABAB patterns; Reception: count beyond ten, numbers to 10 and their composition, number bonds 0-5.

### D2. Denmark: "Den styrkede pædagogiske læreplan" (BEK nr 968, 28 June 2018; ministry publication via emu.dk; BUPL leaflet)
- BUPL leaflet read: https://bupl.dk/sites/default/files/2022-04/Pjecer_%20Den_styrkede_paedagogiske_laereplan.pdf . Full ministry PDF (https://emu.dk/sites/default/files/2020-03/7044%20SPL%20Den_styrkede_paedagogiske_laereplan_21_WEB.pdf) returned HTTP 503: not read directly.
- Six themes (from search summary of the ministry publication, secondary): Alsidig personlig udvikling; Social udvikling; Kommunikation og sprog; Krop, sanser og bevægelse; Natur, udeliv og science; Kultur, æstetik og fællesskab. Plus a pedagogical foundation (child view, good childhood, "dannelse", play, children's communities, learning).
- KEY FINDING (BUPL leaflet, read): goals are set for the learning environment, "ikke det enkelte barn eller børnegruppen" (not the individual child or group), and "børn udvikles i forskellige tempi". Denmark has no national age-banded milestone list; the age structure is institutional: dagtilbud (vuggestue about 0-2/3, børnehave about 3-6) then "børnehaveklasse" (the 0. class, age about 6). So Danish sources will not tell us exact borders; use development science instead. The Danish framework also says that "dannelse", play and children's communities (børnefællesskaber) come first: stories should invite play and togetherness, not drill.
- Danish curriculum uses "børnehaveklasse" transition as the end point: matches a 6-year-old start of formal schooling.
