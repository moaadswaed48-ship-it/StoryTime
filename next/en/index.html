What is inside every story

One story from the shelf for ages 2 to 3, in its four parts.

Slow, Slow, TogetherAges 2–3

Sami runs up the hill to the red flowers, and Grandpa falls far behind.

  1. 1Read

    The picture book, with the words set beside the pictures and pages that turn like paper.

  2. 2Listen

    A narrator reads each page aloud. When the reading ends, the page can turn by itself.

  3. 3Watch

    A short film of the same story: Sami’s dash and Grandpa’s steps, until they walk at one pace.

  4. 4Do

    The Looking Frame: tear the middle out of a paper plate, walk slowly outside hand in hand, and name what you see inside the frame.

Being made now: the first stories are written in all four languages. Their pictures, films and recorded voice are still in production.

What the stories teach

Each story carries one lesson, shown in what the characters do. Examples from the stories written and being written:

  • Respect for older people Ages 4–5

    On a rainy bus, Amin gives his window seat to an older woman with heavy bags. She draws little waves beside his sun and tells him about the sea where she grew up.

  • Respect for classmates Ages 4–5

    Dalia’s voice is small and the room is loud. One classmate puts a finger to his lips, then another, until the circle is quiet enough to hear her shell.

  • Being good to your mother Ages 2–3

    Mama’s arms are full and her keys fall. Lina picks them up: “Here you are, Mama!” At home, it is Mama who says thank you.

  • Numbers Ages 6–8

    Ten pomegranates, two baskets. However Sofia and Liam share them out for the baker and the doctor, there are always ten.

Every activity comes with a note for the grown-up that explains the idea behind it, and a version for a group of children.

Why stories like these matter

What published research says

  • The World Health Organization encourages reading and storytelling with a caregiver when children under 5 are sitting still.

    World Health Organization, 2019

  • A review of 213 school programmes that teach social and emotional skills found that, where school results were measured, children’s results rose by about 11 percentile points.

    Durlak and colleagues, 2011, Child Development

  • Children rated by their kindergarten teachers as more helpful and cooperative did better in education and work 13 to 19 years later. This is a link seen over time, not proof of cause.

    Jones, Greenberg and Crowley, 2015, American Journal of Public Health

These studies looked at school programmes and at children followed over years. They did not study StoryTime.

The founders have run their own study with children. It will be published here when it is ready.

Questions and answers

Which age group fits my child?+

Choose by age: 2–3, 4–5 or 6–8. A three-year-old stays in the first group until the fourth birthday. One account can hold passes for two age groups, for families with children of different ages.

How much screen time is this?+

The activity happens away from the screen, and the book is made to be read together. Films are short and stop at the end; nothing starts playing by itself. The World Health Organization advises no more than one hour of screen time a day for children aged 2 to 4, and less is better.

Which languages are there?+

Arabic, Hebrew, English and Danish, for the site, the stories and the captions. The language of the site and the language of the stories are set separately, so a child can hear a story in one language while the grown-up uses the site in another.

What does the free trial include?+

3 stories of the age group you choose, for 3 days, with every part open. No card is needed, and the trial never turns into a payment.

How do I cancel?+

A pass ends on its end date, and nothing renews. Within 14 days of buying, a purchase can be cancelled in Settings, on the “Cancel a purchase” page, or by email or post. The refund comes within 14 days; a fee of at most 5% of the price or 100 shekels, whichever is lower, may apply.

What do you keep about my child?+

Accounts belong to adults. A child’s profile holds only a first name or nickname and an age group. A teacher’s profile is a class name, with no children’s names. We never ask for a phone number, and there are no ads.

Can kindergarten teachers use it?+

Yes. Choose “Kindergarten staff” when you sign up, and your profile becomes your class. Every activity has a version for a group of children.

Which devices does it work on?+

Phones, tablets and computers, in an up-to-date web browser. There is no app to install.

Is there a recorded voice?+

Not yet. A narrator will record every story in each language, with separate recordings for the book and the film. The voice arrives before passes go on sale; until then, the books are for reading aloud yourself.
